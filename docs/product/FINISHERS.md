# The Finisher record

> **Status:** living. **Last reviewed:** 2026-11-22. Review: yearly. Kept up to date with the code; if it is wrong, fix it.

What the Hub knows about a writer's milestones, kept apart from the project they came from. Words and the ladder (Writer, Finisher, Author, Published Author): `docs/product/WRITER_TITLES.md`. The plan around it, including the public Finishers page that is not built yet: `docs/TODO.md`, "Writer profiles: public author pages, Finishers, three-level visibility".

## What exists

Three milestones. Two are stored, one is derived:

| Milestone | Title it earns | Stored as | Stamp |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wrote THE END | Finisher | `member_milestones.kind = 'the_end'` | THE END |
| Revised until publish-ready | Author | `kind = 'publish_ready'` | one string, `PUBLISH_READY_STAMP` in `src/lib/finishers.ts` (still undecided) |
| Published | Published Author | not stored: a live row in `member_books` | n/a |

The third comes from the Bookshelf, so the two cannot disagree. A course completion (The Ready Writer and The 180 Program for THE END, Self-Editing Academy for publish-ready) may later pre-fill the question; it is never proof.

`member_milestones` (`mlst_` ids, Tier 2: never shown in a URL) holds one row per member, kind **and work**. A milestone belongs to one work: a writing project (`project_id`) and/or a Bookshelf book (`book_id`; a book that has a project is the same work as that project, so the row carries both), or neither with a short `title` the member wrote for a work that is not listed (at most 120 characters, never in the Activity Log). The work key is the project when there is one, else the book, else the lower-cased title, else empty (a row from before works existed, still valid): `workKey` in `src/lib/finishers-works.ts` and the unique index `member_milestones_live_work_unique` say the same thing, so a member can have THE END for many works and one live row for each. A row also has the date it happened (optional, and as exact as the member remembers: see "Approximate dates"), a "before" and an "after" story (optional, 2000 characters each), where it came from (`source`: member, project_stage, course or staff), its `status`, who can see it (`visibility`: private, community or public), one marketing consent (`consent_testimonial`) and a soft delete. `consent_profile` and `consent_homepage` are no longer read or written (the contract step is in `docs/TODO.md`). Code goes through `MilestoneRepository` (`src/lib/data/milestones.ts`); the words and parsing of a member's answer live in `src/lib/finishers.ts`, the work logic (keys, the project and other works, the summary line) in `src/lib/finishers-works.ts`.

## The rules

- **A row is a claim about a person, so the member confirms it.** Staff and courses can only *propose*: a row starts as `proposed`, and only the member moves it to `confirmed` or `declined`. The policies enforce it: an admin can add a staff proposal, and edit or take back one that is still a proposal, but cannot confirm for the member or touch a confirmed row. A member cannot change whose row it is. Nobody is notified in this version (see `docs/TODO.md`).
- **A member can add their own, without waiting for staff.** "I wrote THE END" and "My manuscript is publish-ready" open the same answer form and create the row as `source = 'member'`, `status = 'confirmed'` in one step (date and stories optional, the share tick off, the level "other members can see it"). The INSERT policy allows only that: their own `member_id`, source `member`, status `confirmed` with a time, an optional project that is theirs and live, no book link, `created_by` themselves or empty, and never while an admin views as them (sudo). An admin cannot add one in a member's name. A consent can come in the same insert because the table's CHECK only needs the row to be confirmed. One live row per member and kind: adding a second returns the existing one (the repository looks for any live row of the kind, even one linked to a project, or one staff proposed), so the card shows it instead of an error.
- **Who sees it is the member's choice, one level per milestone.** `visibility` is the lock (private), the house (other members) or the globe (the web), the same three levels as everywhere (`src/lib/writing-visibility.ts`; test `isShownToMembers`, never `=== 'community'`). A milestone starts private. Confirming one (answering a proposal, bringing back a declined one, or adding their own) starts it at **the globe** (`MILESTONE_DEFAULT_VISIBILITY`, `src/lib/finishers.ts`; the database trigger `member_milestones_default_visibility` starts it at the same level and a test keeps the two in step) unless the member says otherwise; changing a confirmed one never moves its level. The owner's reason: a writer's profile should be good enough when they tick "share my story" and click View, without changing each item (the Bookshelf is public already). The globe still reaches the web only when writer profiles are open to the member and their page is on; signed-in members see it either way. The answer form shows the level (lock, house or globe, with a plain line: "Anyone on the internet, once your writer profile is on; members see it now.") next to the story fields, so the default is a visible, one-click choice and the level the member leaves it at is the one saved; choosing the lock is written in a second step, because the trigger reads a private write as "no level named". Only a confirmed row can be anything but private (a CHECK), so a staff proposal stays unreadable by others until the member confirms, and a decline puts it back to private. The story shares the milestone's level: there is no separate story level.
- **The table is still readable by the member it is about and by admins only** (no other member, no visitor, no grant for anonymous). Everything else reads through functions that return only the milestones at the right level, whoever calls (an admin included), and never the row id. The strips read `member_works_pictured_to_members(member)` (community and public, plus Published: the profile other members see) and `member_works_pictured_on_web(member)` (public, plus Published; service role only), which return each work's rows with the precision of each date and an opaque `work_no` instead of an id, and share one body, `member_works_dated`, that no client can call. `shownToMembers`, which the badges read, goes through the same reader (milestones only, never the derived Published rows). The earlier readers (`member_works_shown*`, `milestones_shown_to_members`) were dropped once nothing called them. All are `SECURITY DEFINER` with an empty search path; the repository wraps them as `worksShownToMembers`, `worksShownOnWeb` and `shownToMembers`. Staff reading `/admin/finishers` still see every row, but never a story.
- **The globe is offered only when writer profiles are open to the member** (the `writer_profiles` flag), checked in the action, and the web shows the milestone only while the member's page is on (`src/app/w/[handle]/page.tsx` has already decided that before it asks). The step says so beside the globe: "not on the web, because writer profiles are not open to you yet" or "goes on the web once your writer profile is on".
- **One consent: "Share my story to inspire other writers"** (`consent_testimonial`, `SHARE_STORY_*` in `src/lib/finishers.ts`). It is for marketing: the Finishers section of the website and the testimonials we share. It is **off by default** (`SHARE_STORY_DEFAULT`, one constant), shown on the form only once a story is written or the milestone has a photo, never a condition of saving, and only kept when something is there to share, a story or a photo (the parse drops it otherwise, with the photo looked up by the action and never taken from the form, and the action refuses to tick it on a milestone with neither). Unticking is a plain choice with no warning. The database refuses a consent on a row that is not confirmed, so declining clears it. A future reader must check this flag for the use it serves; being confirmed, or being visible to members, is not consent to be featured. `consent_homepage` was folded into it by the migration (`consent_testimonial OR consent_homepage`).
- **The member can change their mind** on the project's Milestones in My Writing: confirm, change the level or the tick, or say "not me / not yet" on one staff proposed. A decline keeps the row (so a backfill does not ask again) and drops every consent. A milestone they added themselves, or confirmed from the project-stage prompt, has "Take it back" instead (after a confirmation): a soft delete, so the slot is free to add again, and the backfill still does not propose that kind again. Taking back needed the member to read their own deleted rows (Postgres checks the new row against the SELECT policy); the repository always filters on `deleted_at`, so a taken-back row never shows.
- **Sudo looks, never answers.** An admin viewing as a member can see the milestones; the action and the database refuse the write.

## Where members manage them: with each project

Milestones belong to a work, so they sit with it in My Writing (Projects tab) rather than in one list in Settings. A work is a writing project and/or a Bookshelf book (a book with a project is that project's work), or a title the member wrote. Settings, Profile says nothing about them (search and the stage prompt lead to My Writing). The pieces (`src/components/writing/Milestones.tsx`; the data is `loadMilestoneContext` in `src/app/(member)/projects/milestoneData.ts`, read once per page):

- **On each project card**, a closed "Milestones" row says how far the work has got ("1 of 3 reached", and "1 waiting for you to confirm" for a step staff proposed) and opens to that project's steps (`ProjectMilestonesToggle`).
- **On the project's page**, a "Milestones" section (anchor `#milestones`) with the same steps always open (`ProjectMilestonesSection`). The project-stage prompt links to it ("See this project's milestones").
- **"Other works"** (anchor `#other-works`, `OtherWorks`, under the project list) holds every work that is not one of the member's active projects and has something to show: Bookshelf books with no project, works added by title ("Add a work that is not listed here"), rows from before works existed, and the works of projects put away (hibernating), which have no card. `otherWorks` and `workOfProject` in `src/lib/finishers-works.ts` split them. It is always shown, because it is also where a work is added.
- **The summary line** (anchor `#your-milestones`, `MilestoneSummary`) sits above the project list. Search finds "Your milestones" and "Other works" in My Writing (`src/lib/site-pages.ts`).

Each step has the presentation members already knew, with titles from `src/lib/finishers.ts` and `docs/product/WRITER_TITLES.md`. A confirmed row also has the lock, house or globe (`VisibilityControl` with a `levels` list: private and community, plus public when writer profiles are open to the member), the share tick when a story is written (it saves the moment it is clicked), and, right after the first save, a short celebration ("You wrote THE END. We're so proud of you." for THE END; a plain line for publish-ready). In sudo everything is look-only:

- **THE END** (Finisher) and **publish-ready** (Author, stamp `PUBLISH_READY_STAMP`): *not added yet* (button "I wrote THE END" or "My manuscript is publish-ready"), *waiting for you to confirm* (staff proposed it: "Yes, this is me" or "Not me / not yet"), *confirmed* with the date when there is one (Change, and "Take it back" for their own entries or "Not me / not yet" for ones staff proposed), or *declined* ("Actually, it is me").
- **Published** (Published Author) appears on a work that is a Bookshelf book and is read-only; nobody adds it, so the two cannot disagree.
- **A summary line** says where the works stand, as plain counts with no title words ("6 works · 1 published · 2 ready to publish · 3 finished"; `summarizeWorks` in `src/lib/finishers-works.ts`, the one place to change it; it counts every work the member has, projects and others): each work counts once at its **highest** step, only from a milestone the member confirmed or a book on the Bookshelf, so "ready to publish" is never said for a work that is not. It differs from the badges on purpose: they count every step reached (a published work is also a Finisher and an Author), so the line avoids their words to not seem to contradict them. Whether the line should count every step instead is the owner's open choice.
- **Rows say it once.** A row reads "You wrote THE END · Finisher", "It is ready to publish · Author", "It is published · Published Author" (`milestoneRowHeading`), not the title and the stamp again in parentheses. `PUBLISH_READY_STAMP` ("FINAL DRAFT") stays as a constant for the places that name the step by its stamp (the take-back question).
- **The consent is behind the info icon.** "Share my story to inspire other writers." is a tick with an info icon beside its label; what ticking means (the website and what we share about our writers, only what is written here, change your mind any time; `SHARE_STORY_TIP`) is in the icon's tip, not repeated as a paragraph under every work.
- **"Add a work that is not listed here"** takes a title and which milestone, then the same answer form.
- **Approximate dates.** Past milestones are hard to date, so the form's "When did it happen? (optional)" offers *A day*, *Month and year*, *Year only* and *I don't remember* (no date), always optional, and never asked of a staff proposal until the member answers it. The member's choice is `achieved_precision` (`day`, `month` or `year`; default `day`); `achieved_on` keeps the **first day** of the period (a CHECK keeps the two in step: `parseMilestoneDate` in `src/lib/finishers.ts` builds it from "YYYY-MM-DD", "YYYY-MM" or "YYYY"), so every reader still gets a real date. It is shown as it was given ("May 1, 2026", "May 2026", "2026": `milestoneDateWords`). A month or year that has not started yet is refused; this month and this year are fine. For "while a member" an imprecise date counts as the **last day** of its period (`datePeriodEnd`, inside the one shared `reachedWhileMember`), so it counts if any part of the period is on or after the join day: a month ending on or after the join day counts, an earlier one does not. Published (the book's date) is always a day.
- **The answer form shows who will see it.** Beside the story fields it has the lock, house or globe (`VisibilityControl`, all three levels) and one plain line per level (`MILESTONE_LEVEL_LINES`). A milestone not confirmed yet starts at `MILESTONE_DEFAULT_VISIBILITY`, a confirmed one at its own level, and the level goes with the save (`visibility` on `answerMilestone` and `addMilestone`, read by `parseMilestoneAnswer`). Whoever reuses the form (the project-stage prompt) passes `visibility` and `achievedPrecision` from the form's input to its own action, or the default applies.
- **Switching the writer profile on says so, briefly.** Right after the member turns the page on (`WriterProfilePanel`, `onTurnedOn`), `WriterProfileOnNote` shows the page's address, one line that anything marked with the globe is on it (name, photo, bio, links, milestones and books), where each is changed (Settings, Profile; My Writing for milestones and books) and "View as a visitor". It lists nothing and controls nothing: every item has its own lock, house or globe where it lives. "Got it" closes it, and it comes back the next time they turn the page on. (It replaced a longer "who sees what" box with its own controls, which was messy on a phone and repeated what is set elsewhere.)

Adding checks ownership twice: the action (`ownsProject`, the member's own books) and the INSERT policy (own live project, own live book). A book names its project (the action fills it in), a project that is not the book's is refused. A second add for the same work shows the existing row; the same kind for another work is a new row.

## The project-stage prompt

This is how most members will actually record a milestone: when they move one of their projects to a stage that means one, the Hub asks, once and kindly, after the status has saved (`updateProjectPhase` in `src/app/(member)/projects/actions.ts`, the dialog `StageMilestoneDialog`). The status change never waits for it and never fails because of it. The rules are in `milestonesToAsk` (`src/lib/milestone-prompts.ts`), with its tests as the table of every pair:

- **THE END** ("Did you write THE END on <title>?"): a project leaves Planning, Outlining or Drafting for any later stage except Hibernating.
- **Publish-ready** ("Is <title> ready to publish?"): a project reaches Publishing from a stage before it, or wakes from Hibernating straight to Publishing. A project that jumps from Drafting (or Planning, Outlining) straight to Publishing gets both questions in one dialog, "Did you write THE END, and is it ready to publish?", as two steps answered on their own.
- **Reaching Complete with no Bookshelf book** offers the existing Publish flow (the page's Publish modal, opened from the dialog; nothing copied). It is an offer, not a milestone, so it is not remembered.
- **Never asked**: moving back or sideways (Revising to Drafting), moving to Hibernating, waking from Hibernating to anything but Publishing (we do not know where the project was), and anyone viewing as a member (sudo: the status change keeps working, no prompt, and the write is refused by the action and the database).
- **Never asked about a kind the project has a row for.** The server drops a kind the project already has a milestone row for, in **any status, a taken-back one included** (`MilestoneRepository.askedKinds`), so a milestone that exists (confirmed, proposed, declined or taken back) is never asked about.
- **Two ways to say no, and they are different.** The prompt has **"Not yet"** and **"Not for this work"** (`STAGE_PROMPT`). *Not for this work* is the old behavior and stays forever: a declined row (`source = 'project_stage'`, `status = 'declined'`, for that project; `MilestoneRepository.recordNotForThisWork`), private with no consent, which keeps the prompt and the staff backfill from touching that kind for the work; the step shows it as "You said no, or not yet" with "Actually, it is me". *Not yet* is a **snooze**: "ask me again at a later stage". It is not a milestone and not a claim, so it lives in its own table, `member_milestone_snoozes` (`msnz_` ids, Tier 2: one row per project and kind, with the stage group the project was in), written by `MilestoneRepository.snooze` and read by `snoozesOf`. It is **never counted as declined** (`/admin/finishers` counts and lists read only `member_milestones`) and **never stops the staff backfill**, which looks only at `member_milestones`, so a work whose prompt was snoozed can still be proposed to the member. A snooze is written only when the project has no row of that kind; a milestone added later (the project's "I wrote THE END", or "Actually, it is me") needs nothing removed, because the prompt checks the rows first.
- **When a snoozed question comes back** (`milestonesToAsk` with the snoozes, `stageGroupOf` in `src/lib/milestone-prompts.ts`). Stages are in groups: 0 before reading and editing (and Hibernating), 1 First read-through to Final beta read, 2 Publishing, 3 Complete and Published. A "not yet" remembers the group the project was in. The question returns when the project reaches a **later group**, never twice in the same one: THE END, put off at First read-through, is asked again at Publishing, together with publish-ready (both offered in one dialog); put off at Publishing, it is asked again at Complete or Published; put off there, it is not asked again. Publish-ready, put off at Publishing, has no later stage that means it. Going back, or changing stage within the group, asks nothing. A second "not yet" moves the remembered group forward, never back. Closing the dialog any way ("Close for now", Escape, or opening the Publish flow) counts as "not yet" for the questions still open. The member can change a "not for this work" later from the project's milestones ("Actually, it is me"); there is nothing to change on a snooze.
- **"Yes" opens the answer form** (`MilestoneForm`, reused from `src/components/writing/Milestones.tsx`, not copied: optional date, optional stories, the share tick unticked, whatever level it offers) and records the milestone for that project as `source = 'project_stage'`, `status = 'confirmed'`, the member's own action (`answerStageMilestone`, `addOwn` with `source: "project_stage"`), then says the same celebration as the project's milestones. Nothing is created on "Not yet" except its snooze.
- **Going back a stage, or to Hibernating, never cancels or changes a milestone.** A milestone says what the member did, not where the project is now; they can still take back one they made (including one from this prompt) on the project's milestones. This closes the question left open in `docs/product/WRITER_TITLES.md`.
- **Publishing a book from the Publish flow** does not ask: the book itself is the third milestone, and the member can mark the other two on the project.

The insert policy "Members answer a project stage prompt" (for "Not for this work" and "Yes") allows only: their own row, never in sudo, source `project_stage`, status confirmed or declined, one of their own live projects, no book and no title. The snooze table's policies are the member's own: read, add for their own live project, move the group; never while viewing as them, never another member's, nothing for a visitor. Sudo and admins cannot answer for a member.

## Where it shows

- **The member's profile** (`/members/<id>`): a "Milestones" strip, read through `member_works_pictured_to_members` and grouped by `groupShownWorks` (`src/lib/finishers-works.ts`) into works, each with its steps in order (THE END, publish-ready, Published) for milestones that are at community or public. **Published** (Published Author) comes from the live Bookshelf books, is at **the book's own level** (`member_books.visibility`, the lock, house or globe on each book in My Books: derived, no level of its own, read-only; members see it for a book at the house or the globe, the web only for the globe, and a private book has no Published step and does not lend its title to a work), and is the same work as a milestone of the book's project (a book with a project is that project's work); a book alone is a work with only Published, and a title-only work has its milestones and no Published. The work's name is the title the member wrote, else the book's title, else **the project's title when the viewer may see the project itself** (the project's own level is one the read serves: community or public for members, public only for the web; the same rule as the project's page, in `member_works_dated`'s `p.visibility = ANY (p_levels)`); otherwise the strip says "Another work" (`ANOTHER_WORK_TITLE`) and the title never leaves the database, so several unnamed works in a row are not mistaken for duplicates. Each step shows the title it earns (Finisher, Author, Published Author), what they did, the day (the book's published date for Published) and, for a milestone, the story when they wrote one. The member sees the same list as anyone: their own private ones are not there. The Published Books section stays too.
- **A milestone's level is independent of its project's level (owner decision, 2026-11-22).** Unlike a goal, which can never be more visible than its project, a milestone on a private project may be set to the globe. Authors celebrate progress before the title is ready for a public reveal, so the strip shows the milestone as "Another work" and never the project's title. Do not cap a milestone to its project's level, and do not read the project's level for anything but the work's name.
- **The writer profile on the web** (`/w/<handle>`, and the member's own preview): the same strip for milestones at the globe only, behind the `writer_profiles` flag and the page being on, with the same 404 for a page that is off. A story is plain text, never markup.
- **Caching.** Both pages are rendered on demand (the production build lists `/w/[handle]` and `/members/[id]` as dynamic), so each request reads the database again and a withdrawn level, a taken-back milestone or an unticked share tick is gone on the next request. Every milestone action also calls `revalidatePath` for My Writing and the project, `/members/<id>` and the writer profile's path, as the profile items do, in case a page is ever cached. No response header was measured against the production CDN.
- **The share consent** is read by nothing in the Hub yet: the Finishers section of the website and the testimonials we share are not built (`docs/TODO.md`, "Finisher record: what is left"). When they are, they must read `consent_testimonial` (with `status = 'confirmed'` and not deleted) at the moment they render or export, through the service role, so a withdrawn tick takes effect on the next read, and must not copy a story into a static file, a spreadsheet or an email. The wording promises only the website and what we share about our writers, and only what the member wrote in that form and the photo they added to that milestone (the tip says so; see "Photos"): no name (the website use is not designed), so it is not mentioned. A photo or a story copied into a static file would outlive a withdrawn tick, so the same rule applies to the photo: ask the route or the database at the moment of use.

## Photos

A member can add one photo to each step: the day they wrote THE END, the day the manuscript was ready to publish, and themselves holding the published book (printed, or the Kindle cover). They are for the member's own page and, with the consent, for the stories we share about our writers. They are optional and only the member adds, changes or removes one: staff cannot add one, and an admin viewing as the member (sudo) can look and nothing else (the route answers 403). The security review is `docs/trust/MILESTONE_PHOTOS_REVIEW.md`.

**Where a photo is kept**
- **THE END and publish-ready**: two columns on the stored milestone, `member_milestones.photo_path` and `photo_alt` (the description the member wrote, at most `altMaxLength` characters). The path is `<member id>/<random token>.webp`, never an address.
- **Published**: it has no stored row (it comes from the Bookshelf), so the "holding my book" photo is kept apart, one row per book, in `member_book_photos` (`book_id`, `member_id`, `photo_path`, `photo_alt`), and not as columns of `member_books`, which belongs to the Bookshelf. It takes the level of the Published step, which today is always the globe (a Bookshelf book is public). **When a book gets a level of its own, the readers `member_works_pictured*` (and `photoByToken`, which says "public" for a book) are the one place to read it from.** Until then, a member who adds this photo is told, on the step, that it is at the globe (`publishedPhotoLine`).
- **The files** are in the private bucket `milestone-photos`: no public address, no storage policy at all (so no member and no visitor can read, list, sign or write through the API), a WebP of at most 5 MB. Only the server's key reads or writes it.
- **The member cannot write the columns**: there is no UPDATE grant on `photo_path`, a trigger refuses a path on a member's own INSERT, and a CHECK keeps a path in the member's own folder. They can change the description of their own photo (`photo_alt`, a column grant), through `setMilestonePhotoAlt`.

**Uploading** (`POST /api/milestone-photos`, `DELETE /api/milestone-photos?target=&id=`, `src/app/api/milestone-photos/route.ts`). A JPEG, PNG or WebP of at most `milestonePhoto.maxUploadBytes` (`config/app.ts`, 4 MB, under what a Vercel function accepts). The type is decided by sniffing the bytes (`inspectImage`), never the file name or the browser's type; SVG, GIF, HEIC and anything else is refused. The picture is then re-encoded to a WebP of at most `maxEdge` pixels on its longest side, turned upright, with every piece of metadata dropped, EXIF and GPS included (tested with a picture that carries both). It is stored under a fresh random name (128 random bits), so replacing a photo gives a new address and the old file is deleted; removing deletes the file. The milestone must be the member's own, live and confirmed (a book, their own live book), checked before anything is stored. A milestone taken back or answered "not me" takes its photo, and the file, with it.

**Who can see a photo** (`GET /api/milestone-photos/<token>`, `decidePhotoAccess` in `src/lib/milestone-photos.ts`, the one rule, tested at every level). The route looks the token up, then decides, on every request:

| Viewer | Lock | House | Globe |
|---|---|---|---|
| The member it is about (staff viewing as them included) | yes | yes | yes |
| Another signed-in member | no | yes | yes |
| A visitor with no sign-in (or a user with no member record) | no | no | only while the `writer_profiles` flag is on for the member and their page is on |
| Staff who are not that member | only with "Share my story" ticked | only with it ticked | only with it ticked |

Staff who are also members get what any member gets, plus what the tick opens. A photo that may not be seen is the same 404 as one that does not exist, with no cache, so nothing says a hidden photo is there. The picture is streamed by the route (never a redirect to a signed link), so no second address outlives a change of level. A milestone that is taken back, declined or not confirmed, a book that left the Bookshelf, and a row whose file is gone are all 404.

**Caching.** A photo is `private, no-store` for everyone unless a visitor could have it too: only a globe photo whose member's page is on is `public, max-age=60, s-maxage=60` (`milestonePhoto.webCacheSeconds`), so a shared cache (the CDN) can keep it for a minute and not longer. The cost: a globe photo that is withdrawn (lock or house, taken back, the page switched off) can still be served from the edge for up to a minute. Anything else is never put in a shared cache, so a lock or house photo can never be served to someone else from one.

**What each strip shows.** The member's profile (`member_works_pictured_to_members`) and the writer profile on the web (`member_works_pictured_on_web`, service role) return a photo only with a step the viewer may see (these readers replace `member_works_dated*`, whose contract step is in `docs/TODO.md`), as a token and a description, never a path or an id. The strips draw it lazily in a fixed 4:3 box (no layout shift) and show the whole picture, not a crop, with the description as its alt text, or a plain sentence for the step when the member wrote none (`MILESTONE_PHOTO_ALT`).

**On My Writing** each confirmed step, and the Published step of a book, has a small preview, "Add a photo", "Change photo" and "Remove photo" (after a confirmation), a field for what the photo shows, and a line that says who can see it: the same level as the milestone (`milestonePhotoLine`, never promising the web before the page is on). A photo counts as something to share, like a story: the "Share my story" tick is offered, and kept, on a milestone that has a photo and no story (`hasSomethingToShare`).

**Staff** (`/admin/finishers`): each row says whether "Share my story" is ticked (confirmed rows only) and, in the Photo column, shows the picture only when the member ticked it; otherwise it says only "Has a photo, not shared". A lock-level photo is therefore not shown to the team unless the member consented, and the team sees only what the existing page already allows (the list itself is unchanged: staff already read these rows). The Published photo has no consent of its own yet, so staff do not see it (`docs/TODO.md`).

**Activity Log.** The path and the description are redacted (`audit_row_change`); the log says "changed the photo on a writing milestone" and "changed what the photo on a writing milestone shows", never where it is or what it shows. A member's removal request deletes the rows (`member_data_handling`) and the removal code deletes the member's whole folder in the bucket (`src/lib/member-removal.ts`).

**Analytics.** `milestone_photo_set` and `milestone_photo_removed` (the routes: whether it worked, with the status when not) and `milestone_photo_alt_set` (the action). No content in any.

## The Finisher and Author badges

Three automatic **counted** badges (`finisher`, `author`, `published_author`) show the real number: one is "Finisher", two is "2x Finisher", seven is "7x Finisher", with no steps. `badge_types.counted` is the mode: the label is made in code from the name and the count (`countedBadgeName`, `automaticBadgeLevel` in `src/lib/badges.ts`), `badge_levels` is not read for these types (the rows stay until the contract step in `docs/TODO.md`, "Counted badges: contract step"), and for a counted badge the recipients list sorts by the count. Big Sister, the hosted-quarter badge and the prickle badges are not counted and keep their levels. The admin badges list shows "Shows the real count: Finisher → 2x Finisher → 3x Finisher …" for them, still marked Automatic. Nothing is stored per member: the level is computed when a profile is read (`src/lib/badges.ts`), so there is no award row and no "you earned a badge" message; the chip simply appears.

- **What counts.** Finisher is the number of confirmed, live THE END milestones, Author the number of confirmed, live publish-ready ones, one per work (the table allows one live row per work and kind). Proposed, declined and taken-back rows never count. Published Author counts live Bookshelf books (milestone 3 is derived from the same books), under the date rule below.
- **While a member.** One function, `reachedWhileMember` (`src/lib/finishers.ts`): a milestone with no date counts, a dated one counts when its period ends on or after the member's first join date (the date itself for a day, the last day of the month or year for an imprecise one) (`members.first_joined_at`, the same date Founding Hedgie reads), and with no join date on record a dated one counts too. The milestone still shows on the profile whatever its date. **The same function decides books** (`publishedBookCount`): a book counts for the Published Author badge, the Published step in both strips and the "Published Author of N works" summary line only if its `published_date` is on or after the member's first join day (the join day counts, a missing join date counts every book). The reason is to separate activity in the Hedgie house from activity outside it. A book from before still shows on the Bookshelf and the profile; the project's milestones list it as a work without Published and says why. A book is public on the Bookshelf, so its count is the same for every viewer.
- **Who sees what.** A member looking at their own profile sees every confirmed milestone counted. Anyone else (another member, staff on the member page or the recipients list, the chat profile card) sees only the milestones at community or public, so a badge never gives away a private milestone. `getMemberBadges` takes the viewer ("self" or "others", default "others"). The single-member path reads `ofMember` (self) or `shownToMembers` (others); the all-members path (`/admin/badges/<id>/recipients`) reads `allShownToMembers`, one paginated scan with kind and date only. The web profile shows no badges.
- **First computation.** Both badges come from the migration with no award rows, so nothing is sent when they first appear; members with confirmed milestones at community or public level see the chip on their next profile view.

## Staff

`/admin/finishers` (admin only): counts of proposed, confirmed and declined, the list (50 a page, optionally one status), "Propose from published books" and "Add one by hand" (member picker, then one of their works or a title, then the milestone). The list shows the work of each row.

- **Backfill, per book.** `propose_milestones_for_books()` looks at every live (not deleted) book and proposes both kinds for that work unless the work already has a row of that kind in any status (withdrawn ones included, so a decline or a take-back stays). A book and its project are one work, so a row on either keeps the book from being proposed again; a member with several books gets several proposals. One set-based statement run as the calling admin, so it has no page limit, and a second run adds nothing. It returns proposals, members and books, which the page shows ("N proposals for M members across K books"). Members who cannot sign in (lapsed) still get proposals; they stay hidden. The older `propose_milestones_from_books()` (one pair per member) is still in the database until the contract step (`docs/TODO.md`).
- **Backfill and a member's own.** A work the member already added, or took back, is not proposed that kind again.
- **Hand-add** creates one proposal for one work (`loadWorksOfMember` lists the member's projects and books for the picker, or a title); "Take back" soft-deletes a proposal the member has not answered.

## Data, removal and the Activity Log

- **Removal.** `member_data_handling` marks `member_milestones.member_id` as `delete`, so a member's removal request deletes the rows (and the entries about them leave the audit log, like the rest of their data). They are never part of the "keep my public work" choice.
- **Activity Log.** A proposal, a member adding their own ("added the THE END milestone"), a confirmation, a decline, a change of who can see it ("made a writing milestone private", "showed a writing milestone to other members", "showed a writing milestone on their public writer profile"), a change of the share tick, a take-back and an answer to the project-stage prompt ("added the milestone when a project moved forward", "said not yet to the milestone when a project moved forward") are audit entries on the member (`member_milestone`), with the stories and the work's title redacted; `describeRow` in `src/lib/activity-feed.ts` says them as sentences without dates or words the member wrote.
- **Analytics.** The member's answer is the `milestone_answered` action outcome ("confirmed" or "declined"); adding their own is `milestone_added` ("added" or "already_there": per work) and taking it back `milestone_taken_back`; the answer to the project-stage prompt is `milestone_stage_answered` ("confirmed", "not_yet", "not_for_this_work" or "already_there"); a change of level is `milestone_visibility_set` (the level, a fixed word) and the share tick `milestone_share_set` ("shared" or "withdrawn"). No content in any.
