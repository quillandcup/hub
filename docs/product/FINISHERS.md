# The Finisher record

> **Status:** living. **Last reviewed:** 2026-11-21. Review: yearly. Kept up to date with the code; if it is wrong, fix it.

What the Hub knows about a writer's milestones, kept apart from the project they came from. Words and the ladder (Writer, Finisher, Author, Published Author): `docs/product/WRITER_TITLES.md`. The plan around it, including the public Finishers page that is not built yet: `docs/TODO.md`, "Writer profiles: public author pages, Finishers, three-level visibility".

## What exists

Three milestones. Two are stored, one is derived:

| Milestone | Title it earns | Stored as | Stamp |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wrote THE END | Finisher | `member_milestones.kind = 'the_end'` | THE END |
| Revised until publish-ready | Author | `kind = 'publish_ready'` | one string, `PUBLISH_READY_STAMP` in `src/lib/finishers.ts` (still undecided) |
| Published | Published Author | not stored: a live row in `member_books` | n/a |

The third comes from the Bookshelf, so the two cannot disagree. A course completion (The Ready Writer and The 180 Program for THE END, Self-Editing Academy for publish-ready) may later pre-fill the question; it is never proof.

`member_milestones` (`mlst_` ids, Tier 2: never shown in a URL) holds one row per member, kind **and work**. A milestone belongs to one work: a writing project (`project_id`) and/or a Bookshelf book (`book_id`; a book that has a project is the same work as that project, so the row carries both), or neither with a short `title` the member wrote for a work that is not listed (at most 120 characters, never in the Activity Log). The work key is the project when there is one, else the book, else the lower-cased title, else empty (a row from before works existed, still valid): `workKey` in `src/lib/finishers-works.ts` and the unique index `member_milestones_live_work_unique` say the same thing, so a member can have THE END for many works and one live row for each. A row also has the date it happened (optional), a "before" and an "after" story (optional, 2000 characters each), where it came from (`source`: member, project_stage, course or staff), its `status`, who can see it (`visibility`: private, community or public), one marketing consent (`consent_testimonial`) and a soft delete. `consent_profile` and `consent_homepage` are no longer read or written (the contract step is in `docs/TODO.md`). Code goes through `MilestoneRepository` (`src/lib/data/milestones.ts`); the words and parsing of a member's answer live in `src/lib/finishers.ts`, the work logic (keys, the card's works, the summary line) in `src/lib/finishers-works.ts`.

## The rules

- **A row is a claim about a person, so the member confirms it.** Staff and courses can only *propose*: a row starts as `proposed`, and only the member moves it to `confirmed` or `declined`. The policies enforce it: an admin can add a staff proposal, and edit or take back one that is still a proposal, but cannot confirm for the member or touch a confirmed row. A member cannot change whose row it is. Nobody is notified in this version (see `docs/TODO.md`).
- **A member can add their own, without waiting for staff.** "I wrote THE END" and "My manuscript is publish-ready" open the same answer form and create the row as `source = 'member'`, `status = 'confirmed'` in one step (date and stories optional, the share tick off, the level "other members can see it"). The INSERT policy allows only that: their own `member_id`, source `member`, status `confirmed` with a time, an optional project that is theirs and live, no book link, `created_by` themselves or empty, and never while an admin views as them (sudo). An admin cannot add one in a member's name. A consent can come in the same insert because the table's CHECK only needs the row to be confirmed. One live row per member and kind: adding a second returns the existing one (the repository looks for any live row of the kind, even one linked to a project, or one staff proposed), so the card shows it instead of an error.
- **Who sees it is the member's choice, one level per milestone.** `visibility` is the lock (private), the house (other members) or the globe (the web), the same three levels as everywhere (`src/lib/writing-visibility.ts`; test `isShownToMembers`, never `=== 'community'`). A milestone starts private. Confirming one (answering a proposal, bringing back a declined one, or adding their own) makes it **community** unless the member says otherwise; changing a confirmed one never moves its level. Only a confirmed row can be anything but private (a CHECK), so a staff proposal stays unreadable by others until the member confirms, and a decline puts it back to private. The story shares the milestone's level: there is no separate story level.
- **The table is still readable by the member it is about and by admins only** (no other member, no visitor, no grant for anonymous). Everything else reads through two functions that return only the milestones at the right level, whoever calls (an admin included), and never the row id: `milestones_shown_to_members(member)` (community and public: the profile other members see) and `milestones_shown_on_web(member)` (public only; service role only). They are `SECURITY DEFINER` with an empty search path; the repository wraps them as `shownToMembers` and `shownOnWeb`. Staff reading `/admin/finishers` still see every row, but never a story.
- **The globe is offered only when writer profiles are open to the member** (the `writer_profiles` flag), checked in the action, and the web shows the milestone only while the member's page is on (`src/app/w/[handle]/page.tsx` has already decided that before it asks). The card says so beside the globe: "not on the web, because writer profiles are not open to you yet" or "goes on the web once your writer profile is on".
- **One consent: "Share my story to inspire other writers"** (`consent_testimonial`, `SHARE_STORY_*` in `src/lib/finishers.ts`). It is for marketing: the Finishers section of the website and the testimonials we share. It is **off by default** (`SHARE_STORY_DEFAULT`, one constant), shown on the form only once a story is written, never a condition of saving, and only kept when a story exists (the parse drops it otherwise, and the action refuses to tick it on a milestone with no story). Unticking is a plain choice with no warning. The database refuses a consent on a row that is not confirmed, so declining clears it. A future reader must check this flag for the use it serves; being confirmed, or being visible to members, is not consent to be featured. `consent_homepage` was folded into it by the migration (`consent_testimonial OR consent_homepage`).
- **The member can change their mind** in Settings, Profile, "Your milestones": confirm, change the level or the tick, or say "not me / not yet" on one staff proposed. A decline keeps the row (so a backfill does not ask again) and drops every consent. A milestone they added themselves, or confirmed from the project-stage prompt, has "Take it back" instead (after a confirmation): a soft delete, so the slot is free to add again, and the backfill still does not propose that kind again. Taking back needed the member to read their own deleted rows (Postgres checks the new row against the SELECT policy); the repository always filters on `deleted_at`, so a taken-back row never shows.
- **Sudo looks, never answers.** An admin viewing as a member can see the card; the action and the database refuse the write.

## The card

Settings, Profile, "Your milestones" is always shown. It lists the member's **works**: their writing projects and their Bookshelf books, deduplicated (a book with a project is that project's work), plus any work only a milestone knows (a title they added). Each work has its own rows, in plain words, with titles from `src/lib/finishers.ts` and `docs/product/WRITER_TITLES.md`. A confirmed row also has the lock, house or globe (`VisibilityControl` with a `levels` list: private and community, plus public when writer profiles are open to the member), the share tick when a story is written (it saves the moment it is clicked), and, right after the first save, a short celebration ("You wrote THE END. We're so proud of you." for THE END; a plain line for publish-ready). In sudo the card is look-only:

- **THE END** (Finisher) and **publish-ready** (Author, stamp `PUBLISH_READY_STAMP`): *not added yet* (button "I wrote THE END" or "My manuscript is publish-ready"), *waiting for you to confirm* (staff proposed it: "Yes, this is me" or "Not me / not yet"), *confirmed* with the date when there is one (Change, and "Take it back" for their own entries or "Not me / not yet" for ones staff proposed), or *declined* ("Actually, it is me").
- **Published** (Published Author) appears on a work that is a Bookshelf book and is read-only; nobody adds it, so the two cannot disagree.
- **Compact**: works that have a milestone, are on the Bookshelf or are past the drafting stage (`isPastDrafting`: any phase after `drafting` in `PROJECT_PHASES`, except hibernating) come first; the rest are under "Show all my projects".
- **A summary line** says the titles in plain words from the whole set ("Published Author of 1 work · Author of 1 work · Finisher of 2 works"): each work counts once at its highest rung, only from a milestone the member confirmed or a book on the Bookshelf, so "Author" is never said for a work that is not publish-ready.
- **"Add a work that is not listed here"** takes a title and which milestone, then the same answer form.

Adding checks ownership twice: the action (`ownsProject`, the member's own books) and the INSERT policy (own live project, own live book). A book names its project (the action fills it in), a project that is not the book's is refused. A second add for the same work shows the existing row; the same kind for another work is a new row.

## The project-stage prompt

This is how most members will actually record a milestone: when they move one of their projects to a stage that means one, the Hub asks, once and kindly, after the status has saved (`updateProjectPhase` in `src/app/(member)/projects/actions.ts`, the dialog `StageMilestoneDialog`). The status change never waits for it and never fails because of it. The rules are in `milestonesToAsk` (`src/lib/milestone-prompts.ts`), with its tests as the table of every pair:

- **THE END** ("Did you write THE END on <title>?"): a project leaves Planning, Outlining or Drafting for any later stage except Hibernating.
- **Publish-ready** ("Is <title> ready to publish?"): a project reaches Publishing from a stage before it, or wakes from Hibernating straight to Publishing. A project that jumps from Drafting (or Planning, Outlining) straight to Publishing gets both questions in one dialog, "Did you write THE END, and is it ready to publish?", as two steps answered on their own.
- **Reaching Complete with no Bookshelf book** offers the existing Publish flow (the page's Publish modal, opened from the dialog; nothing copied). It is an offer, not a milestone, so it is not remembered.
- **Never asked**: moving back or sideways (Revising to Drafting), moving to Hibernating, waking from Hibernating to anything but Publishing (we do not know where the project was), and anyone viewing as a member (sudo: the status change keeps working, no prompt, and the write is refused by the action and the database).
- **Ask once per project and kind.** The server drops a kind the project already has a row for, in **any status, a taken-back one included** (`MilestoneRepository.askedKinds`), so a milestone that exists (confirmed, proposed, declined or taken back) is never asked about, and moving forward and back never asks again.
- **"Not yet" is remembered as a declined row** (`source = 'project_stage'`, `status = 'declined'`, for that project; `MilestoneRepository.recordNotYet`). It fits the existing model: a decline already means "not me / not yet", already keeps a backfill from proposing the kind again, is private with no consent (the table's CHECKs), and the card shows it as "You said no, or not yet" with "Actually, it is me", so a later "I wrote THE END" still works. Closing the dialog in any way ("Close for now", Escape) counts as "not yet" for the questions still open, so we never ask twice. Staff see these rows among the declined on `/admin/finishers`.
- **"Yes" opens the card's answer form** (`MilestoneForm`, reused from `MilestonesPanel`, not copied: optional date, optional stories, the share tick unticked, whatever level it offers) and records the milestone for that project as `source = 'project_stage'`, `status = 'confirmed'`, the member's own action (`answerStageMilestone`, `addOwn` with `source: "project_stage"`), then says the same celebration as the card. Nothing is created on "Not yet" except the declined row above.
- **Going back a stage, or to Hibernating, never cancels or changes a milestone.** A milestone says what the member did, not where the project is now; they can still take back one they made (including one from this prompt) on the card. This closes the question left open in `docs/product/WRITER_TITLES.md`.
- **Publishing a book from the Publish flow** does not ask: the book itself is the third milestone, and the member can mark the other two on the card.

The insert policy "Members answer a project stage prompt" allows only: their own row, never in sudo, source `project_stage`, status confirmed or declined, one of their own live projects, no book and no title. Sudo and admins cannot answer for a member.

## Where it shows

- **The member's profile** (`/members/<id>`): a "Milestones" strip with one line per milestone that is at community or public, read through `milestones_shown_to_members`: the title it earns (Finisher, Author), what they did, the work (the title the member wrote, or the book's; a milestone of a project shows no work name, because a project's name follows the project's own level), the day when they gave one and the story when they wrote one. The member sees the same list as anyone: their own private ones are not there. The third milestone, Published, is the Published Books section, not part of this strip.
- **The writer profile on the web** (`/w/<handle>`, and the member's own preview): the same strip for milestones at the globe only, behind the `writer_profiles` flag and the page being on, with the same 404 for a page that is off. A story is plain text, never markup.
- **Caching.** Both pages are rendered on demand (the production build lists `/w/[handle]` and `/members/[id]` as dynamic), so each request reads the database again and a withdrawn level, a taken-back milestone or an unticked share tick is gone on the next request. Every milestone action also calls `revalidatePath` for the card, `/members/<id>` and the writer profile's path, as the profile items do, in case a page is ever cached. No response header was measured against the production CDN.
- **The share consent** is read by nothing in the Hub yet: the Finishers section of the website and the testimonials we share are not built (`docs/TODO.md`, "Finisher record: what is left"). When they are, they must read `consent_testimonial` (with `status = 'confirmed'` and not deleted) at the moment they render or export, through the service role, so a withdrawn tick takes effect on the next read, and must not copy a story into a static file, a spreadsheet or an email. The wording promises only the website and what we share about our writers, and only what the member wrote in that form: no photo (milestone photos are not built) and no name (the website use is not designed), so neither is mentioned.

## The Finisher and Author badges

Two automatic, leveled badges (`finisher`, `author`) count the milestones, the way Published Author counts Bookshelf books ("5x Finisher", "3x Author", "2x Published Author"). Levels at 1, 3, 5 and 10 works, seeded like Published Author's. Nothing is stored per member: the level is computed when a profile is read (`src/lib/badges.ts`), so there is no award row and no "you earned a badge" message; the chip simply appears.

- **What counts.** Finisher is the number of confirmed, live THE END milestones, Author the number of confirmed, live publish-ready ones, one per work (the table allows one live row per work and kind). Proposed, declined and taken-back rows never count. Published Author is unchanged: it counts Bookshelf books, not milestone 3.
- **While a member.** One function, `reachedWhileMember` (`src/lib/finishers.ts`): a milestone with no date counts, a dated one counts when its date is on or after the member's first join date (`members.first_joined_at`, the same date Founding Hedgie reads), and with no join date on record a dated one counts too. The milestone still shows on the profile whatever its date. Note that the Published Author badge says "while a member" in its description but its code does not apply a date rule (it counts every live book); that is a separate decision (`docs/TODO.md`).
- **Who sees what.** A member looking at their own profile sees every confirmed milestone counted. Anyone else (another member, staff on the member page or the recipients list, the chat profile card) sees only the milestones at community or public, so a badge never gives away a private milestone. `getMemberBadges` takes the viewer ("self" or "others", default "others"). The single-member path reads `ofMember` (self) or `shownToMembers` (others); the all-members path (`/admin/badges/<id>/recipients`) reads `allShownToMembers`, one paginated scan with kind and date only. The web profile shows no badges.
- **First computation.** Both badges come from the migration with no award rows, so nothing is sent when they first appear; members with confirmed milestones at community or public level see the chip on their next profile view.

## Staff

`/admin/finishers` (admin only): counts of proposed, confirmed and declined, the list (50 a page, optionally one status), "Propose from published books" and "Add one by hand" (member picker, then one of their works or a title, then the milestone). The list shows the work of each row.

- **Backfill, per book.** `propose_milestones_for_books()` looks at every live (not deleted) book and proposes both kinds for that work unless the work already has a row of that kind in any status (withdrawn ones included, so a decline or a take-back stays). A book and its project are one work, so a row on either keeps the book from being proposed again; a member with several books gets several proposals. One set-based statement run as the calling admin, so it has no page limit, and a second run adds nothing. It returns proposals, members and books, which the page shows ("N proposals for M members across K books"). Members who cannot sign in (lapsed) still get proposals; they stay hidden. The older `propose_milestones_from_books()` (one pair per member) is still in the database until the contract step (`docs/TODO.md`).
- **Backfill and a member's own.** A work the member already added, or took back, is not proposed that kind again.
- **Hand-add** creates one proposal for one work (`loadWorksOfMember` lists the member's projects and books for the picker, or a title); "Take back" soft-deletes a proposal the member has not answered.

## Data, removal and the Activity Log

- **Removal.** `member_data_handling` marks `member_milestones.member_id` as `delete`, so a member's removal request deletes the rows (and the entries about them leave the audit log, like the rest of their data). They are never part of the "keep my public work" choice.
- **Activity Log.** A proposal, a member adding their own ("added the THE END milestone"), a confirmation, a decline, a change of who can see it ("made a writing milestone private", "showed a writing milestone to other members", "showed a writing milestone on their public writer profile"), a change of the share tick, a take-back and an answer to the project-stage prompt ("added the milestone when a project moved forward", "said not yet to the milestone when a project moved forward") are audit entries on the member (`member_milestone`), with the stories and the work's title redacted; `describeRow` in `src/lib/activity-feed.ts` says them as sentences without dates or words the member wrote.
- **Analytics.** The member's answer is the `milestone_answered` action outcome ("confirmed" or "declined"); adding their own is `milestone_added` ("added" or "already_there": per work) and taking it back `milestone_taken_back`; the answer to the project-stage prompt is `milestone_stage_answered` ("confirmed", "not_yet" or "already_there"); a change of level is `milestone_visibility_set` (the level, a fixed word) and the share tick `milestone_share_set` ("shared" or "withdrawn"). No content in any.
