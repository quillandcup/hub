# Courses in the Hub

> **Status:** living. **Last reviewed:** 2026-11-25. Review: yearly. Kept up to date with the code; if it is wrong, fix it.

Quill & Cup's courses live in the Hub, synced from Kajabi until the Hub's course builder is good enough to replace it. Courses are open to every member (changelog entry "Courses in the Hub", `src/content/changelog.ts`, slug `courses-open-to-everyone`): members find the courses they have access to under `/courses`, watch lessons in the Hub's own player, mark them complete, pick up where they stopped (also from "Continue watching" on the dashboard), and download lesson files. Staff import courses from Kajabi, publish them, and can duplicate, take over or delete them and build Hub-owned courses in the course editor. This doc is the plan and the record for moving Quill & Cup's courses from Kajabi into the Hub, starting with **The Ready Writer**, the first course; update it as decisions land.


**Two independent axes.** Where a course is authored is `courses.source` (`kajabi`: read-only here, rebuilt on sync; `hub`: edited in the builder). Whether members can open it is `courses.is_draft`, for every course whatever its source. A new course (imported or Hub-made) starts as a draft; **Publish** and **Unpublish** on the course's admin page (`setCoursePublished`) flip it. Publishing a Kajabi course also builds its access (the people who bought it get it); unpublishing keeps the rows but opens nothing. A draft is hidden from members and from staff in sudo, and visible to staff acting as themselves (marked "Draft") to preview. Syncing is separate from availability: the Kajabi import gives every course a row (`ensureDraftCourses`) and processing syncs the content of every imported course, drafts included, so lessons have Hub ids and the builder pages work before publishing; only access follows `is_draft`. The Import Data publish step posts `publish: true` to `/api/process/courses`; without it processing never publishes.

**Admin addresses use Hub ids, never Kajabi's.** `/admin/courses/<crs_ id>`, `/lessons/<les_ id>`, `/stats` and `/roster` all take our ids; the Kajabi id stays the importers' natural key and is resolved server-side (`kajabiIdOfCourse`). A course imported before its row exists is listed without a link until the next import.

## Decisions so far

- **The Ready Writer goes first and launches in the Hub.** It's an **evergreen** program, not cohort-based. Its first run launches together, and lessons are released as they're finished (a release schedule for content, not drip for its own sake).
- **Member experience first, course builder later.** Ania keeps authoring in Kajabi's editor, **including after launch, until the Hub's course builder is good enough to replace it.** So the Kajabi → Hub migration isn't a one-off: it's an **ongoing sync**, safe to re-run any time, and run once more right before launch.
- **The full course outline is visible from day one.** Unreleased lessons show as "Coming soon" (see the lesson-state rules under Silver).
- **Access is configured per course.** The Ready Writer is **one year from purchase and includes membership**. Other courses may be lifetime or not include membership.
- **The Ready Writer's existing `programs` row is linked** to the course (or replaced, if it doesn't fit), so Big Sisters and membership status keep working from one record.
- **Evergreen courses only, for now.** The data model leaves room for cohort-based courses (Self-Editing Academy, the 180) without building them.
- **Kajabi stays the checkout.** Buying the offer in Kajabi is what grants access in the Hub.
- **Video host: Mux.** Our own themed player on top (Video.js v10 if a half-day test passes, Vidstack otherwise) and our own per-member watch tracking. See "Video" below.
- **Captions are burned into the existing videos** (Descript), so nothing caption-related blocks launch.

## Terms: program, course, Kajabi product

Kajabi's "product" is broad: a course, community, coaching, podcast, newsletter. The Hub already splits that into separate ideas, and the course work keeps them separate:

| Term | What it is | Lives in |
|---|---|---|
| **Program** | Something a member buys or enrolls in, as the business tracks it: its Kajabi offers (`kajabi_offer_names`), whether it includes membership, cohorts and enrollments, badges, Big Sisters, the leakage report. Exists today. | `programs`, `program_cohorts`, `member_program_enrollments` |
| **Course** | The curriculum a program delivers: modules, weeks, lessons, videos, downloads, and member progress. New in this plan. | `courses`, `course_modules`, `course_lessons` |

- **A program may have no course.** BFF is matching and coaching; retreats are events (`community_events`); the membership itself is subscription logic, not a program.
- **A course belongs to at most one program** (`courses.program_id`), and the program is where access and membership rules live (`programs.includes_membership`), so those rules stay in one place.
- **We don't build a separate `kajabi_products` catalog** (the old idea in `../decisions/KAJABI_DATA_MODEL.md`). `programs` plus its Kajabi offer names covers it. Communities (Hub Chat replaces them), podcasts and newsletters aren't enrollments we track. If a non-course program needs to be told apart later, add `programs.kind` then.

## What Kajabi gives us

Verified 2026-10-17 against the Ready Writer, 180, Self-Editing Academy and membership courses.

| Piece | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| Courses, modules (including nested "weeks"), lessons, order, draft/published/locked status, video length, offers | **Kajabi public API**: `/v1/courses/{id}?include=modules,lessons,lessons.media,offers` (client credentials, the same ones the purchase sync uses). Modules nest through `parent_module`. |
| Lesson text (rich HTML), PDF downloads, lesson thumbnails, module posters | **Not in the API.** The admin lesson editor (`/admin/posts/{id}/edit`) shows them. |
| Video files | **Not in the API.** Each lesson's "Video actions → Download video" link in the admin editor. There is no bulk export. |
| Quizzes (e.g. Self-Editing Academy's reflections) | **Not in the API.** The admin quiz editor (`/admin/quizzes/{id}/edit`, reached from the quiz lesson's editor), captured with the lesson content; see "Quizzes". |

So the migration has two kinds of steps: **API steps** that can run on the server, and **session steps** that need a logged-in Kajabi admin browser. The session steps are a local script that Ania or Cody runs; they log in themselves, and the script reuses that browser session. The script never handles a password.

**Day-1 spike result (2026-10-18, on a duplicate draft of a Ready Writer lesson; nothing published):**

- **Lesson text, video and downloads come from the editor's own JSON.** Opening a lesson loads `GET /api/admin/posts/{postId}` (JSON:API): `body` (the lesson HTML), `title`, `position`, `status`, `posterImageUrl`, and an included `video` object. `GET /api/admin/posts/{postId}/downloads` lists the attachments. The course outline is `GET /api/admin/products/{productId}/posts`.
- **That JSON API is not cookie-authenticated.** A plain `fetch` from the page, or any script replaying the cookies, gets 401. The editor sends its own auth. So step B must **drive the real editor** and record the responses as each lesson loads (Playwright response listeners), not call the API itself, and it must not read tokens out of the page.
- **The video object carries what step D needs:** Kajabi media `id`, `wistiaId`, `originalVideo` (an `.mp4` path), `status`, `mediaLibraryAssetId` (set for videos uploaded through the newer Media Library) and `urls.downloadUrl`. The lessons' videos are **hosted on Wistia**; the S3 original is what we download.
- **`downloadUrl` is an S3 presigned URL** (AWS signature v4, host `kajabi-storefronts-production.s3.amazonaws.com`, `X-Amz-Expires=604800`, i.e. 7 days). The editor mints a fresh one on every load, so step B should capture it right when step D needs it (or run D soon after B). Downloads (PDFs) use the same kind of presigned S3 link (also readable with no cookie), with the original file name in the path. A `/downloads` item is `{ id, type: "download", attributes: { name, displayName, displayAssetName, position, assetUrl, mediaLibraryAssetId } }`, and the response's `meta.uploadConfig` is the uploader's own config, which the script should ignore. **Confirmed:** it needs no cookie, and **Mux ingests straight from it** (`POST /video/v1/assets` with `inputs: [{ url }]`, `video_quality: "basic"`, signed playback policy): a 156 MB, 9:05 lesson video was `ready` about 10 seconds after the request, with the right duration, so nothing is downloaded locally. **The link is signed for `GET` only:** a `HEAD` returns 403. For the change fingerprint, use `GET` with `Range: bytes=0-0` instead: it returns 206 with the total size in `Content-Range`, plus `ETag`, `Last-Modified` and `Accept-Ranges`.
- **A replaced video gets a new media ID.** Removing a lesson's video and attaching another one changed the video `id` (and `wistiaId`). **Duplicating a lesson also creates a new media ID** (and a new `wistiaId`) for the copy of the same file. So the fingerprint in "Telling whether a video changed" works for replacements, and the SHA-256 reuse step matters for duplicates.
- **A transcript uploaded in Kajabi can be read back.** "Video actions → Closed captions → Upload transcript" takes an SRT. `GET /api/admin/media/video_localizations/{mediaId}/transcriptions` then lists it (`languageCode`, `status`, `manual: true`) but not the text. The text comes from Wistia's public embed JSON, **`https://fast.wistia.com/embed/medias/{wistiaId}.json`** (the `captions` entry holds `language` and `text`), with no login. It is the plain text, so the Kajabi route works for Ania, but the Hub's Videos screen stays the primary path since it keeps the timed `.vtt` and the file itself.
- **A transcript doesn't follow the video.** The replacement video (new media ID) started with no transcript, so a replaced video needs its caption re-uploaded.
- **Capture gotchas:** the editor caches `/downloads` and the post JSON within a page session, so a script that opens a lesson a second time in one session sees no new request. And a *direct* load of a lesson's edit URL doesn't request the post JSON at all; reach it through the app's router (see "Step B in practice").
- **Not yet checked:** a lesson from the real course that has PDFs (the shape above is from a test attachment on the duplicate).

## Importing Kajabi per-lesson progress

**Wanted** (decided 2026-10-28): a member who is halfway through a Kajabi course arrives in the Hub with those lessons complete in `lesson_progress`, and ideally the last-watched position. The Ready Writer starts fresh in the Hub, so the likelier users are the courses that move later (Self-Editing Academy, BFF Mindset Training, Hedgies on First). Status: **the Product Progress report import is built** (percent per student, Bronze only, see "Product Progress report import" below); the member-confirmed catch-up that turns it into marked lessons is not built yet.

### What Kajabi exposes

**Verified 2026-10-28 (read-only, client credentials, public API v1; counts and field names only).** The spec in `src/lib/kajabi/openapi.yaml` and live probes agree: **there is no per-lesson progress in the public API.**

| Endpoint | Result |
|---|---|
| `GET /v1/contacts` (952 contacts) | Profile fields only (name, email, address, phone, `subscribed`, `external_user_id`, timestamps). Relationships: `site`, `offers`. |
| `GET /v1/customers` (300 customers) | Adds `sign_in_count`, `last_sign_in_at`, `last_request_at`, `net_revenue`, public profile. Relationships: `site`, `contact`, `offers`, `products`. No course data. |
| `GET /v1/purchases`, `/v1/order_items`, `/v1/offers`, `/v1/courses`, `/v1/products` | Who bought what and when (already used by the purchase and course sync). `products` has `members_aggregate_count` (a count per product, not per member). `courses` has modules and lessons, no student data. |
| `GET /v1/contacts?filter[completed_assessment_id]=…` (also `passed_` and `failed_`, and the same on `/v1/customers`) | Answers 200. A **quiz-level** yes/no per assessment, but the API has no endpoint that lists assessments and their ids, so the ids would have to come from the admin app. Not lesson progress. |
| `/v1/enrollments`, `/progress`, `/lesson_progress`, `/lesson_completions`, `/completions`, `/assessments`, `/quizzes`, `/quiz_results`, `/assessment_submissions`, `/activity`, `/events`, `/reports`, `/lessons`, `/posts`, `/members`, `/v1/courses/{id}/enrollments`, `/progress`, `/students`, `/relationships/contacts`, `/v1/contacts/{id}/progress`, `/relationships/courses`, `/enrollments` | **404** (not endpoints). |
| `/v1/course_progress`, `/v1/hooks` | **403** with our credentials. `/v1/hooks` is webhooks, which the spec lists only for tags, forms, purchases, payments and orders, none for lesson completion. `course_progress` was a guess that answered differently from the 404s, so ask Kajabi whether it is a real, permission-gated endpoint. |

The one usable signal is coarse: `last_sign_in_at` / `last_request_at` per customer (when they last visited Kajabi at all, not which lesson).

**Unverified (needs a logged-in Kajabi admin browser, which an agent cannot do).** Kajabi's admin app very likely has the data, since a product's Students page shows per-student progress, and the editor's own JSON API (`/api/admin/...`, see "What Kajabi gives us") is what such pages call. By analogy with `/api/admin/products/{id}/posts` we would expect a per-product students list with a completion percentage and a per-student view with completed lessons, but **the paths, fields and whether per-lesson completion dates exist are guesses.** Expect the same auth constraint as the editor JSON (not cookie-replayable, 401 outside the page), so a script would have to drive the real pages and record responses, as `scripts/kajabi-course-content.ts` does.

### What the user checks (about 15 minutes, in their own logged-in browser)

1. Open the product (e.g. Self-Editing Academy) in Kajabi admin, then its **Students** (or **Contacts**, filtered by the product) page. Is there a per-student **progress %** column? Does a student's detail page list lessons with a completed mark or date?
2. Open dev tools, Network, filter on `Fetch/XHR`, reload that page, then open one student. Note the request URLs and the JSON field names that carry completion. Do not paste any student's name or email anywhere public; field names and one redacted sample are enough.
3. Look for **Reports**/**Analytics** (course completion, quiz results) and an **Export CSV** button on the Students page. A CSV with one row per student and lesson (or one column per lesson) would be enough on its own.
4. Ask Kajabi support: "Is there an API or export for per-student, per-lesson completion and last position? Is `/v1/course_progress` a real endpoint?"
5. Check whether Kajabi records completion per lesson for each course at all: it only does when students click "Complete and continue", and a video-only course may show nothing.

### Options, in the order to prefer them

1. **Kajabi export or admin JSON (best, if checks 1 to 3 find it).** Same shape as the other session steps: a local script (`pnpm courses:progress --course <slug>`) run by Ania or Cody in their own logged-in browser; it never sees a password. It reads progress from the page's own responses (or ingests a CSV the user downloads) into Bronze.
2. **Coarse import from what the public API has.** Treat a lesson as done only for members with a clear signal (a passed assessment via the `completed_assessment_id` filter, once ids are known) and otherwise start at zero. Cheap and safe, but only useful for quiz-bearing courses (Self-Editing Academy).
3. **Self-service "catch me up".** A one-time prompt on the course outline when a migrated member has access and no progress: "Mark everything up to here as done" (a lesson or module picker). It writes ordinary `lesson_progress` rows as the member, needs no Kajabi data and is honest about being self-reported. Good as the fallback for every course and as the repair path when an import is partial.
4. **Derive from Hub signals.** Not possible before the member has used the Hub (no watch segments yet), so it only helps after migration. Do not infer completion from a purchase date.

Recommended: run the checks above; if a usable source exists build option 1 (with option 3 as the member-facing repair), otherwise build option 3 alone. Nothing in The Ready Writer launch depends on this.

### Product Progress report import (built)

**The source is known (decided 2026-10-28):** Kajabi's admin has a **Product Progress** report per product (with an **Export** button). The CSV has one row per student with `Name, Email, Product Progress, Logins, Start Date, Last Activity At`. `Product Progress` is a 0 to 100 percentage of the **whole product**, not per lesson; the start date reads "Not Started" for people who have not begun. The file does not say which product it is for, so the admin picks the course when uploading. It needs no browser session and no script.

**Per-lesson detail exists (confirmed 2026-10-29 from a screenshot).** Each student has a "Customer Progress" page in the Kajabi admin (click a name in the Product Progress report): their overall percent and last activity, a list of modules and weeks with a percent or check each, and, after clicking a module or week, the lessons in it, each with a check and a "Completed: date" or a percent for one in progress. So Kajabi does record per-lesson completion with a date. The page loads a week's lessons only when it is clicked, so the capture has to open each module for each student. **Decided: this is part of `npm run courses:content`** (a `--progress` mode sharing its browser login), not a manual download and upload: it reads the report page for the roster (percent, logins, start date, last activity) and each student's page for the lessons, and writes Bronze like the other steps (dry run unless `--apply`). The CSV upload above stays as the fallback. **First step, built:** `courses:content --probe <page address>` opens a page in the logged-in browser, records the JSON it loads while you click around (open a week, a module), and prints request paths and field names and types, never values. Run it on the Product Progress page and on one student's page, paste what it prints, and the capture is written from those facts.

**What the import does** (admin Import Data page, "Kajabi Course Progress"; `src/app/api/import/kajabi-product-progress/route.ts`, `src/lib/courses/product-progress.ts`):

- Writes **`bronze.kajabi_product_progress`** only: natural key (Kajabi course id, lowercased email), `percent`, `logins`, `started_on`, `last_activity_on` (the report gives dates, not times), `import_batch`, `source_file`, `imported_at`, `deleted_at`. The student's **name is not stored** (matching is by email, and the admin who uploads already has the file). Admin read, service role write. It **does not write `lesson_progress`**.
- **Re-runnable per course.** A new report replaces the course's snapshot: new and changed rows are upserted, rows the file no longer lists get `deleted_at`, identical rows are left untouched (re-importing the same file changes nothing), other courses are never touched. Any invalid row rejects the whole file (422, with line numbers and reasons, nothing written), so a malformed line can never soft-delete a student.
- **Parser** (`src/lib/courses/product-progress-csv.ts`): columns by header name (case-insensitive, any order, extras ignored; Email and Product Progress required), tolerates a BOM, CRLF, quoted fields with commas, quotes and line breaks, a percent with or without `%` or decimals, and "Not Started", blank or missing dates.
- **Matching** is by email only, never by name: the member's email, or an active email alias that resolves to it (`member_email_aliases`), through the members and aliases repositories. The response gives counts (rows read, imported, matched, unmatched, ambiguous = one email, several members) and lists the unmatched emails back to the admin (not logged). Unmatched and ambiguous rows are still kept in Bronze, so they apply later if the member appears or the duplicate is merged.
- **Read for the catch-up:** `memberProductProgress(db, repos, memberId, courseKajabiId)` returns a member's imported percent, logins and dates for a course (or null). If the report lists several of the member's addresses, the highest percent wins.

**Turning a percentage into marked lessons is a separate step, not built** (docs/TODO.md, "Catch-up from imported Kajabi progress"): when Self-Editing Academy migrates, the member sees what Kajabi said (for example 62%) on the course outline and confirms "mark everything up to here as done". The percentage never completes lessons on its own, because it cannot say which ones were done.

### Per-lesson capture and Silver step (built; the capture dry-ran on a real product 2026-10-30)

**What `--probe` showed (2026-10-29; field names and types only).** Both are session-authenticated `/api/client/...` calls on `app.kajabi.com`, the ones the Product Progress report pages make:

- **Roster:** `GET /api/client/reports/product_progress/{productId}?productId=…&send_to_background=…&site_id=…` returns `{ count, filters, segments, status, members[] }`; each member has `contact_id`, `member_id`, `email`, `name`, `percent_complete`, `sign_in_count`, `start_date`, `last_request_at`. Whether it pages is unknown, so the capture compares `count` with what it got.
- **One student:** the same path plus `/members/{memberId}` returns `{ member, product{ title, total_progress, categories[] } }`. Each `categories[]` entry (a module or week, nested by `parent_category`) has `posts[]` with `id`, `title`, `completed`, `completed_date` (a date or a timestamp), `progress_percent`. One response already holds every week, so nothing is clicked.

**What the first real dry run showed (2026-10-30, a product with 41 students; counts only).** All 41 students were captured with no failures, 2,132 lessons listed (1,088 completed), and none of the lessons was missing from Bronze, so Kajabi's post id does match `kajabi_course_lessons.kajabi_id`. Two things the probe could not show, learned the hard way and now handled: (1) Kajabi builds the roster report in the background, so the page's first roster answer can be empty (`send_to_background`, a `status`), and the capture waits for a response that has students and asks again at the same address; (2) the per-student request answers 401 to a plain in-page `fetch` even when the window is logged in, so the capture copies the headers of the page's own successful roster request (never logging their values) onto each student request. Roster paging was not an issue at 41 students; whether a much larger product pages is still unknown (the capture warns when `count` differs from what arrived).

**Capture: `npm run courses:content -- --course <kajabi-course-id> --progress [--apply]`** (`scripts/kajabi-course-content.ts`, `src/lib/kajabi/progress-run.ts`, `src/lib/kajabi/progress-capture.ts`). Same browser login as the lesson capture, dry run unless `--apply`. It opens `/admin/sites/{KAJABI_SITE_ID}/reports/product_progress_report?productId={courseId}`, records the roster response the page makes (this also gives the exact URL and query string), then fetches each student's URL (same origin, path + `/members/{id}`, same query) with `fetch` inside that page, two at a time with a short pause, one retry, and stops on a 401. It prints counts only (students, lessons, rows written, unchanged, removed, failures), never names or emails. **The in-page `fetch` is unverified**: no one could log in to Kajabi while it was written. If Kajabi refuses it, the message names the status and the path (with the student id masked) and nothing for that student is written; the first dry run is the test.

**Bronze.** The roster goes to the existing `bronze.kajabi_product_progress` through the same save function as the CSV upload (`src/lib/courses/product-progress-store.ts`), so the two cannot disagree and the CSV card stays as a fallback. A roster whose `count` differs from what it sent removes no one. Lessons go to the new **`bronze.kajabi_lesson_progress`** (`course_kajabi_id, email, kajabi_lesson_id`, Kajabi contact and member ids as text, `completed`, `completed_at`, `percent`, `captured_at`, `deleted_at`; admin read, service role write). Each student is a snapshot: changed lessons are updated, lessons no longer listed are soft-deleted, an unchanged capture writes nothing, and a student whose report failed, lists no lessons or is for another student writes nothing. A bare completion date is read as noon in `ORG_TIMEZONE`. The Kajabi post id is expected to equal `kajabi_course_lessons.kajabi_id`; the summary counts captured lessons Bronze does not have, so a mismatch shows up as a large number there.

**Silver: `POST /api/process/course-progress`** `{ "course": "<slug>", "dryRun": true }` (admin; `src/lib/courses/progress-import.ts`). Insert-only, because Hub progress is member-written:

- For each completed, live Bronze row it finds the member by email or active alias (never by name; an email several members share is skipped) and the lesson by Kajabi source key. **It matches any existing member (current, lapsed, cancelled, on hiatus) and needs no `course_access` row** (decided 2026-11-25: losing Kajabi must not lose anyone's record of what they completed). A member without access can't open the lesson, so nothing shows them or others anything new; the rows are there if access returns. Importing grants no access.
- **A removed person never comes back.** A member removal deletes their Bronze rows and keeps a fingerprint of their address (`removal_tombstones`, "Removal on request" under "Importing past quiz responses"); triggers keep the capture from storing them again, and this import also skips any Bronze row whose address is fingerprinted (counted as `removed`), so even a row that slipped in writes nothing. A row can only be written for a member who exists.
- It inserts a `lesson_progress` row with Kajabi's `completed_at`, position 0 and `imported_from = 'kajabi'` (so imported rows can be told apart and removed), only when the member has no row for that lesson. Any existing row, completed or not, is left alone: an incomplete one may be one the member un-completed, which cannot be told from a started one. It never lowers, overwrites or removes anything, writes no `member_activities`, and running it twice inserts nothing the second time.
- **Counted, not written** (per course, in the dry run and the real run): lessons it would add / members already completed / rows left alone (a started or un-completed row); students (distinct emails) matched / **kept, no member yet** (no member has that address; the rows stay in Bronze and a later run applies them once a member with that address or alias exists; members are never created from this) / ambiguous (shared email) / removed; completed lessons with no Silver lesson (orphans, never guessed; they apply on a later run once synced).
- Imported completions count wherever `lesson_progress.completed_at` is read (the member's own course progress, including a course whose access has ended). **Readers checked 2026-11-25 for a member with progress but no access:** the roster ("who's where") is built from `course_access` rows, so a lapsed member's progress is never listed there; the member course list and "continue watching" start from the member's access rows (and continue watching reads only unfinished lessons), so a member with no access sees nothing new; marking a lesson complete and the watch route check access first; deleting a lesson or course still refuses when any member has progress in it, which now includes imported completions of lapsed members (their record is protected); member merge and removal repoint the rows (removal to the Former-member placeholder, which has no address, so nothing matches it). No badge rule reads `lesson_progress`.

Order: run step A and `/api/process/courses` (so lessons and `course_access` exist), `--progress`, then this.

### Design, if per-lesson data is found


- **Bronze `bronze.kajabi_lesson_progress`**: one row per `(contact reference, kajabi_lesson_id)` with `completed_at` (nullable), `position_seconds` (nullable), `percent` (nullable, if only that is given), `raw_payload`, `fetched_at`, `deleted_at`. Upserted on the natural key; a row missing from a fully successful fetch is soft-deleted, and a failed or partial fetch deletes nothing (as in the other Bronze imports). Service role only, since it is member data.
- **Identity**: Kajabi contact id (or email as a fallback) to `members` through the members repository (`kajabi_id`, then canonical email), **never by name**. Rows that match no member are counted and kept in Bronze, not guessed; they apply later if the member appears.
- **Silver**: a step after the lessons exist (it needs no `course_access`: see "Silver: `POST /api/process/course-progress`" above for the rule as built) maps Kajabi lesson ids to `course_lessons` and **inserts missing `lesson_progress` rows only**. It never overwrites a row the member already has, never lowers or removes a `completed_at`, and keeps the earlier `completed_at` when both exist. Hub progress is member-written and Local (never reprocessed), so this is an insert-if-absent merge, not DELETE + INSERT; re-running is a no-op. Mark imported rows (a `source` column, `kajabi_import` or `hub`, via a migration) so they can be told apart and a bad import is reversible.
- **Orphans**: a Kajabi lesson with no Silver lesson (deleted, or still a draft) is skipped and counted; once that lesson is synced, the next run picks up its progress.
- **Access and launch**: not limited to members with a `course_access` row (decided 2026-11-25). For a course not yet open in the Hub (`launch_at`), the import can run early so members arrive with progress on day one, but nothing shows before launch. Cohort courses (`courses.kind = 'cohort'`) would also need the member's cohort and level, which an import does not carry; defer that until a cohort course is built.
- **"Complete" means Kajabi's mark, not 90%.** Kajabi completion is an explicit "Complete" click (or the lesson's own rule); the Hub completes at 90% of the playhead or by the button. Take Kajabi's completed flag as complete, with `completed_at` from Kajabi if given, else the fetch date. Never convert a percentage into complete. A last position is imported only if Kajabi gives seconds, and only when the Hub row has none.
- **Activity and analytics**: imported rows do not write `member_activities` (they are not Hub viewing), so the engagement score and Activity Log are not inflated.
- **Privacy**: progress is member data. Nothing real in the repo, PR or docs; tests use fake members; reports are counts and shapes. The import takes its input from outside the repo (the browser session or a downloaded CSV kept out of git).
- **Tests** (per `.claude/rules/testing-requirements.md`): Bronze idempotency (first import, re-import without duplicates, changed data updates, soft delete only after a full fetch); Silver reprocessability (insert-if-absent, never overwrites newer Hub progress, orphan lessons and unmatched members skipped, no access means no rows); a parser test over a fake fixture; and a pgTAP check that the Bronze table is service role only.

## Data model

This follows the medallion rules in `.claude/rules/data-architecture.md`: Bronze mirrors Kajabi, upserted on Kajabi's IDs and soft-deleted with `deleted_at` when gone from a fully successful fetch; Silver is rebuilt from Bronze plus Local; Local is ours.

### Bronze (raw Kajabi)

- `bronze.kajabi_courses`, `bronze.kajabi_course_modules`, `bronze.kajabi_course_lessons`: from the API. Each has `kajabi_id` (unique), the parent ID, `position`, `publishing_option`, `title`, `raw_payload`, `fetched_at` and `deleted_at`.
- Lesson content, from the session script: `body_html`, `thumbnail_url`, `video_source` (Kajabi's media ID and length), `content_fetched_at` and `content_hash`.
- `bronze.kajabi_lesson_files`: PDFs and other downloads per lesson, by Kajabi file ID.
- `bronze.kajabi_quizzes`: a quiz lesson's quiz as the quiz editor delivered it (raw documents, by Kajabi's quiz ID); Silver reads it into `quizzes`, `quiz_questions` and `quiz_options` (see "Quizzes").
- `bronze.kajabi_course_offers`: which offers grant which course.

### Silver (what members see)

- `courses`, `course_modules` and `course_lessons`. **Upserted on their source key and orphans removed, like `prickles`,** so IDs and URLs (`/courses/the-ready-writer/lessons/<id>`) stay stable across re-runs.
  - Each row has `source` (`kajabi` now, `hub` once the builder exists), so the Kajabi reprocess never touches Hub-authored rows.
  - `course_modules.parent_id` handles weeks inside modules.
- **One model for imported and Hub-owned courses: settings in, state out** (migration `20270401000000`). A module and a lesson each store their **own setting** (`publishing_option`), and the **state** members get (`open`, `coming_soon`, `hidden`) is worked out from them by one rule, the same for both owners; only `courses.source` differs, and it is the switch that locks editing (the database policies key on it). Whether a **course** is available to members at all is a separate switch, `courses.is_draft` (Publish / Unpublish on the course page, for both owners), and is not part of the item rule: items work out their state as if the course were open, and a draft course is simply not reachable. Each module and lesson has its own (`draft`, `published`, `locked`; `scheduled` and `drip` are kept for imported rows, and need a date or delay before the editor offers them: `docs/TODO.md`). A new Hub-owned item starts as a **draft**, like Kajabi's, so nothing goes live until someone publishes it. (An earlier version also had a setting that followed what is above; Kajabi has no such thing, nothing used it, and marking everything in a section Published and switching the section covers the same need, so it was removed.) **The rule:** an item's own setting gives its visibility (published is open; locked or drip is coming soon; a draft, no setting or anything unknown is hidden), and nothing is more visible than what is above it (for a top-level module or a lesson in no module there is nothing above, so it is limited by nothing). It is `itemState` and `planFullTree` in `src/lib/courses/visibility.ts` for the Kajabi sync, and `course_item_state` and `recompute_course_states` in the database for Hub-owned courses, with the same cases tested in both (`tests/lib/courses-visibility-rule.test.ts`, `supabase/tests/database/course_publishing_settings.test.sql`). Kajabi rows keep whatever Kajabi says (the sync stores it in `publishing_option` beside the state); the editor changes a Hub-owned item with `set_course_item_status` (admin, Hub-owned only, `unsupported_status` for what isn't offered). **Nothing is refused for what is above an item**: a lesson, or a whole section, can be marked Published under a draft module, and waits, hidden, until what is above it is switched on. That is the workflow: prepare a section, mark everything in it Published, then open or close all of it with the section's own setting alone (and Kajabi does the same: "Meet Your Instructor" is Published inside the draft module "Welcome to the Quill & Cup 180!"). The row says what is holding it ("Inside “Welcome…” (Draft)"). Changing a setting is **one audit entry for the thing changed**; the states underneath that merely follow are not audited row by row (`docs/architecture/ACTIVITY_AND_AUDIT_LOG.md`, "Record the intent, not the noise"). The Hub outline of a Hub-owned course is explained by the same `explainCourse` as an imported one's, so the reasons read alike ("Published", "Inside “Module 2” (Locked)").
- **Lesson state** from Kajabi's publishing option (modules follow the same rules, and a lesson is never more visible than its module):
  - `published` → open.
  - `locked`/`drip` → **"Coming soon"**: the title (and module) is visible but can't be opened. This is how the full course plan appears from day one.
  - `draft` → hidden. Drafts are work in progress, and some of The Ready Writer's are still leftovers copied from the 180, so they must not leak. They *are* stored in the Hub (`state = 'hidden'`) so staff can preview them, and members never get them: the member loaders leave hidden rows out of every read (`loadStructure`), row-level security backs that up for lessons and modules (migration `20261129000100`), and a hidden lesson answers "not found", not "coming soon", so even its title isn't confirmed.
  - **Convention for Ania:** a planned lesson that should appear as "Coming soon" is set to *Locked* in Kajabi; publishing it in Kajabi opens it in the Hub on the next sync.
- `course_lessons.body_html`: **sanitized on import** with an allowlist (headings, lists, links, emphasis, images), never rendered raw.
- `course_access`: who can open which course, with `starts_at` and `ends_at`.
  - Derived from active Kajabi purchases of the course's offers (`deactivated_at IS NULL`), plus `course_access_grants`.
  - Rebuilt on every member/purchase reprocess, so a refund removes access.
  - `starts_at` is the purchase date (the same coalesced date cohort matching uses: `created_at_kajabi`, else `effective_start_at`). `ends_at` is `starts_at + courses.access_duration`, or null for lifetime.
  - **When access ends the course stays visible, but nothing inside it opens** (decided 2026-11-02, after a first build that hid the course was undone): it stays in the member's `/courses` list marked as ended, and its overview page shows the outline and their progress. A member can't open a lesson, watch a video, download a file, or read or write a comment. Later: a banner at the top of that page, "Access ended: resubscribe to this course here" with a link (`docs/TODO.md`, "Resubscribe link on an ended course"). A course with no end date (lifetime access) never ends, so nothing is per-course beyond `course_access.ends_at`. Staff acting as themselves are unaffected.
- **Per-course access settings on `courses`:**
  - `access_duration` (an interval, null = lifetime). The Ready Writer: 1 year.
  - Whether the course includes membership. This lives on the linked `programs` row (`includes_membership`, which Big Sisters and member status already read), not duplicated on `courses`.
  - **Includes membership** means an active `course_access` counts as active membership in member status (`reprocess_members_atomic`) for the length of the access, the way program enrollment does today.
- `courses.program_id` links a course to its `programs` row. The Ready Writer links to (or replaces) its existing row; the migration upserts that link by program slug, so it survives re-runs.
- `courses.kind` (`evergreen` | `cohort`), so cohort courses can later use `member_program_enrollments` without a schema change. Only `evergreen` is built now.

### Local (ours, never reprocessed)

- `course_access_grants`: comps and staff access. Admin-written and audited.
- `videos`: one row per Mux asset.
  - Fields: `mux_asset_id`, `mux_playback_id` (signed policy), `status`, `duration`, `quality` (Mux tier), `source_kind` (`kajabi` | `upload`), `source_key` (the Kajabi media ID), `source_fingerprint` and `source_sha256`. A row is unique per `(source_kind, source_key)`; the hash lets two media IDs share one Mux asset.
  - This is how re-runs skip videos already uploaded (see "Telling whether a video changed"). Lessons point at it with `course_lessons.video_id`.
  - Local rather than Silver because uploading is an external side effect we must not repeat.
- **Captions and other per-video assets.** Captions are per video, not a Mux account setting:
  - `videos.has_burned_in_captions` (boolean): the picture itself contains captions, as the existing videos do. For those, track files default to off so viewers don't see them twice. (Named for what it means, since "includes captions" could be read as "has caption tracks".) New videos from Descript are exported **without** burned-in captions, plus a separate subtitle file.
  - `video_text_tracks`: one row per caption, subtitle or transcript file on a video.
    - `kind` separates what the file is for: **`captions`** (with sound cues) and **`subtitles`** (dialogue only) are timed text the player shows, so they're attached to Mux; a **`transcript`** is text for search and the follow-along panel (plain, or timed per line or word) and is never attached to the player. One Descript SRT can serve both: store it as `captions`, and derive the transcript lines from it.
    - Fields: `video_id`, `kind`, `language`, `label`, `source` (`descript` | `whisper` | `mux_auto` | `manual`), `is_default`, the file in the private storage bucket (`.vtt`, kept in the Hub), and `mux_track_id` once attached to the Mux asset.
    - A track is attached to the Mux asset through its API (Mux reads the file from a short-lived link) so the player gets it with the stream. The stored file stays the source of truth: re-ingesting a video re-attaches it.
    - A track from Descript replaces an automatic one in the same language; the automatic one is removed.
  - **Safety net:** new videos are created with Mux's free auto-generated captions, so a video never ships with no captions at all if Ania's file isn't attached yet (her new Ready Writer lessons won't have burned-in captions).
  - Whisper transcripts (video phase 1) go in the same table, with timed lines for search and the follow-along panel.
  - Other per-video assets use the same pattern: thumbnails and posters, the audio-only file (Mux generates it per video), and downloads (`course_lesson_files`).
- `course_lesson_files`: PDFs copied into a private Supabase Storage bucket, keyed by Kajabi file ID and content hash. Served through short-lived signed URLs after an access check.
- `lesson_progress` (`member_id`, `lesson_id`, `completed_at`, `last_position_seconds`). Member-written, admin-readable, **read-only in sudo**.
- `video_watch_segments` (`member_id`, `video_id`, `session_id`, `start_seconds`, `end_seconds`, `playback_rate`, `started_at`). Append-only, one row per continuous stretch. **Not written in sudo**, so admins previewing as a member don't create viewing history. Admin-readable.

RLS:
- Course content is readable by members with a `course_access` row, and by admins.
- Progress and segments are the member's own, plus admins.
- Videos are never public. Playback links are minted server-side only after the access check.
- Schema is in `20261111000100_create_courses.sql`, tested by `supabase/tests/database/courses.test.sql`. Content policies use `has_course_access(course_id)`, which requires *current* access, so a member whose access ended gets their `/courses` listing and outline from the service role after the app's own check, not from RLS.

## Taking a course over from Kajabi

On an imported course's admin page, once it is in the Hub, **Take over from Kajabi** (`TakeOverPanel`, `takeOverCourse` in `src/app/(admin)/admin/courses/take-over-actions.ts`) turns it into a course the Hub owns, so the editor works on it. It is **one way**, and the dialog says so before it does anything; it also shows the dry run (`loadTakeOverPreview`, `src/lib/courses/take-over.ts`): how many modules and lessons, how many have text or a video, how many downloads, and how many items have no status in Kajabi.

- **One database call** (`take_over_kajabi_course`, migration `20270415000000`, run as the admin so the audit log names them). It flips `source` from `kajabi` to `hub` on the course, its modules and its lessons, in one transaction. Ids, `source_key`s, videos, downloads, images, access and members' progress are untouched, so every address and every member's place keeps working. An item with no status in Kajabi (or one the editor doesn't use) becomes a **draft**, which is what members already saw (no setting is hidden). Then the states are worked out again by the usual rule (nothing should move).
- **Refused, writing nothing:** a course that is already the Hub's or isn't there; and one whose modules nest deeper than the editor does (a module has submodules, a submodule has lessons only; `too_deep`): flatten it in Kajabi, import, and try again.
- **The Kajabi import skips it from then on**, without a flag: every import step (structure, images, files, videos, progress) looks only at `source = 'kajabi'` rows. The course keeps its Kajabi key in `source_key`, which is how the draft-maker (`ensureDraftCourses`) knows not to make it again if its address changes, and how the admin course list leaves out the Kajabi copy (`loadAdminCourses`). **Members' progress in Kajabi is no longer imported for it** (`docs/TODO.md`).
- **Audit: one entry**, for the course (`{"source": kajabi -> hub}`). The update triggers on modules and lessons now watch only rows that were already Hub-owned, so the flip doesn't write one entry per row (`Record the intent, not the noise`, `docs/architecture/ACTIVITY_AND_AUDIT_LOG.md`).

## Duplicating a course

On a course's admin page (imported or Hub-owned), **Duplicate course** (`DuplicatePanel`, `duplicateCourse` in `src/app/(admin)/admin/courses/duplicate-actions.ts`) makes a new course from it, so the editor can be tried on a copy while the real course stays linked to Kajabi and keeps syncing. The dialog says what is and isn't copied first (the numbers come from `loadDuplicatePreview`, `src/lib/courses/duplicate.ts`), then opens the new course.

- **One database call** (`duplicate_course`, migration `20270710000000`, run as the admin: an admin or the service role, else `not_editable`; an unknown course is `not_found`, modules nested deeper than the editor handles are `too_deep`). It returns the new course's id.
- **The copy is a draft course the Hub owns:** `source = 'hub'`, `source_key` null (the Kajabi import never touches it, and it is not a "Kajabi copy" for the list: `loadAdminCourses` hides a Kajabi course only when a Hub course carries its `source_key`), `is_draft = true` (hidden from members, and no access is built for a draft), every module and lesson a draft (`publishing_option = 'draft'`, hidden), a new prefixed id for the course, each module and lesson, a free address ("<slug>-copy", then "-copy-2", ...) and the title "<title> (copy)".
- **Copied:** description, kind, access length and opening day, the cover and banner (same media-library assets), modules, submodules and lessons (also lessons in no module) with their positions, text, thumbnails and posters, the link to the **same video** (`video_id`: no re-upload to Mux, no new `videos` row), the downloads, and the **quizzes** (see below).
- **Not copied:** members' progress, `course_access` and grants, watch segments, comments, the Kajabi link and offers, and **the program**: the copy has none, because publishing it later would otherwise hand it to everyone with the program. An admin links it on purpose.
- **Downloads** (also for `duplicate_course_lesson` and `duplicate_course_module`, migration `20270710000100`, through `copy_course_lesson_files`): each download becomes a `course_lesson_files` row of its own on the new lesson, with a key of its own (`up-<uuid>`, like a download added in the Hub; the original's key is Kajabi's file id or its own upload key and must stay unique) and the **same `storage_path`**: no second object in the `course-files` bucket, so the path keeps the original course and lesson ids. That is safe because the two places that remove an object (`removeLessonFile`, and the Kajabi file step for a file Kajabi dropped) ask `CourseRepository.storagePathShared` first and keep the object while another row uses it. The one thing not covered: if the Kajabi step re-uploads a changed file over the same path (`upsert`), a copy shows the new content too. Deleting a whole lesson or course (rows cascade) leaves the object in the bucket, as it always did.
- **Quizzes** (also for `duplicate_course_lesson` and `duplicate_course_module`, migration `20271126100000`, through `copy_lesson_quiz`): a quiz lesson's copy gets a quiz of its own with the same passing grade and hide-answers setting and every question and option in order, with the right answers marked, under new ids and with no Kajabi keys (so an imported quiz's copy is an ordinary Hub quiz). Rows are copied whole, with only the id, links, keys and timestamps replaced, so a column added to these tables later is carried without editing the function. Attempts, answers and members' progress are never copied. The copy runs as the caller, so the quiz policies decide (an admin, a Hub-owned lesson).
- **Audit:** one entry, for the new course (who, from which course: `copied_from`, and `modules_copied`, `lessons_copied`, `files_copied`, `quizzes_copied`; the activity feed reads "duplicated a course as ..."). The insert triggers of the copied modules and lessons are quiet for that course during the call (they check the `app.audit_skip_course` setting the function sets for its own transaction), so there is no entry per row (the same goes for the copied quizzes: the insert audit of `quizzes` checks `app.audit_skip_quiz_copy`); duplicating one lesson or module audits each new lesson and quiz as usual; later edits are audited as usual.
- **The copy's address is a placeholder** until someone picks one. In the course details, renaming the copy (its "(copy)" gone) offers "Use <address>" for the name's address (a suggestion, never a silent overwrite; see "Course addresses" below). The placeholder skips an address an old redirect holds.
- **Usage:** the action records `course_duplicated` (`src/lib/analytics/actions.ts`); the buttons carry `data-track` names.

## Deleting a course

On a course's admin page, **Delete course** in the ⋯ menu (`DeletePanel`, `deleteCourse` in `src/app/(admin)/admin/courses/delete-actions.ts`, behind `courses.manage` like duplicate; a distinct `courses.delete` verb was not added because no role holds `courses.manage` in part: it is the one bundle for the whole area) removes a draft course nobody has access to, mainly the draft copy a duplicate leaves behind. The item is always listed; when it is off, the reason shows beside it (`deleteBlockedReason`, `src/lib/courses/blocked-reasons.ts`, numbers from `loadDeletePreview`, `src/lib/courses/delete.ts`). The dialog is the lifecycle registry's delete confirmation (`lifecycleConfirm("delete", ...)`): it names the course, says how many modules and lessons go with it, lists exactly what else is lost (lesson progress records, comments, and chat messages that lose their video link: `deleteLosses`), and says it can't be undone.

- **One database call** (`delete_course`, migrations `20271123000000` and `20271123100000`, run as the admin so the audit log names them; an admin or the service role, else `not_editable`). It checks and deletes in one transaction. Errors: `not_found`, `not_deletable`, `published`, `has_members`.
- **Allowed only when:** the Hub owns it (`source = 'hub'`: a Kajabi course would be brought back by the next import, so it is removed in Kajabi), it is a draft (`is_draft`; a published one is unpublished first), and no `course_access` or `course_access_grants` row exists for it. Anything else is refused and nothing changes; the hint and the server error say how many have access and how many have a grant. Lesson progress, comments and chat video links do **not** block: a draft nobody has access to holds only stray activity from staff trying it out (the first version refused it, which left a copy nobody could remove).
- **Goes with it** (existing `ON DELETE CASCADE` keys, no new table): modules and submodules, lessons, the lesson download rows, the course's old addresses (`course_address_redirects`), comment follows and reads, and what was left on its lessons: `lesson_progress`, `lesson_comments` (with replies and reports) and the `chat_message_videos` link of a chat message that shared a lesson's video. All of these are cascades; the chat message itself, with its text, is kept and only loses its video link. **Stays:** the stored download files (a copy may point at the same object), the videos (`videos`) and the media-library images, which other courses may share.
- **Audit:** one entry, the course's delete (who, and what it was), with `modules_deleted`, `lessons_deleted`, `progress_deleted`, `comments_deleted` and `chat_video_links_deleted` (the activity feed reads "deleted a course ..."). The delete triggers of the modules and lessons are quiet for that course during the call (the `app.audit_skip_course` setting, as for a duplicate), so there is no entry per row; deleting one lesson or module on its own is audited as usual.
- **Usage:** the action records `course_deleted` (`src/lib/analytics/actions.ts`); the menu item carries `data-track="admin-course-delete-open"`.

## Course addresses: suggestions and redirects

A Hub course's address (`courses.slug`, members open it at `/courses/<slug>`) can change without breaking links people already have. Migration `20270712000000`; a Kajabi course's address is Kajabi's, rebuilt by the import, and is out of scope.

- **Suggestion.** In `CourseDetailsForm`, when the name changes and the address was "following" the name (it is what the name at load, or at the last accepted suggestion, would have given, or it is a duplicate's `-copy` / `-copy-N` placeholder and the name no longer says copy), a one-click "Use <new address>" appears under the field (`suggestAddress`, `src/lib/courses/address-suggestion.ts`). An address chosen by hand is left alone; a new, unsaved course keeps the existing behavior (empty address = derived from the name on save).
- **Ever published.** `courses.first_published_at` is stamped by a trigger the first time `is_draft` is false (on insert or update, however it is published: the editor, the Kajabi publish path, a script) and never cleared, so unpublishing keeps it. The migration backfilled it for courses published when it ran (from `created_at`, or the first `is_draft` true to false entry in the audit log, so for them it is approximate). Production at the time: 8 courses, all imported from Kajabi, 1 published and 7 drafts, no audit entries; so one row was backfilled and no Hub course existed yet. `launch_at` is a different thing (the opening day) and is not used for this.
- **Redirects.** `course_address_redirects` (`id` prefix `cslug_`, never shown in a URL: Tier 2) has one row per old address, pointing at the **course by id** (so a second rename needs no update and there are no chains: every earlier address resolves to the newest). The same trigger on `courses` (`courses_address_and_publication`) that sees the slug change writes the row, in the same statement and transaction as the rename, for a Hub course that is published or ever was; a never-published course changes its address freely with no row. Renaming back to an address the course used before deletes that row (it is canonical again); deleting the course cascades. Admins write nothing here directly (select only for signed-in users; the trigger is `SECURITY DEFINER`, and the rename itself is the admin's own audited update).
- **One address space.** No course's slug may equal another course's redirect, and no redirect may equal another course's slug: enforced in the database (a unique address, plus triggers on both tables, serialized by an advisory lock), error 23505, which the editor shows as "Another course uses that address now or used to." `duplicate_course` skips held addresses too.
- **Audit.** The rename is the one entry the course's update already writes (`slug` old and new); the redirect rows are not audited, so there is no per-row noise.
- **Serving.** `/courses/<old>`, `/courses/<old>/modules/<id>` and `/courses/<old>/lessons/<id>` answer a permanent redirect (308) to the same page at the current address, keeping the rest of the path and the query (`redirectOldCourseAddress`, `src/lib/courses/address-redirect.ts`; read from the proxy's `x-hub-path` header, `currentPath`); the lesson download route does the same with `NextResponse.redirect(..., 308)`. The signed-out previews (`/course-preview/<slug>`, `/lesson-preview/<slug>/<lesson>`, reached by the proxy's rewrite) resolve an old address to the course and redirect to the member address **only when the preview would have been shown** (a public course); otherwise the login page, as for the current address. Access rules apply after the redirect, since the redirect lands on the canonical page; and a redirect is only given when the canonical page would be visible to the caller (a draft's current address is not given to a member). Admin addresses with a slug (`resolveCourseParam`) resolve old addresses as well.
- **Canonical URL.** The member pages are behind sign-in and no page in the app emits a canonical link, so none was added; the preview pages' `og:url` uses the current address.
- **Not covered:** an address a Kajabi import changes (see `docs/TODO.md`, "Course address redirects: loose ends"). A signed-out visitor to an old address of a public course takes two hops: the proxy's rewrite to the preview, then its 308 to the member address, where the proxy rewrites again.
- **Usage:** nothing new to track: the form's button carries `data-track="course-address-use-suggestion"`; saves are recorded by `course_details_saved`.

## Migration pipeline

The four steps below are each safe to re-run and only do new work. **`pnpm courses:migrate` runs all of them for one course** (see "One command"); each step can still be run alone (A and C also run with the standard Kajabi sync and from the admin Courses page, B as `pnpm courses:content`, D from the admin course page's Videos and Downloads panels).

| Step | Runs where | What it does | Idempotency |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Structure** (`/api/import/kajabi-courses`) | Server, triggered by the migrate command | API → Bronze courses, modules, lessons, offers | Upsert on Kajabi ID; soft-delete what a *complete* fetch no longer returns |
| **B. Content** (`scripts/kajabi-course-content.ts`) | Local, with the admin's Kajabi session | Lesson HTML, file list, video source → Bronze | Skips lessons whose content hash is unchanged |
| **C. Process** (`/api/process/courses`) | Server | Bronze → Silver courses/modules/lessons/access | Upsert on source key, delete orphans; IDs stay stable |
| **D. Media** (`scripts/course-media.ts` or a route) | Local or server | Videos → Mux (from URL if allowed, else download and upload); PDFs → Storage | Skips a video whose source fingerprint is unchanged; re-uploads when it changed (see "Telling whether a video changed") |

**Built (steps A and C):**
- **A, `/api/import/kajabi-courses`.** Body `{ "courseIds": [...] }` limits the run, otherwise every course on the site is imported. It fetches everything before writing, so a failed Kajabi call writes and soft-deletes nothing. It never lists step B's columns, so a re-import leaves lesson text and video sources alone. The result reports `unplacedLessons` per course: the OpenAPI spec doesn't say how included lessons point at their module, so parents are resolved from either side (a lesson's `module`/`category`, a module's `lessons`) and a count above zero means the live response differs from what's assumed. **Not yet checked against the live API** (the local credentials couldn't get a token): run it once and look at that number.
- **C, `/api/process/courses`.** Body `{ "course": "<slug>", "accessDuration": "1 year" }`. A new course takes the slug of its Kajabi title (`the-ready-writer`) and keeps it if the title changes; `accessDuration` is stored on the course when given (`null` = lifetime) and left alone when omitted; omit `course` to refresh every Kajabi course already in Silver. The program is linked on creation when a `programs` row has the same slug. Lesson text goes through an allowlist sanitizer (`sanitize-html`, `src/lib/courses/sanitize.ts`). `course_access` is upserted and its stale rows deleted, so members never see a gap mid-run.
- **Part of the standard sync.** The admin "Sync from Kajabi API" button (and the nightly member reconciliation) runs step A for every course and then step C for the courses already published, after the member import, so `course_access` is rebuilt from fresh purchases (`src/lib/courses/sync.ts`). A courses problem is reported in the result and never fails the member sync. A new course is never published by itself: the result lists it under "Not published in the Hub yet" with a required access length and a Publish button, which calls step C.
- **Pre-orders: access starts at launch.** `courses.launch_at` (set when publishing or from the admin Courses page; the route takes `launchOn` as a day in the organisation's timezone) moves anyone who bought earlier to start at launch, and their access length runs from then (`starts_at` is the later of purchase and launch). Until then the member's `/courses` shows "Opens <date>" and the course page says when it opens, with none of the course visible (access state `upcoming`, which can't open lessons).
- **Staff preview of "Coming soon" lessons.** Staff acting as themselves (not in sudo) can open lessons members see as "Coming soon", from the outline or from the lesson's "Preview" link on the admin course page; a banner says so, progress isn't tracked, and previous/next step through unreleased lessons too. **Drafts open the same way**, with a banner that says "Draft" instead of "Coming soon" and "Draft" badges in the outline: they are stored as `hidden` (a lesson under a draft module is hidden too) and only staff acting as themselves are given them, so they need a sync (step C) to appear. A lesson that is drafted again after being published stays as the same row (same id, members' progress kept) instead of being deleted. Drafts never count as available lessons. Members read only *open* lessons through row-level security (migration `20261112000300`): before it, a member could read a coming-soon lesson's text and video link through the API even though the app never showed it.
- **D, media: lesson downloads** (`src/lib/courses/files.ts`, `POST /api/process/course-files`, the "Downloads" panel on the admin course page, migration `20261112000500` for the private `course-files` bucket). For the files of every lesson, **drafts' included** (marked "Draft" in the panel; files cost nothing to keep and staff preview those lessons), in a *published* course, "Copy N files" fetches each file from the presigned link the content step recorded and stores it as `course-files/<course>/<lesson>/<kajabi file id>-<name>`, with a `course_lesson_files` row (filename, type, size, SHA-256, position). It costs nothing, so there's no confirm step. A file already copied is never copied again, a file Kajabi no longer lists (or whose lesson is gone) is removed from storage and the table, a file over 50 MB is reported, and an expired or missing link is reported ("run the Kajabi content step again"). Members never touch the bucket: the lesson page's Downloads card (beside the video on a wide screen, below the text on a phone) links to `/courses/<course>/lessons/<lesson>/files/<file>`, which applies the lesson page's access rule (staff also preview coming-soon lessons) and redirects to a signed link that expires in a minute and saves the file under its own name; anything else is a plain 404. **A draft's files stay staff-only** with no extra rule: a draft is a hidden Hub lesson, members' row-level security reads only files of *open* lessons, and the route refuses anyone who can't open the lesson (members get "Coming soon"/404, staff acting as themselves get the preview). **Not done:** noticing that a file kept its Kajabi id but changed its content (a replaced file is only picked up if Kajabi gives it a new file id).
- **D, media: images** (`src/lib/courses/images.ts`, same button and route as the downloads). Kajabi's course cover (`courses.thumbnail_url`), module posters (`course_modules.poster_url`) and lesson thumbnails (`course_lessons.thumbnail_url`) were hotlinked; the step stores each as a shared **media library** image (`docs/product/MEDIA_LIBRARY.md`; tags `kajabi` and the course slug, the Kajabi link as its source) and records it as `thumbnail_asset_id` / `poster_asset_id` on the row, plus `*_source` (the Kajabi URL without its query string). A re-run skips an image whose source is unchanged (links are signed, so the query string is ignored), stores a new image when it changed (removing the old one from the library once nothing shows it), clears the image Kajabi dropped, and removes this course's Kajabi images that no row shows. A lesson and its module that use the same link share one library image, fetched once. Silver reprocessing never writes the asset columns, so images survive it. Members never touch the bucket: pages show `/api/courses/image/<kind>/<id>`, which needs a courses user and keeps a draft's image for staff acting as themselves; until the copy has run, the pages fall back to the Kajabi URL (`courseImageSrc`). The first version copied into a private `course-images` bucket (`*_path` columns); those images were moved into the library on 2026-10-30 and the columns dropped (migration `20270305000000`); the empty bucket is deleted through the Storage API or the dashboard, since SQL can't. **The course's "hero" banner** (behind its title in Kajabi; not the thumbnail, and not in the public API or the admin product page) has a pipeline but no source yet: `bronze.kajabi_courses.hero_image_url` (migration `20270221000000`; the API import never writes it) is copied by processing to `courses.hero_url`, then stored like the cover (`hero_asset_id`/`hero_source`, served as `/api/courses/image/hero/COURSE_ID`), and the course page draws it behind the title under a dark overlay (a course without one, like The Ready Writer, keeps the plain band). Where it would come from is in the TODO "Course Hero Image".
- **D, media: videos to Mux (drafts)**: a video used only by Kajabi drafts is planned too, marked `draft`, and listed in its own "Draft lessons' videos" section of the Videos panel. The normal upload never sends it (every upload is billed, and some drafts are leftovers); the draft list has a checkbox per video (none ticked to start, "Select all" and "Clear" above it; a video that can't be uploaded, say its link expired, can't be ticked), and a separate "Upload N selected draft videos" button (`action: "upload_drafts"` with `mediaIds` on `/api/process/course-media`, with its own confirmation naming the ticked videos' minutes) uploads exactly those, so staff can preview just the lessons they care about. `mediaIds` is also accepted by the normal `upload`; an empty or malformed list is a 400, never read as "all", and ids the action doesn't cover come back as `notAvailable`. A draft's downloads are copied with the rest (see above). The Kajabi content step (lesson text and video links, run with a Kajabi session) has to include drafts for their text and videos to arrive.
- **D, media: videos to Mux** (`src/lib/courses/media.ts`, `POST /api/process/course-media`, the "Videos" panel on the admin course page). An explicit button, not part of the automatic sync, because every upload is billed: it lists the course's videos, says how many minutes it's about to upload, and asks first. Only videos of lessons members can see (open or coming soon) are uploaded, never drafts. For each, Mux fetches the presigned download link the content step recorded (no local download), the quality is `plus`, the `videos` row records the Kajabi media id, the source fingerprint (size, ETag, Last-Modified from a one-byte ranged GET) and `has_burned_in_captions`, and the Mux asset id is saved straight away. A video already in Mux is never uploaded again; an errored one is retried into the same row; a lesson whose link has expired is reported ("run the Kajabi content step again") instead of failing. "Update status" reads Mux directly, for when the webhook hasn't delivered. Afterwards step C links each lesson to its video. **Not done:** reusing one Mux asset for the same file under two media ids (that needs the file's SHA-256, and Mux fetches the link itself so we never hold the bytes), copying lesson PDFs to Storage, and uploading new videos of an already-published course automatically.
- **Completing a lesson:** the player completes the lesson itself once the playhead passes 90% *while playing* (jumping a paused playhead to the end doesn't count), the button changes from a white, outlined "Mark as complete" to a filled "Complete" in the Hub's color, and a "Great job! Keep going!" card previews the next lesson (thumbnail, title, link). Pressing "Complete" clears it, and once a member has pressed the button themselves, watching on never completes it behind their back. It's one shared state (`LessonCompletion.tsx`), read-only in sudo and in a staff preview. This is a playhead threshold, not the watch-segment tracking planned later, so a lesson can be completed by jumping ahead while it plays; the segments will make "watched" exact. The section list also has a "Next Module" button (to the next module's first open lesson) when there is a next module.
- **Lesson page layout** (modelled on Kajabi's): a breadcrumb (course / module / week / lesson), then the video and this section's lessons as **one component** (a dark panel flush against the player, the same height, scrolling if long; hidden on a phone), then Back / Mark complete / Next under the video, then the title and text, with the Downloads card beside the text (a quiz lesson is the exception: its title and text come first, then the questions in place of Mark complete, so the page opens with what it is about). The section list shows each lesson's number (a check when done, a marker for the current one), an aligned thumbnail and a wide title, dimmed and unlinked when unreleased and with no "Coming soon" label. The staff preview notice is a plum strip under the header, the same strip as the notification banners (`src/components/PageBanner.tsx`) but not a stored notification, since it describes the page, not an event; Mark complete stays visible but disabled in a preview.
- **Admin Courses page** (`/admin/courses`): every Kajabi course with its Hub state, access length, launch date, lesson counts and who has access (current / upcoming / ended). A course's page explains each module and lesson (open, coming soon or hidden, and which Kajabi setting or module above it decides that, from the same rule members get), shows whether the content step has fetched its text and video, and has the access length and launch date form for a published course.
- **Both routes write with the service role** after checking the caller is an admin: signed-in admins can read the Bronze and Silver course tables but not write them.
- **Not built yet:** "includes membership" counting as active membership in `reprocess_members_atomic`.

Tests, per `.claude/rules/testing-requirements.md`:
- Bronze idempotency tests in `tests/api/idempotency/`: first import, re-import with no duplicates, a changed lesson, a removed lesson.
- Silver reprocessability tests in `tests/api/reprocessability/`: create, a deleted source removes the lesson, a changed source updates it, and IDs stay stable.
- Media tests with Mux mocked: a re-run uploads nothing new, and a changed source re-uploads.

### One command

`pnpm courses:migrate --course <kajabi-course-id> [--env .env.prod] [--base-url <url>] [--yes] [--access-duration "1 year"|lifetime] [--launch-on YYYY-MM-DD] [--skip-drafts] [--skip-progress] [--skip-comments]` (`scripts/courses-migrate.ts`; the logic is `src/lib/courses/migrate.ts`, argument parsing `migrate-args.ts`). It runs, in order, and stops at the first failure with what to do next:

1. **A.** `POST /api/import/kajabi-courses` for the one course; prints counts. Any `unplacedLessons` stops the run here (the importer's assumption about the live response is wrong).
2. **B.** Spawns `scripts/kajabi-course-content.ts` (Chrome and the saved Kajabi login, output on your terminal), **a dry run first**, then asks "Write ...? [y/N]" and runs it again with `--apply`. A dry run that finds nothing new skips the write: no lesson text, no video link and no new or changed quiz. B also captures Kajabi quizzes, and the summary counts them (new, changed, unchanged).
3. **C.** `POST /api/process/courses` by Kajabi id. A course that is not published yet asks for its access length and launch day (the flags answer both), then asks to publish; with `--yes` and no flags it refuses instead of guessing, and nothing is published.
4. **P1, P2, K and K2, optional** (after C, because progress needs the Hub's lessons and `course_access` rows; `--skip-progress` leaves out P1 and P2, `--skip-comments` leaves out K and K2):
   - **P1, progress capture.** `scripts/kajabi-course-content.ts --progress` run the way B is: a dry run with a summary, then "Write ...? [y/N]" (`--yes` skips it), then `--apply`; a dry run that finds no new roster or lesson rows skips the write. It writes only Bronze (`bronze.kajabi_product_progress`, `bronze.kajabi_lesson_progress`). A member who arrives part-way through a course is the reason it exists.
   - **P2, progress into the Hub.** `POST /api/process/course-progress` with `{course, dryRun: true}`, prints the counts (would add, already completed, left alone, students matched / kept no member yet / ambiguous / removed, lessons the Hub doesn't have), then, if anything would be added, asks "Add N completed lessons to the Hub's progress? [y/N]" (`--yes` skips it; a no skips only P2, exit code 0, and the summary says it was declined), then the real call. **Insert-only**: it adds a completed row only where the member has none and never changes or removes a row that exists (Hub progress is member-written), so it is safe to repeat and a second run adds nothing. Skipped when P1 failed.
   - **K, comments capture.** `--comments` run the same way (dry run, summary, ask, apply, skip when nothing is new). It fills `bronze.kajabi_lesson_comments` only. The capture is unverified against the live Kajabi site; its output is the way to check it (see "Reading the capture's summary" under "Lesson comments").
   - **K2, comments into the Hub.** `POST /api/process/course-comments` with `{course, dryRun: true}`, prints the counts, asks (`--yes` skips the question), then the real call if anything would be added or updated. The dry run also prints a read-only completeness line: how many of the course's lessons have captured comments. Keyed on Kajabi's ids, so it is safe to repeat; it notifies no one. Skipped with `--skip-comments` and when K failed.
   - Declining a question skips only that capture (the run goes on, exit code 0). **A failure in P1, P2 or K is listed in the summary, the run still goes on to D (a comments capture that was never verified must not block videos and files), and the exit code is 1.** Both captures are idempotent on a re-run: unchanged rows are compared field by field (the capture time is not part of the comparison), so an identical snapshot writes nothing; a comment whose page gave no id is keyed by a hash of its text, so an edited one shows as one removed and one written.
5. **D.** Downloads and images (`/api/process/course-files`, free), then the videos: `course-media` with `action: "plan"` (read-only, no Mux needed) says "N videos, M minutes" and **pauses to ask before uploading, because Mux bills every upload** (`--yes` skips it). It then repeats `upload` until nothing is left or a round makes no progress (each call is capped at 300 seconds), and finishes with "Update status". Videos only draft lessons use are never uploaded here.

It ends with one summary (lessons captured, quiz counts, progress and comments, files and images copied, videos uploaded or pending, anything failed). Every step is idempotent, so a re-run only does what is missing and a run with nothing new is a no-op (the launch-day check). Exit code 1 for a failed step or item; declining a question is not a failure.

**Where it points.** `--env` names the env file for both the Supabase keys step B writes with and the secret the routes check (`CRON_SECRET`, else `SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY`; never printed). The routes are `--base-url`, else the deployed app for `.env.prod` (`appConfig.appUrl`) and the local dev server (`pnpm dev`) for anything else. A non-local target asks "This writes to <url>. Continue?" first. A new step (the quiz capture) is one more object in `buildSteps()`.

### Step B in practice

`npm run courses:content -- --course <kajabi-course-id> [--apply] [--env .env.prod]` (or `--lesson <id>`). Logic is in `src/lib/kajabi/lesson-content.ts`; the browser part is `scripts/kajabi-course-content.ts`.

- **It needs a login, once.** It opens Chrome with its own profile (`~/.hub-kajabi-profile`), waits while you log in to Kajabi, and reuses that login on later runs. It never sees a password or token. It records what each lesson's editor loads (see the spike result under "What Kajabi gives us"). **It reaches each lesson through the app's own router, not by loading the lesson's URL:** a direct load of `/admin/posts/{id}/edit` never makes the `/api/admin/posts/{id}` request (it only fetches `/downloads`), so each worker loads the course's admin page once and then does `history.pushState` + `popstate` to each lesson. After the first login, `--headless` works with the saved login.
- **The lesson arrives one of two ways, and the capture accepts either** (changed 2026-11, found when every lesson of a run timed out): Kajabi's admin used to answer that navigation with the `/api/admin/posts/{id}` call, and now (it navigates with Turbo) it fetches the lesson's *page* as HTML instead, with the same document embedded in a `<script type="application/json" id="kjb-redux-store-data">` tag (`parseEmbeddedPost`). The `/downloads` call is unchanged. The symptom of the old capture against the new admin was `page.waitForResponse: Timeout 60000ms` on every lesson, with the login fine. If it ever happens again, the quickest diagnosis is to list the requests the page makes after the navigation (`--probe`, or a throwaway Playwright script) and see where the lesson data went. `--progress` and `--comments` read other pages and were not checked against this change.
- **A quiz lesson is skipped, not failed.** A lesson that is a Kajabi quiz or assessment has an editor page that redirects (302) to the quiz editor (`/admin/quizzes/{id}/edit`), which holds questions, not lesson text, video or downloads. The capture recognises that redirect (`isQuizRedirect`), reports the lesson as `quiz` right away (no 60-second wait, no retry), counts it as "quiz" in the summary (not as a failure, so the exit code stays 0) and leaves whatever Bronze already holds for it. Quizzes are not migrated (see "Not planned"). Before the November 2026 change to the page-embedded data, the old API call still answered for a quiz lesson, so one that had been captured then keeps its old content in Bronze.
- **Dry run by default.** Without `--apply` it captures and reports (`updated`, `unchanged`, `urls_refreshed`, `missing`, `failed`) and writes nothing. Exit code 1 if any lesson failed.
- **Only fills in what step A imported.** It updates the content columns of existing Bronze lessons (`body_html`, `thumbnail_url`, `video_media_id`, `video_source`, `content_hash`, `content_fetched_at`) and never creates one; a lesson Bronze doesn't have comes back as `missing`. A step A re-run doesn't touch those columns. Draft lessons are captured too, since staff preview them (`state = 'hidden'` in Silver); `--skip-drafts` leaves them out. (`--include-drafts`, the old opt-in, is still accepted and does nothing.)
- **`content_hash`** covers the text, thumbnail (without its query string), video media/Wistia IDs and the file list, not the expiring links, so reloading an unchanged lesson changes nothing.
- **Downloads** go to `bronze.kajabi_lesson_files`: upserted on Kajabi's file ID (restoring one that had been soft-deleted), and any the editor no longer lists are soft-deleted.
- **Expiring links are stored, for step D.** `video_source.download_url` and each file's `source_url` are presigned S3 links valid for 7 days (`download_url_expires_at`). Bronze is admin-readable, so those links are readable by admins for that week. A lesson whose links are within 36 hours of expiry is refreshed on the next run even if nothing else changed; step D should run soon after B, or B re-run first.

**Launch rehearsal:** run the full migration against production at least twice before launch day, the second time after Ania's final edits. The launch-day run should then be a no-op except for her last changes.

### Telling whether a video changed

Re-hashing every file on every run would mean downloading hundreds of GB each time, so it's layered, cheapest first:

1. **Fingerprint, every run, no download:** Kajabi's media ID and duration (from the API), plus the download link's total size, `ETag` and `Last-Modified` (a one-byte ranged `GET`; the link is signed for `GET`, so a `HEAD` is rejected). Stored on `videos` as `source_key` and `source_fingerprint`. Replacing a lesson's video in Kajabi produces a new media ID, which this catches (confirmed in the day-1 spike).
2. **SHA-256, only when we download:** computed while streaming the file to Mux and stored as `videos.source_sha256`. If a new media ID has the same hash as an existing row (a lesson duplicated between the 180 and The Ready Writer, say), **reuse that Mux asset** instead of uploading and paying to store it again.
3. **When it did change:** upload a new Mux asset, repoint the lesson once it's `ready` (members never see a gap), and retire the old asset once nothing references it.

## Quizzes

A lesson of a Hub-owned course can be a quiz, so Ania can stop writing them in Kajabi. Kajabi's quiz (observed read-only in the admin) is a lesson with a title, a description and a thumbnail, a Draft or Published state, and the tabs Questions, Settings and Results: ordered questions, each with response options and a correct answer (auto-graded); an optional passing grade; "hide answers on the results page"; and a list of submissions. The Hub builds multiple choice with one answer, multiple choice with several answers, an optional passing grade that gates progress, hidden answers and a results list. Kajabi's other two question types, **short answer** (free text) and **file upload** (one file), and its "Graded question" toggle, are built as questions that are **recorded and never scored** (see "Questions that are not graded"). The completion emails are not built (`docs/TODO.md`, "Quizzes: results export and completion emails").

**Status:** the data layer, the scoring rules, the builder, the member experience, questions that are not graded, and the Kajabi import of quizzes and of members' past responses are built (migrations `20271124000000`, `20271124400000`, `20271125100001` and `20271126100002`). The Kajabi readings of the quiz editor and of the Results tab are **not yet verified against the live admin** (see "Importing quizzes from Kajabi" and "Importing past quiz responses").

**How a lesson becomes a quiz.** There is no lesson "type" column: a lesson is a quiz lesson when a `quizzes` row points at it (`quizzes.lesson_id`, unique, so one quiz per lesson, removed with the lesson). The lesson keeps everything else (title, text, thumbnail, draft or open, place in the outline, progress, comments), so a quiz rides on every lesson feature, including the status menu and the locks (see below), without a second kind of outline item. The cost is that code asking "is this lesson a quiz?" reads a second table; `QuizRepository.quizzesOfLessons` answers it for a whole outline in one query.

**Tables** (all new, Local; `src/lib/data/quizzes.ts`):

- `quizzes` (`quiz_`): `lesson_id`, `passing_percent` (1 to 100, or null for a quiz that is not graded), `hide_answers`, `revision` (counts edits of the questions), `source_key` (Kajabi's quiz id when imported).
- `quiz_questions` (`qques_`): `quiz_id`, `position`, `prompt` (plain text, 1 to 1000 characters), `kind` (`choice`, `short_answer` or `file_upload`), `graded` (whether it counts toward the score; a short answer or a file upload is never graded: a table constraint), `allows_multiple`, `source_key`.
- `quiz_options` (`qopt_`): `question_id`, `position`, `label`, `is_correct`, `source_key`. A single-answer question has exactly one correct option, a multiple-answer one at least one, two to twelve options each: enforced by the save function (`save_quiz_question`), not left to the screen.
- `quiz_attempts` (`qatt_`): `quiz_id`, `member_id`, the score as it was when submitted (`question_count`, `correct_count`, `score_percent`, `passed`) and `submitted_at`. Editing the quiz later never changes an old attempt's score.
- `quiz_attempt_answers` (keyed by attempt and question): the option ids picked and whether the question was right (null for a question that was not scored), the text typed for a short answer (`answer_text`, at most 20,000 characters) and the file uploaded for a file upload (`file_path`, `file_name`, `file_bytes`, all or none, at most 10 MB). Deleting a question removes the answers to it and keeps the attempt.

These ids are Tier 2 and never appear in an address: staff open a quiz through its lesson (`/admin/courses/<crs_ id>/lessons/<les_ id>`) and a member through the lesson page.

**Scoring** (`src/lib/courses/quiz-scoring.ts`, pure, with example and `fast-check` property tests):

- A question is right only when the picked options are exactly the correct ones: **all or nothing, no partial credit**, for single and multiple answer alike. Picking nothing is wrong; a single-answer question with two picked is wrong.
- Option ids that are not the question's are ignored; a repeated id counts once. A question with **no correct option marked is not scored** (it leaves the denominator, so it can never make a quiz impossible to pass).
- The score is the share of scored questions answered right, **rounded down**, and passing is decided on exact integers (`correct * 100 >= passing * questions`), so a score shown as 80 never fails an 80 percent grade.
- **No passing grade means not graded: every submitted attempt passes.**
- **A quiz with nothing to score has no score, and no passing grade can apply to it:** every submitted attempt passes, so submitting completes the lesson (the stored `passing_percent` is ignored, and the builder and the member page don't show it). This replaces the first release's rule that a graded quiz with nothing to score never passed, which could leave a member of a reflection quiz unable to finish it. It needs at least one answer (an empty submission is refused), so a lesson is never completed by clicking submit on a blank form.

**Who may do what** (migration `20271124000000`; the policies read the lesson's `source`, as the rest of the editor does):

- Staff (admins; the permission is `courses.manage`, the same bundle as the rest of the course editor, since no role holds it in part) read everything and write the quiz of a **Hub-owned** lesson with their own client, so the audit log names them. A quiz of an imported lesson is written only by the Kajabi processing run (service role) and becomes editable when the course is taken over, because the policy reads the lesson, which `take_over_kajabi_course` flips to `hub`.
- **Members have no access to questions or options**: `is_correct` must never reach a browser before an attempt is submitted. The server reads them with the service role after its own access check, scores the answers, and records the attempt. A member's client reads only the quiz settings of a lesson they can read, and only their own attempts and answers.
- **A member's own client cannot complete a quiz lesson**: a trigger on `lesson_progress` refuses a user-client write that sets `completed_at` on a lesson that has a quiz. Only the server, after scoring (a pass, or any submission when the quiz is not graded), writes the completion. Saving a position and everything else on `lesson_progress` is unchanged.
- **Audit:** one entry on the quiz for the intent, named by its lesson (a quiz made, its settings or questions changed, a quiz removed). Question and option saves go through `save_quiz_question`, `delete_quiz_question` and `reorder_quiz_questions` (all-or-nothing, running as the admin), which bump `quizzes.revision`; questions and options write no entry of their own, and question text is never in the log. Writes by the service role (the Kajabi run) write none.

**The builder** (a Hub-owned course; `src/app/(admin)/admin/courses/[id]/lessons/[lessonId]/`, actions in `src/app/(admin)/admin/courses/quiz-actions.ts`, rules in `src/lib/courses/quiz-editor.ts`):

- **Add quiz** is in a module's Add content menu (and the row ⋯ menu), next to Add lesson: it makes a draft lesson in that module and its quiz in one action (if the quiz can't be made the new lesson is taken back). A quiz lesson shows the quiz icon and "Quiz" in the outline instead of the text and video line, and "Edit quiz" opens its pages.
- A quiz lesson's pages are tabs as paths, with the course's lesson address as the base: **Details** (`/admin/courses/<crs_ id>/lessons/<les_ id>`: title, module, description, status and thumbnail, as for any lesson, without media and downloads), **Questions** (`/questions`), **Settings** (`/settings`) and **Results** (`/results`). The quiz is always addressed by its lesson; the actions take the lesson id and look the quiz up, checking it belongs to the course.
- **Questions:** each question is saved whole (text, "members can choose more than one answer", two to twelve answers with the right ones marked: one for a single-answer question, any number for a multiple-answer one). Answers keep their ids when a question is edited, so past attempts still point at them. Reorder with the "Move to position" menu (the same as the writing lists), delete with the lifecycle registry's confirmation (it says the answers members gave go with it and their scores stay). Blank answer rows are dropped when saving. The form says what is missing in plain words before anything is sent; the database function is the last word.
- **Settings:** "Set a passing grade" (a whole percent from 1 to 100; off means not graded), "Hide answers on the results page", and **Remove quiz** (the lesson stays as a plain one; its questions and every attempt are deleted, and the dialog says how many attempts).
- **Results:** every attempt, newest first, with the member's display name linked to their record, the date, the score ("100% (2 of 2)") and Passed or Not passed (or "Completed the lesson" when the quiz is not graded). It is read in one batch of attempts and one lookup of names.
- **Question types:** a new question starts as multiple choice or checkboxes ("Graded question" ticked, which is the first release's behavior), or is a **Short answer** or a **File upload** (no answers to fill in). The kind of a saved question can't change (delete it and add another): its recorded answers would mean something else. A choice question can be left ungraded (untick "Graded question"): it then has no right answer to mark, its picks are recorded and never scored. The kind and grading go through `save_quiz_question_of_kind`, which bumps the quiz's revision like every question save (the audit entry).
- **Results of a quiz with answers in words:** an attempt with nothing scored shows "Not scored" and "Completed the lesson"; each row links to **View answers** (`/results/<qatt_ id>`), a page with the member's name, the time, and for every question what they picked, wrote and uploaded (a file as a link through the checking route below). It is read-only and writes nothing; it is behind `courses.manage` like the rest. Deleting a question or removing a quiz also deletes the files members uploaded to it from storage.
- **Imported quizzes are read only** (a banner says to take the course over). **Duplicating** a lesson, module or course that holds a quiz copies the quiz (see "Duplicating a course"). Deleting a lesson with members' progress is refused as for any lesson (a passed quiz leaves progress).
- **Usage:** the actions record `course_quiz_added`, `course_quiz_question_saved`, `course_quiz_question_deleted`, `course_quiz_questions_reordered`, `course_quiz_settings_saved` and `course_quiz_removed` (`src/lib/analytics/actions.ts`). Removing a quiz is its own wording, not a lifecycle registry action (it isn't the lesson's delete); deleting a question uses the registry's delete.

**The member experience** (the lesson page, `src/app/(member)/courses/[slug]/lessons/[lessonId]/page.tsx`; `src/components/courses/QuizTaker.tsx`, `src/lib/courses/quiz-member.ts`, `src/app/(member)/courses/quiz-actions.ts`):

- A quiz lesson shows its questions in the card under the lesson title, **in place of the Mark complete button** (a lesson that is a quiz is completed by passing, not by a click; `setLessonComplete` refuses it, and so does the database). Each question is "Choose one" (radio buttons) or "Choose all that apply" (checkboxes), in native controls so the keyboard works as everywhere; "N of M answered" is announced politely; unanswered questions may be submitted and count as wrong.
- **The correct answers never reach the browser before a submission.** The page reads the quiz with the service role (members' own clients can't read questions or options) and builds the member's view field by field (`loadMemberQuiz`: prompt, whether several answers are allowed, option ids and labels, nothing else; a test fails if any field named like a right answer appears). The browser sends only the picks; the server (`submitQuiz`) checks the member is signed in, not in sudo, the lesson is open and the course is open to them, parses the picks defensively (bounded sizes, option ids that aren't the question's are ignored), scores (`src/lib/courses/quiz-scoring.ts`), records the attempt and its answers, and completes the lesson on a pass.
- **A pass gates progress.** With a passing grade the lesson is complete only on a pass; with none, any submitted attempt completes it. The completion is written with the service role, once: the first one keeps its date and writes the "Completed <lesson>" activity row; a later pass changes nothing and a later fail never un-completes it. Retaking is always allowed, as often as the member likes. The progress, roster, "continue watching" and course-progress readers all see `lesson_progress.completed_at`, so they work unchanged.
- **After submitting** the member sees their score and what it means ("You scored 67% (2 of 3). You need 80% to pass. You can try again."; a pass says the lesson is complete) and the score takes keyboard focus. Unless the quiz hides answers, each question shows Right or Not quite, what they chose, and the right answers for one they missed. A quiz that hides answers shows only the score (not even which questions were right, so a retake can't be narrowed down) and says so. "Try again" or "Take the quiz again" brings back a clean form with focus on the first question. The last five attempts are listed under the form with dates in the member's own time zone.
- **Questions that are not graded, for the member:** a short answer is a text box (up to 10,000 characters); a file upload takes one document or picture (`config/app.ts` `quizAnswers`: 10 MB, the extensions pdf, doc, docx, odt, rtf, txt, md, pages, png, jpg, jpeg, gif, webp, heic; never a program or script). The page says "Only you and the course team can read what you write or upload here" (true by the rules below). After submitting a quiz with nothing scored there is no score: "Your answers are in and this lesson is complete.", with what they wrote and uploaded shown back; a mixed quiz shows the score of its graded questions and the review of its choice questions. Earlier attempts list "What you wrote and uploaded" (the member's own words and file links).
- **Sudo and previews:** in sudo the quiz is read-only ("Progress can't be changed while viewing as a member", and the server refuses too); staff previewing a lesson members can't open yet see the questions with submit off ("Progress isn't tracked..."). Staff acting as themselves on an open lesson submit as themselves: their attempts count in Results, and the staff tools' "Clear my progress" on the lesson now also deletes their own attempts at the quiz.
- **Usage:** `submitQuiz` records `quiz_attempt_submitted` (`src/lib/analytics/actions.ts`); the buttons carry `data-track` names.
- **Not limited:** nothing caps how often a member retakes a quiz. The browser can't learn the right answers without a submission, but a member can submit repeatedly and read them from the review of a quiz that shows answers; the "hide answers" setting is the lever for that (`docs/TODO.md`, "Quizzes: smaller follow-ups").

**Questions that are not graded** (migration `20271125100001`).

- **Mixed quizzes.** The score counts **graded choice questions with a right answer marked, and nothing else**. Short answers, file uploads and ungraded choice questions are recorded with the attempt and left out of the score (`question_count` is the number scored). A quiz with a passing grade is passed on its scored questions alone; what a member wrote or uploaded never decides a pass. A quiz with nothing scored has no score (shown as "Not scored") and every attempt with at least one answer passes.
- **Files.** A member's file is uploaded straight from the browser to the private `quiz-answer-files` bucket (a server action can't take a big body), with a signed address the server made for one path in the member's own folder (`prepareQuizFileUpload`: checks the member, the lesson, that the question takes a file, the name's kind and the size). The path is `<member id>/<random>/<name>`. The submission names the path; the server accepts it only if it is the member's own shape of path, an allowed kind, and **really in storage** (the size comes from storage, not from the browser). The bucket has no storage policy and a 10 MB limit, so only the service role reaches it. **Who can read a file:** the member who made the attempt (even after their access to the course ends: it is their writing) and staff with `courses.manage`, through `/api/course-quiz-answers/<attempt>/<question>`, which redirects to a signed link that lasts one minute and saves the file under its own name (never shown in the browser); anyone else gets a plain 404. **Text and files are read by RLS the same way:** the member reads their own attempts' answers, admins all, nobody writes with a user client.
- **Removal on request.** A member's quiz attempts used to be re-pointed at the former-member placeholder; because they now hold the member's own words and files, `member_data_handling` says **delete** for them and the removal deletes the member's folder of the bucket. The copies the Kajabi capture holds of their past responses are removed too: see "Removal on request" under "Importing past quiz responses".
- **Not covered yet:** files uploaded but never submitted, or whose answers vanish when Kajabi drops a question, stay in the bucket (`docs/TODO.md`, "Quizzes: sweep orphaned answer files"). No AI service touches these answers: nothing in the code sends a member's text or file anywhere but the Hub's own database and storage.

**Importing quizzes from Kajabi** (`src/lib/kajabi/quiz-capture.ts`, `scripts/kajabi-course-content.ts`, `src/lib/courses/quiz-sync.ts`, migration `20271124400000`). Kajabi's quiz is a lesson of the course (it is in the API's lesson list like any other), whose editor page redirects to the quiz editor (`/admin/posts/{lesson id}/edit` to `/admin/quizzes/{quiz id}/edit`; the sample, "Let's Check In" in The Ready Writer, is lesson 2200968704, quiz 1573355, three questions, no passing grade). Bronze then the existing processing into Silver, like the rest of a course:

- **Capture (step B, a logged-in browser, never run by an agent).** `npm run courses:content -- --course <kajabi-course-id> [--apply]` (the run that captures lessons) now follows a quiz lesson's redirect instead of skipping it: it opens the quiz editor through the app's router, keeps the editor page's embedded JSON store and every quiz-looking `/api/admin/` JSON response it makes while the page settles, and writes them as they came to **`bronze.kajabi_quizzes`** (natural key: Kajabi's quiz id; `course_kajabi_id`, `lesson_kajabi_id`, `raw_payload` as `{ documents: [...] }`, a content hash, `captured_at`, `deleted_at`; admin read, service role write: it holds the right answers). A dry run (no `--apply`) reads and reports, writing nothing. An unchanged quiz writes nothing. **Change detection** hashes the quiz as the Hub reads it (`parseKajabiQuiz`: questions, answers, which are right, passing grade, hidden answers), never the raw documents: the editor page's embedded store also holds signed image URLs and other pages' data that differ on every load, which made every run report "changed" (found on the first production run, 2026-11). Bronze still stores the raw documents. The stored hash carries a `q2:` prefix; rows written before it hold the old whole-document hash, so each quiz shows "changed" once on the first run after the fix, is rewritten once, and is unchanged from then on. Step B of `pnpm courses:migrate` also asks to write when only quizzes are new or changed. a lesson that was a quiz and is plain again has its Bronze quiz retired (soft-deleted). It prints counts per quiz (new, changed, unchanged, how many questions the reading found) and never a question.
- **The reading is a pure function of the Bronze documents** (`parseKajabiQuiz`), done at processing time, not at capture time, so fixing it is a reprocess, not a new capture. It is deliberately tolerant: a JSON:API document (`data` and `included`, relationships resolved) or plain nested JSON; the list of questions is found wherever it is (objects with a prompt-like text and two or more answer-like objects, or answers kept apart that name their question); which answers are right comes from a flag on the answer (`correct`, `is_correct`, ...) or an id list on the question, and is **never guessed**: a question with no right answer marked is imported and left unscored (the scoring rules leave it out of the count). Several right answers make a question multiple-answer. Passing grade (a percent, or a fraction, or off) and "hide answers" are read from wherever they are. HTML in a prompt or answer is reduced to plain text. A question the database wouldn't take (fewer than two or more than twelve answers) is left out and counted.
- **Silver (`/api/process/courses`, and so the admin "Sync from Kajabi")** attaches each captured quiz to its lesson through `sync_imported_quiz`, one transaction per quiz, on Kajabi's keys (`quizzes.source_key`, `quiz_questions` and `quiz_options` per `(parent, source_key)`): **idempotent** (running again rewrites no row that did not change and keeps every id, so members' attempts keep pointing at the same questions), and **reprocessable** (a changed capture updates in place; a question or answer Kajabi dropped is removed, its recorded answers with it, while the attempt and its score stay; a quiz whose Bronze row is gone is removed from Silver; a quiz Bronze holds but the reading cannot make sense of is left as it is and counted, never deleted for a failed reading). The report per course has `quizzes: { synced, questions, removed, unreadable, noLesson, skippedQuestions }`, shown on the import page.
- **Imported quizzes are read only in the builder** (the policies read the lesson's source) and members take them like any quiz. **Taking the course over from Kajabi keeps its quizzes** (the lesson flips to Hub-owned, the quiz rows stay and become editable) and the import then skips them: the sync function never touches a lesson that is not Kajabi's. A quiz taken over still carries its Kajabi key as a note; if a lesson is imported again and claims that Kajabi quiz, the key moves to it rather than failing the run.
- **Short answer and file upload questions are imported** as questions that are recorded and never scored (they have no answers to read: they are recognised by their type name, `short answer` or `file upload`, in a type-like field). Kajabi's "Graded question" toggle is read for every question: off means the choice question is imported ungraded (no right answer needed). A short answer or file upload Kajabi marks "graded manually" is imported as not graded too (the Hub has no manual grading) and counted as `ungradedManual` in the processing report (shown on the import page), so the owner knows. **Unverified against the live editor**, like the rest of the reading: the type names and the toggle's field are guesses from what the admin shows.
- **Not imported with the quiz:** Kajabi's completion emails. Members' past submissions (Kajabi's Results tab) come in by their own capture and import: see "Importing past quiz responses". A member's past completion of a quiz lesson may also arrive through the per-lesson progress import, if Kajabi lists the quiz lesson there (unchecked).
- **How to run it, and what to send back if it reads nothing.** From a worktree with the production env file: `npm run courses:content -- --course <kajabi-course-id> --env .env.prod` (dry run), look at the quiz lines, then again with `--apply`, then Sync from Kajabi on the admin import page (or `POST /api/process/courses`). If a quiz line says "no questions read", the script prints the captured documents' field names and types (never values); `npm run courses:content -- --probe <the quiz editor address>` prints the same for any page. Send those lines back and the reading is fixed from facts and re-run on the stored Bronze documents (`docs/TODO.md`, "Kajabi quiz capture: verify against the live quiz editor").

**Importing past quiz responses** (migration `20271126100002`; `src/lib/kajabi/quiz-responses.ts`, `quiz-responses-run.ts`, `src/lib/courses/quiz-response-import.ts`, `scripts/kajabi-course-content.ts --responses`, `POST /api/process/quiz-responses`). The owner's rule: **losing Kajabi must not lose anyone's writing or files**, so every submission of every quiz is kept, for everyone who answered, whether or not they are a member now.

- **Bronze** (`bronze.kajabi_quiz_responses`, one row per submission, keyed by Kajabi's quiz id and the submission's key): the responder's **email** (lower case; **no name**), when it was submitted, and each answer: the question **as it was worded in the submission** (a quiz is edited later, and Kajabi's own submission view showed different wording than its editor), what was written, the answers picked (Kajabi's keys and their wording), and for a file its name, size, source address and **where our copy is** (or why there is none). Admin read, service role write. A submission is **never removed** from Bronze because Kajabi no longer lists it. Uploaded files are copied into the private `quiz-answer-files` bucket at capture time (Kajabi's links may expire), under `kajabi-import/<quiz>/<submission>/<name>` (never inside a member's folder), checked against the Hub's own limits (10 MB, the allowed kinds); anything that could not be copied is counted by reason (`expired`, `too_big`, `type`, `empty`, `no_link`, `http`, `network`, `storage`), never by name.
- **The capture** is a mode of the content script, run by the owner in her logged-in browser (an agent cannot): `npm run courses:content -- --course <kajabi-course-id> --responses [--apply] [--env .env.prod]`. It works on the quizzes the lesson capture already found (run the lesson capture first), and for each reads the quiz's **Results list by id** from inside the logged-in page (`page.evaluate` with a same-origin `fetch`, GET only, the page's own session, nothing sent anywhere else). **The cookie alone is refused with a 401** (the first real dry run: "the login expired" while the window was logged in), so for each quiz the capture first loads its Results page through the app's router, records the headers the page's own `/api/admin/` requests carry (the Results list request wins; never cookie, host or what the browser sets itself, the same filter as the progress capture's `forwardableHeaders`) and replays exactly those on every fetch. Header values are secrets: only the names are printed (`replaying headers: authorization, x-csrf-token`). A 401 says which it is: if the page's own request was 401 or went to the login, "the login expired"; if the page loaded and only the replayed fetch was refused, "Kajabi refused the capture's request even with the page's headers (names: ...)", so she is not told to log in when she already is. If the page made no Results list request to copy from, the capture says so and uses the click path. The paths: `/api/admin/quizzes/:id/results` for the submission ids, then `/api/admin/quizzes/:id/results/:id` for each one. The list is paged by the API, and the page parameter is not known, so it tries `page[number]=N` and then `page=N` and **accepts one only when the answer's own `meta.currentPage` is N**; a server that ignores both stops the paging with "could not page" (never a loop). Clicking each row's "Grade quiz" control (the first version) stays only as the fallback when the list can't be fetched at all. Each quiz line says how many were listed, opened, failed to open and were **listed but never opened**, so listed and read always reconcile. **It only reads:** it never presses Publish grade, Pass, Fail or anything that writes in Kajabi. A dry run (no `--apply`) reads and copies nothing, and reports. It prints counts only (never a name, an email, an answer or a file name); a page it cannot read is described by field names and types. Exit code 1 when anything failed, was unreadable or was listed but never opened.
- **First run, in this order, and what to send back.** (1) **Probe** one quiz's results page and one submission, to learn what the pages really load before anything is trusted: `npm run courses:content -- --probe https://app.kajabi.com/admin/quizzes/<quiz id>/results --seconds 120`, opening a submission's "Grade quiz" while it records (it prints request paths and field names and types, never values). (2) The dry run above on one course (Self-Editing Academy), then with `--apply`. (3) Send back the printed totals and, if a quiz line says submissions are unreadable, the field names it printed. **What the probe showed (field names and types only), and what is still a guess.** The list is `GET /api/admin/quizzes/:id/results` (JSON:API: each row has `contactEmail`, `contactName`, `dateSubmittedAt`, counts, and `meta` with `currentPage`, `pageCount`, `itemsTotalCount`); one submission is `GET .../results/:id` with its answers in `included`. Each answer carries `questionTitle`, `questionType`, `questionPosition`, `answer` (text, or null) and `fileUrl`, and **no question id**. The reading handles exactly that: the responder is `contactEmail` (the name is never read or stored), the date is `dateSubmittedAt` (an ISO string, or the admin's own words such as "May 10, 2026 6:38 PM", read as the org's wall clock), a question with no id is keyed by its **position** (`pos:<n>`) with its title kept as the wording, a file is the `fileUrl`, and a choice question is read from its **choice records**: Kajabi lists every choice of the question as its own record (`choiceTitle`, `choicePosition`, `selected`) and the answer record names them all, so **only the choices with `selected: true` count** (by their title, in the order of their positions; Bronze keeps just those labels). A question with nothing selected is no answer; a choice record with no `selected` field at all is read the older way (the answer's own text or list). In the Hub a picked label is matched to an option of the question by its wording; if no option has it (it was reworded since), what was picked is kept as text and no option is chosen for them. Still guessed: the **values** of `type` and `questionType` (the reading recognises short answer, file upload and multiple choice or checkbox names tolerantly, and the capture **prints every distinct `type` and `questionType` value it saw**, which are Kajabi's own enum names and safe to paste back), whether `choices` ever carries ids or text, and how the Results tab is paged (the list's `meta` says when the last page is done and how many rows there are, so a run that opened fewer says it did not finish; going to the next page is a click on a Next control, which is unverified). The reading re-runs on a new capture; nothing in Bronze depends on a wrong guess because only parsed fields are stored. (`docs/TODO.md`, "Verify the quiz responses capture against the live Kajabi admin".)
- **The import to the members** (`POST /api/process/quiz-responses`, body `{ "course": "<slug>", "dryRun": true }`; the answer is counts only; permission `data.pipeline.run`; step after the capture): Bronze to Local, **insert only and repeatable**.
  - **Matching** is by **email only**, to **any** existing member (current, lapsed, cancelled, on hiatus), through their own email or an active email alias; a merged member's other addresses are aliases of the member who was kept (`member_email_aliases`, `docs/product/MEMBER_MERGE.md`). One address shared by several members is ambiguous and is skipped, never guessed. **No course access is required.** A responder with no member is **kept, no member yet**: it stays in Bronze, is counted, and is picked up by a later run once a member with that email (or alias) exists. Members are never created from this.
  - **What is written:** an attempt per submission (`quiz_attempts.imported_from = 'kajabi'`, `source_key` = Kajabi's submission key, unique per quiz), with its answers. Imported attempts are **not scored** (a history, not a re-grade: `question_count` 0, counted as passed) and **send no notification and write no activity**. **An answer is joined to the Hub's question** by the editor's question id when the submission carries one, otherwise by its **position in the quiz with the title as a check** (the same title, ignoring case and spacing, and the same kind when the submission names one). A quiz edited after the answer was given no longer lines up, and then there is no link: the answer is **kept on the attempt with its own wording and position** (`quiz_attempts.unlinked_answers`, its file copied into the member's folder), shown to staff under "Answers to questions that no longer match" and to the member with their earlier answers, never attached to a question it may not belong to and never dropped; it is counted as `unlinked`. A choice answer is mapped by the option's Kajabi key, then by its wording; an answer to a question Kajabi dropped, or one that can't be read, is counted and left out. Each answer keeps the question as it was worded then (`quiz_attempt_answers.prompt_snapshot`), which staff and the member see in place of the current wording. A submitted file is **copied from the Bronze copy into the member's own folder** (so a removal request deletes it) and the Bronze copy stays for reprocessing; one whose copy is missing is counted.
  - **Insert only:** a submission is added once (the database refuses a second per quiz), an attempt the member made in the Hub has no key and is never touched, and a second run inserts nothing.
  - **Who can see it.** The member (their own, including when their access to the course has ended: it is their writing) and staff with `courses.manage`. A member without access to the course can't open the lesson, so their imported answers are visible to staff in the Results tab ("From Kajabi", "View answers"), and to the member again if access returns; the import grants nothing they didn't have.
  - **Completion is not this import's.** It writes attempts and answers only, never `lesson_progress`: which lessons a member completed comes from the separate Kajabi progress import (`courses:content --progress`, `src/lib/courses/progress-import.ts`, "Importing Kajabi per-lesson progress"), the one place that decides who completed what (members, dates, existing rows). Run the progress import for the course as well if quiz lessons should show complete; it matches members the same way (any existing member, no access needed), so a lapsed member's completion is recorded too.
- **Counts the import returns, per course:** submissions in Bronze, responders matched / kept (no member yet) / ambiguous, submissions imported / already imported / kept / ambiguous / quiz not in the Hub, answers imported / unlinked (kept with their own wording) / question gone / unreadable, files copied / not copied.
- **Not covered:** the capture is not a step of `courses:migrate` (`docs/TODO.md`, "`courses:migrate`: add the quiz step and exercise it against real Kajabi").

**Removal on request** (migration `20271127000000`; `remove_member_data` and its dry run `member_removal_extras`, `src/lib/member-removal.ts`, the "Remove on request" panel on the admin member page, `/privacy`'s promise that a removal request "either deletes it or takes your name off it"). Removing a member deletes **everything the Hub holds about their quiz work, in the Hub and in the Kajabi capture copies**, and the "See what would be removed" step lists each kind with its count **before** anything is removed (counts only: never a word they wrote, a name or a file name).

| What | Where | What a removal does |
|---|---|---|
| Quiz attempts and their answers (written, and the choices picked) | `quiz_attempts`, `quiz_attempt_answers` | Deleted (the catalog's `delete`; answers go with the attempt). |
| Files they uploaded as answers, and the copies of imported ones made into their folder | `quiz-answer-files/<member id>/...` | Deleted after the database part has committed, folder by folder; a failure is shown as something to finish by hand. |
| Past Kajabi responses | `bronze.kajabi_quiz_responses`, by their email **and every alias** (active or not) | Deleted (a real delete, not the soft delete Bronze uses elsewhere). |
| The capture's copies of their uploaded files | `quiz-answer-files/kajabi-import/<quiz>/<submission>/<name>`, named by the rows just deleted | Deleted after the commit, in batches. |
| Their per-lesson and per-product Kajabi progress | `bronze.kajabi_lesson_progress`, `bronze.kajabi_product_progress`, by email and every alias | Deleted. |
| Their lesson comments as captured | `bronze.kajabi_lesson_comments`, only the rows the comments import **matched to them** | Deleted. |
| Captured comments that only carry the same name | the same table | **Kept, and counted** in the dry run (see below). |
| Rows under an address another member also holds | the Bronze tables above | **Kept, and counted**: they can't be told apart. |
| The Hub comment and the Hub's lesson progress they imported | `lesson_comments`, `lesson_progress` | Unchanged by this: the catalog keeps them under the "Former member" placeholder (`member_data_handling`). |

- **Why comments are special.** A captured comment carries only the name Kajabi shows, and the Hub never matches a person by name. So a Bronze comment is deleted only when the import has already matched it to this member, which is exact: the Hub comment's `source_key` (`<lesson>/<comment key>`) names the capture row. A capture row that merely has the same name, never matched (the name matched nobody, or several people), could be anyone's and is **not** deleted; the dry run counts them ("Kajabi comments under the same name, not matched") so staff can look at them by hand in Kajabi. Deleting by name would delete a namesake's words. The Hub's own copy of a matched comment stays as the catalog decided (the text, written by "Former member"); only the capture copy with the real name goes.
- **It can't come back: removal fingerprints** (`public.removal_tombstones`, service role only). The removal first writes a one-way fingerprint (SHA-256 of the trimmed, lower-cased text, `public.removal_hash`, computed the same way in `src/lib/data/removal-hash.ts`) of each address it acts on and of each comment key it deletes. Triggers on the four capture tables (`skip_removed_*`) then drop any row whose fingerprint is there, whoever writes it: the capture scripts, the Product Progress CSV upload, a re-run. The responses capture also checks before downloading anything, so a removed member's files are never copied again, and says how many submissions it skipped. Nothing in the fingerprint says who it was, but anyone who already has an address can compute its fingerprint, so it is kept only for this purpose and only for removed people. The Silver import finds nothing to import for them: their Bronze rows are gone, and their aliases and address no longer match any member.
- **Idempotent and reprocessable.** `remove_member_data` is one transaction and refuses a member who was already removed. After it commits, the caller runs `purge_removed_member_bronze()`, the same deletion driven by the fingerprints alone, to catch a capture row written between the removal reading the tables and its commit; it changes nothing when there is nothing to do and can be run by hand at any time (it returns the files to delete, which the caller removes). A capture or import run again, or the Bronze reprocessed, cannot restore a removed person's rows.
- **If the person comes back and asks to be captured again,** delete their fingerprint (`DELETE FROM public.removal_tombstones WHERE kind = 'email' AND key_hash = public.removal_hash('<address>')`, run by staff in the database); until then their later Kajabi responses are not stored. Someone who rejoins is a new member and starts fresh either way.
- **What a removal does not reach** is in `docs/TODO.md`: the Kajabi contact, customer and payment copies, and the Stripe, Zoom and Slack copies, are still removed at the source (the panel says so); the Hub comment's text stays under the placeholder.

**Locks.** A pass is what completes the lesson, and Kajabi lets a passed quiz unlock a locked module. The Hub does not have lock prerequisites yet (`docs/TODO.md`, "Course editor: prerequisite unlock for locked modules"); when it does, "complete" means `lesson_progress.completed_at`, which a quiz lesson sets on a pass, so a quiz can be the prerequisite with no quiz-specific code.

## Lesson downloads

Files members can save from a lesson are `course_lesson_files` rows pointing at objects in the private `course-files` bucket (limit 50 MB); members get them through the lesson download route, which checks they can open the lesson and redirects to a 60-second signed link. An imported lesson's are copied from Kajabi (`src/lib/courses/files.ts`). On a **Hub-owned lesson** (`src/app/(admin)/admin/courses/lesson-file-actions.ts`) an admin adds and removes them: `startLessonFileUpload` checks the name and size and makes a one-use signed upload for a path under the course and lesson; the browser sends the file straight to the bucket (a server action can't carry a file this size); `finishLessonFileUpload` then looks in the bucket, records the row with the size the bucket reports (not the browser's) and puts it last, and removes the object again if the row can't be saved. The path comes from the server, never the browser: the key must be one the start action made. Removing deletes the row first and then the object. Deleting a lesson, or a module with lessons, from the outline or the lesson page removes their stored files too, once the database delete has worked (a failure there is logged and leaves an unreferenced object, not a failed delete). `source_key` is `up-<uuid>` for these (Kajabi's are Kajabi's file ids), so the Kajabi sync, which only looks at imported lessons' files, never touches them.

## Captions

Captions are timed text the player shows from the Mux stream: the member player (`LessonPlayer`) already lists every text track the stream has in its captions menu, remembers the choice by name, and records which one was on while a stretch was watched. The Hub's rows are `video_text_tracks` (one per caption, subtitle or transcript file of a `videos` row). On a **Hub-owned lesson whose video is ready**, the page has a Captions section (`LessonCaptions`, actions in `src/app/(admin)/admin/courses/lesson-caption-actions.ts`): **Upload captions** takes a WebVTT or SubRip file (up to 800 KB, read in the browser and sent as text, since a server action carries little more than that), checks it is one (`src/lib/courses/captions.ts`), keeps it in the private `course-files` bucket under `video-tracks/<video id>/` (the source of truth, so the track can be attached again) and attaches it to the Mux asset from a 10-minute signed link; the track is a `captions` row, `source` `manual`, marked as closed captions. **Make captions automatically** asks Mux to write subtitles from the audio (a `subtitles` row, `source` `mux_auto`, named "(automatic)"; Mux fills it in after a while). A language that already has captions can't be generated again, a name can't repeat on a video, and **an uploaded file replaces the automatic track in the same language**. **Remove** takes the track off Mux (a track Mux no longer has counts as removed), out of the bucket and out of the table; if a step fails after another succeeded, the earlier step is undone (no stray file in the bucket or track on Mux). Languages offered are the 22 Mux can write subtitles for. Imported videos have captions burned into the picture and are not offered this (`docs/TODO.md`, "Course editor: captions, the rest").

## Lesson thumbnails

A lesson's thumbnail is a media library image (`course_lessons.thumbnail_asset_id`), served whole by `/api/courses/image/lesson/<id>` (so there is no crop or focal point to adjust, and the picker leaves out Adjust crop). On a **Hub-owned lesson** the page has the shared image picker (`LessonThumbnailField`, surface `lesson_thumbnail`): choose from the library or upload one there. `setLessonThumbnail` (`outline-actions.ts`) takes a library image that exists and isn't deleted, and also forgets the Kajabi picture the lesson had (`thumbnail_url`), so clearing the thumbnail doesn't bring that back. Under the video, **Use the frame at m:ss as the thumbnail** (`setLessonThumbnailFromVideo`, `lesson-video-actions.ts`) takes where the player is now: it asks Mux for a signed still at that moment (1280 wide), keeps it in the library (`source` `video_frame`, titled with the lesson and the time, tagged "lesson thumbnail"; the same frame twice is one image) and sets it. The still comes from Mux, so it needs the video to be ready.

## Video

- **Uploaded in the Hub** (a Hub-owned lesson, `src/app/(admin)/admin/courses/lesson-video-actions.ts`): a **direct upload**, so the file goes from the admin's browser straight to Mux in pieces (`@mux/upchunk`, which resumes after a dropped connection) and never through our server. Starting an upload (`startLessonVideoUpload`) makes the Mux upload address, a `videos` row (`source_kind` `upload`, `source_key` the Mux upload id) and points the lesson at it, so the page shows "being prepared" at once. Mux's webhook then does the rest: `video.upload.asset_created` tells the row which asset was made, `video.asset.ready` gives it the playback id and length, and the length is copied onto the lesson (`syncLessonDurations`). Where there is no webhook (a preview, or if it is down) the page asks Mux itself every 15 seconds and with **Check again** (`refreshLessonVideo`). Replace uploads a new one and points the lesson at it; Remove only takes the video off the lesson (`docs/TODO.md`: the old assets stay in Mux). Same quality and signed playback as the imported videos.
- **Mux:**
  - Signed playback policy, 1080p cap.
  - **Quality tier** (`video_quality`) is a per-video setting, set when the asset is created. *Basic* uses a reduced encoding ladder at a lower target quality and has no encoding charge. *Plus* uses per-title AI encoding (raises bitrate where the picture is complex, lowers it where it isn't) and costs about $0.03/min once at upload. Basic is the default in new Mux accounts.
  - **Slides with small text are what basic tends to soften,** and webinars are mostly slides. So: course lessons use *plus* (The Ready Writer is about 650 minutes, roughly $20 once); the webinar archive starts as *basic* and moves to *plus* only if a side-by-side test of a few replays shows a difference.
  - **Changing the tier later means re-ingesting:** the docs describe the tier only as a create-time setting, so a quality change is a new asset from the same source, then repointing the lesson (the same swap as a changed video) and deleting the old asset. It isn't a config toggle on existing videos. Storage cost is the same either way.
  - Free auto-captions off for now (captions are burned in).
  - Audio-only static rendition once audio mode is built.
- **Keys:** `MUX_TOKEN_ID`/`MUX_TOKEN_SECRET` (Video read+write, Data read, High priority), `MUX_SIGNING_KEY_ID`/`MUX_SIGNING_KEY_PRIVATE`, and `MUX_WEBHOOK_SECRET` (declared in `config/env-vars.ts`). The bulk migration should use a separate Low-priority token so it doesn't compete with member traffic.
- **"Video ready":** production uses the Mux webhook at `/api/webhooks/mux`, verified with `MUX_WEBHOOK_SECRET`. The route **rejects every request while that secret is unset** (`webhookSecretRequired()` in `src/lib/webhook-auth.ts`, PR #105), instead of skipping verification. Preview deployments have changing URLs, so they poll Mux for status instead. Built: `src/lib/mux.ts` (asset creation, signed playback tokens, webhook verification) and `src/app/api/webhooks/mux/route.ts`, which marks the matching `videos` row ready or errored (idempotent). It answers 500 for an asset with no `videos` row yet, so Mux redelivers; the media step must save `mux_asset_id` right after creating the asset.
- **Player:** a half-day test of Video.js v10 (GA 2026-10-01) against a Mux stream. It must have a speed menu, keyboard shortcuts, seek previews (Mux storyboards) and theming; fall back to Vidstack if it doesn't. Watch tracking listens to the underlying video element, so the choice doesn't affect data.
  - **Spike result (2026-10-18, `/admin/player-spike`, `@videojs/react` 10.1.0 with `MuxVideo`):** playback, the speed menu, keyboard shortcuts and Mux storyboard seek previews work. A `<track kind="captions">` child of `MuxVideo` adds the captions button and shows cues; with several tracks the gear menu gets a Captions submenu (Off, one entry per track) and the `c` key toggles captions. Theming works through the skin's public CSS properties (`--media-accent-color`, `--media-accent-text-color`, `--media-border-radius`, `--media-font-family`), set on `VideoSkin`; the Hub's plum is applied in the spike.
  - **Signed playback (checked 2026-10-18 against a signed-policy test asset):** the server mints three JWTs per lesson view with `@mux/ts` (`mux.jwt.signPlaybackId(id, { type: ["video", "thumbnail", "storyboard"], expiration })`) and passes them as `source.playback.token`, `source.poster.token` and `source.storyboard.token`. Mux answers 403 without a token, and 200 with the right one for the manifest, poster and storyboard; **a token is only good for its own type** (the video token on a poster gets 403), so all three are needed. The spike page mints them behind the admin check (`/admin/player-spike?signed=<playbackId>`). Playback, the poster before play, seek previews on hover, captions and watch segments all work in a real browser with the signed asset (checked by hand 2026-10-18), so the spike has nothing left to check.
  - **Watch segments work from the media element:** `useMedia()` returns the attached media, which takes `addEventListener` for `playing`, `pause`, `seeking`, `seeked`, `ratechange`, `timeupdate` and `ended` (plus `visibilitychange` and `pagehide` on the page). The spike opens a segment on play and closes it on pause, seek, speed change, tab hide, page leave or end; a seek of +10s with the arrow key produced two segments (`0.0s to 4.7s (seek)`, `14.9s to 19.0s (pause)`). Speed-change and page-leave closing are wired but weren't exercised by hand.
  - **Segments carry the captions state** (`captions_language`, null = off), and toggling captions closes the segment and opens a new one, like a speed change. Checked: pressing `c` mid-playback gave `captions Spanish (captions)` then `captions off (pause)`.
  - **Segments carry `visible` too** (was the tab on screen), and both hiding and showing the tab close the segment and open a new one if the video is still playing. Hiding must not end tracking (audio keeps playing, and no `playing` event fires on return), and it separates watching from playing in the background, which audio mode will need. Batch these small rows before sending.
  - The captions button is the skin's own "subtitles" icon (a rectangle with lines), not the "CC" glyph viewers know from YouTube, and `c` is the same toggle: it shows or hides the most recently chosen language.
  - **Captions aren't remembered either:** `useCaptionsOptions` gives the options and `setValue`. Save the chosen option's *label* (track ids change per video) and restore it once `canPlay`, skipping saves until the restore has landed.
  - **Speed isn't remembered by the player:** we save it (per member) and restore it ourselves. Store actions throw `NO_TARGET` until the media element is attached, so wait for `selectSource` → `canPlay` before calling them.
  - **Needs `@videojs/mux-video`** installed alongside `@videojs/react`.
  - **Skin CSS and Tailwind v3 (settled):** the skin stylesheet uses native `@layer base|components`, which Tailwind v3 rejects ("no matching `@tailwind base` directive"). `config/postcss-tailwind-skip-vendor-layers.mjs` wraps the Tailwind plugin and skips files under `node_modules/@videojs/`, so the skin is imported normally (`import "@videojs/react/video/skin.css"`) and keeps its native cascade layers. Turbopack resolves PostCSS plugin names from inside `.next`, so `postcss.config.mjs` gives the plugin by absolute path. The stale copy in `public/` is gone.
- **Playback failures explain themselves, and an expired link is retried once (built).** Links last 1 hour (`src/lib/mux.ts`), so a lesson tab left open longer fails its next request with a network error (media error code 2). `PlaybackReporter` in `LessonPlayer.tsx` handles it: on a network error with a page older than ~50 minutes (or an HTTP 401/403 from Mux, or a page that can't say when its links were minted), it asks `POST /api/courses/playback-tokens` for fresh links for that lesson, loads them (a new `source` reloads the video), picks up at the same second and plays again if it was playing, and records `course_video_token_refreshed` with no alert. The endpoint repeats the lesson page's checks (courses on for the effective member, course access current, an open lesson or staff preview, a ready video), adds the restriction claim by the same rule as the page, answers every refusal after sign-in with the same 404, is never cached and is limited to 10 per user per 10 minutes. If the retry fails, or the error isn't one a fresh link fixes, the player records `course_video_error` and alerts staff, once per page view. The event and the alert say what failed: the lesson id, the Mux playback id, whether the token carried the restriction, and, for the hls.js players, the HTTP status and hls.js's error detail (`manifestLoadError`); Safari's native player gives only the code. The playback token is never recorded or sent. The decision logic is `src/lib/courses/playback-refresh.ts`. **Not verified in a real browser:** that a changed `source` reloads a player after a fatal hls.js error; check by letting a lesson tab sit for over an hour.
- **Mux Data (on for everyone, consent-gated):** the lesson player reports playback quality (start time, buffering, errors) to Mux Data through the `MuxData` extension, anonymously and only where usage statistics are allowed. See `docs/infrastructure/MUX_CONFIG.md`, "Mux Data", for what it sends, the consent rule and the rollout; `docs/product/ANALYTICS.md` for why it is consent-gated when the watch data below is not.

  - The lesson player (`LessonPlayer.tsx`) records each continuous stretch (start, end, speed, captions language, tab visible) from the media element, as the spike did. It opens on play and closes on pause, seek, speed or captions change, tab hide or show, page leave or the end, and drops stretches under a quarter second.
  - Stretches are batched and sent to `POST /api/courses/watch` on pause, end, tab hide and page leave (`fetch` with `keepalive`, so it finishes while the page unloads), and checkpointed every 30 seconds while playing, so a crash loses at most that much.
  - **Retries are safe:** the player gives each stretch an id (`client_segment_id`) when it closes, unique per member, and the route inserts with `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`, so a resend of a save that already arrived (its response was lost) is stored once. A save that fails for a passing reason (offline, a 5xx) goes back in a queue (newest 200) and is resent with the next one, in batches of at most 50; a refusal (4xx) is dropped. A save lost while the page unloads can't be retried, since the page is gone.
  - The route checks like Mark complete does (courses on, access, an open lesson) and that the lesson has a video, takes the video from the lesson (never from the request), and writes as the member. It writes **nothing in sudo**, and the player doesn't track in a staff preview of a "Coming soon" lesson. It also stores the playhead in `lesson_progress.last_position_seconds`, leaving `completed_at` alone.
  - **Resume:** the lesson page passes the saved position (ignored under 10 seconds) and the player seeks to it once the length is known, unless that is 95% or more of the video (then it starts over).
  - **Staff watch stats (built):** `/admin/courses/<course id>/stats` lists each lesson with a video (viewers, average share watched, viewers who covered 90%+, a small engagement graph), and `/admin/courses/<course id>/stats/<lesson id>` has the full view: the numbers, an **Engagement panel** (`EngagementPanel`: Kajabi's heatmap and a Wistia-style engagement graph in one). Per moment of the video it shows three counts on one axis, each stricter than the last, so **people <= plays <= times watched**: **times watched** (every pass over the moment, a rewind or replay counting each time: the area), **plays** (visits, i.e. player sessions, that watched it, so rewinding inside a visit is still one) and **people** (different members, each once however often or on however many visits). One person watching a part twice in one visit is 2, 1, 1; coming back another day is 2, 2, 1; with nothing replayed the three lines coincide. The video's own frames are the backdrop (signed Mux thumbnails, one token per slice since Mux wants a signed still's `time` inside the token's claims, not the URL): moving the pointer or the arrow keys shows the still from that moment, a vertical line, and a tooltip with the time, the three counts and the share of plays that watched it. The frames can be switched off with the panel's checkbox or the `showFrames` prop (a plain dark panel with the same chart; also what it falls back to when stills can't be signed), and a table view has every value. All three counts are measured the same way, as how much of the slice was covered (a slice covered half by one person is 0.5 on each, not a whole person), which is what keeps people from ever exceeding times watched. Also shown: how it was watched (playback speeds, captions on, playing in the background). Each stat tile opens to its definition. A **play** is one visit (player session) in which the video was played, so the Total plays tile counts visits, and the same member returning another day is another play (and still one *unique* play over the range). **Engagement** is Kajabi's: total watch time / (total plays x video length), where a **play** is a player session (one visit to the lesson) with at least one stretch, so a member returning another day is another play. **Play rate** is plays / views, where a *view* is the player appearing for a session: the player sends a **`viewed` marker** when it mounts (a row in `video_watch_segments` with `kind = 'viewed'`, a point at 0, `client_segment_id` = the player session id so a resend is a no-op, and the browser's `user_agent`), whether or not anything is played. Anything counting stretches filters on `kind = 'watched'`; a session with stretches counts as viewed even without a marker, so a marker that never arrived (a dropped request) can't push play rate above 100%. The lesson page also lists the **views** (`ViewerList`): one row per player session, newest first, with the **member's name**, a bar of which parts of the video they covered and the percent, and on opening a row the device (from the user agent, via `describeDevice`) and how long they played; "Load more" asks for another 20 (`?limit=`). Opening a row also shows **where the visit came from** (place and raw IP): not stored with the video data, but looked up from the access log as the member's latest located `access_events` row before the visit (see `docs/product/ANALYTICS.md`, "Course viewing data"), "Not recorded" when there is none. The marker is first-party course data like the stretches, not a consent-gated analytics event (see `docs/product/ANALYTICS.md`, "Course viewing data"). **Over time** (`TimeSeriesSection`, `src/lib/courses/watch-timeseries.ts`, pure): like Kajabi's Analytics modal, one metric by day, week (Sunday first) or month over a date range: Today, Last 7/30 days, Last and This month/quarter, This year, Last 12 months, All time, or custom dates, all in the organisation's timezone as whole local days. The metrics: **Total plays** (visits with a stretch), **Unique plays** (different members who played, each once), **Unique viewers** (different members who opened the video, played or not), **Play rate** (plays / views), **Engagement rate** (watch time / (plays x video length)) and **Hours watched**. Each visit counts on the local day it *began*; the header total is for the whole range (unique counts and rates don't add up from the points); every day in the range is a point, zero where nothing happened; a daily range over 400 days is shown by week. It is a plain GET form (`?metric=&range=&by=&from=&to=`), so a view is a shareable link, and "Load more" keeps the settings. Kajabi's "location" filter (which page the video is on) has no equivalent: our segments belong to a video, not to a lesson. The maths is `src/lib/courses/watch-stats.ts` (pure; times are video time, stretches are clipped to the video's length, and the graph is at most 100 slices), the reads are `src/lib/courses/watch-stats-data.ts` (service role behind `requirePermission(...)`, paginated). Staff members' own viewing (admins' member records) is left out by default, since it is test data (`?staff=1` includes it). A per-member view is a follow-up.
  - Completion is still the 90%-playhead rule in `LessonCompletion.tsx`; moving it to "90% of the video actually watched", from these rows, is not done. Neither is the per-member speed setting or a per-member stats view.
- **Cost:** The Ready Writer is about 650 minutes, roughly $2/month of storage, inside Mux's $20/month usage credit. Viewing is free under 100,000 minutes a month.

### Recommendation: their own Local tables

Three options were weighed:
- **Own Local tables (recommended).** Comments belong to a lesson, follow course access, and need instructor badges, an "unanswered" view and rules tied to access. Those are course rules, not chat rules.
- **Reuse `chat_*`.** Chat mirrors Slack channels (`docs/product/SLACK_BRIDGED_CHAT.md`): its membership, privacy (restricted channels, break-glass) and bridge model don't fit "everyone with course access", and a channel per lesson would flood the sidebar.
- **A Slack thread per lesson.** Needs a channel and bot posts per lesson, shows content only to people who connected Slack, and ties course access to Slack membership.

We still copy chat's good parts: soft delete, tombstones, and the escaping and name-resolution helpers (`src/lib/chat/render.ts`, `src/lib/chat/names.ts`). Nothing is bridged to Slack; staff hear about new comments through notifications and the admin queue.

### Data model

New Local tables (never reprocessed), text ids per `.claude/rules/resource-ids.md` "New Tables and Ids", grants and RLS in the same migration (`supabase-table-grants.md`):
- **`lesson_comments`**: `id` (`cmnt_`), `lesson_id` (text FK to `course_lessons`), `member_id` (author, text FK to `members`), `parent_id` (null for a top-level comment), `body` (plain text, 1 to 4,000 characters), `is_staff_reply` (the column keeps its old name; it means "the author is one of the course's instructors", set by the server when the comment is posted and recomputed when the course's instructors change: see "Course instructors"), `created_at`, `edited_at`, `hidden_at`, `deleted_at`, `deleted_by`.
- **`lesson_comment_reports`**: `id`, `comment_id`, `reporter_id`, `reason`, `created_at`, `resolved_at`, `resolved_by`. One open report per reporter and comment.
- **`lesson_comment_follows`**: `(member_id, lesson_id, following)`. Posting or replying follows implicitly; rows exist only for explicit choices and unfollowing is `following = false`.
- **`lesson_comment_reads`** (phase 3): last-seen per member and lesson, for unread counts.

Decisions:
- **Prefix and tier.** Notifications link to a comment, so its id is shown to people. A Tier 2 id must not be exposed (`.claude/rules/resource-ids.md` "Resource ID Tiers"), so `lesson_comments` needs a Tier 1 prefix agreed with the owner (`cmnt_`: `cmt_` is already `prickle_commitments`); reports, follows and reads stay Tier 2.
- **Threading depth: one level.** A reply points at a top-level comment; replying to a reply attaches to the same parent, quoting the author's name. Alternatives: flat; unlimited nesting (unreadable on a phone).
- **Edit window: 15 minutes**, then locked (the author can still delete). Edits set `edited_at` and show "edited". Alternative: always editable, which leaves replies out of context.
- **Soft delete** with `deleted_at`. A deleted comment with replies shows a tombstone ("Comment removed"); without replies it disappears. Text is cleared by a retention job (below), not at delete time, so staff can undo.
- **RLS.** Read: members with *current* `has_course_access(course_id)` for the lesson's course, and admins. Insert: the same members as themselves. Update/delete: own rows (edit window, soft delete); admins act through server actions. Reports: insert own, read admin only. Follows and reads: own rows. Everything is **read-only in sudo** (an admin previewing as a member can read, never post, report or follow). Server actions write through the service-role client scoped to the effective member like `src/lib/courses/member.ts`, with RLS as the second layer.
- A lesson the member can't open ("Coming soon", hidden) shows no comments.
- Lessons already upsert on their source key, so comments stay attached across migration re-runs.

### Who can comment

- **Members with current access to the course.** Admins can always read and comment. A per-course instructor list is a later option, if Ania wants non-admin helpers.
- **Ended access: comments are closed with the lessons** (decided 2026-11-02): comments live on lesson pages, which don't open once access ends, so a member whose access ended can neither read nor write them. The course overview with their progress stays visible.
- **Instructor replies** carry an "Instructor" badge from `is_staff_reply` (decided later: it means the author is one of the course's instructors, not that they are staff; see "Course instructors").

### Moderation

- **Report**: any member can report a comment with a short reason; the author is never told who.
- **Hide vs delete**: staff can hide (reversible, tombstone for members) or delete (soft delete). Both write the audit log (`docs/architecture/ACTIVITY_AND_AUDIT_LOG.md`): who, which comment, which action, why. Authors can delete their own.
- **Rate limits** in the server action, not the client: 5 comments a minute and 30 an hour per member.
- **Spam and links**: links render as plain links with `rel="nofollow noopener"`; a comment with more than two links is held for staff review (visible to its author only). No profanity filter in phase 1: reports and the queue cover it, and filters mangle writing talk ("kill your darlings"). Alternative: a blocklist that holds, never rejects.
- Admin pages start with `requirePermission(...)` and staff actions with `requireActionPermission(...)` (`.claude/rules/admin-route-protection.md` "Admin Route Protection").

### Safety of rendered text

- **Store plain text, render as text.** No HTML and no Markdown in phase 1; React escapes it. Line breaks are kept; only `http(s)` URLs are auto-linked.
- **Mentions** (phase 4): `@name` autocompletes through `EntitySearch` (`reuse-ui-components.md`) and is stored as a token holding the member id, resolved to the current display name at render, as Slack tokens are (`src/lib/chat/names.ts`). Only people with access to the course can be mentioned; a mention is a notification.
- **Escaping lessons from the rule files in `.claude/rules/`**: store text as typed, escape only where a target needs it (Slack: `&`, `<`, `>`), never decode on display. Tests use a name containing `&` and `<`, so a literal `&amp;` on screen fails.
- Comment text never goes into analytics events, logs or Sentry (`docs/product/ANALYTICS.md`).

### Notifications

- **`lesson_comment_instructor_reply`**: an instructor replied to me. A distinct kind so a member can mute others' replies but keep instructors'.

- **Staff** get new top-level questions in the admin queue and an instructor digest, not a DM per comment.
- Links go to the exact comment (`/courses/<slug>/lessons/<id>?comment=<cmnt_...>`, which scrolls to and highlights it). Not time-sensitive; no link previews. Check the link with the test message (`/admin/notifications`).

A `lesson_comment_notifications` flag with `liveFor` (as in `src/lib/member-reminders.ts`) logs member id, kind, channels and message and writes no dedup row; then widen: Cody, then staff, then everyone. A member with no reachable channel doesn't consume a dedup claim.
- Opening the thread calls `resolveInAppNotifications` for its notifications.

The member site is a different address from `app.kajabi.com`, so the browser window may ask for a member login there. **First probe (2026-10-30) of a lesson page:** it made no JSON request for comments, only for the lesson's videos, so the comments are most likely in the page's HTML and \"Show More\" loads an HTML fragment, which the probe did not record (it listed JSON only). The probe now records every tab (the link opens a new one), samples the page structure while it records, and says so when it ends on a login. **Third probe (2026-10-31, started from the lesson's admin editor, clicking Preview and Show More).** (1) The preview link is `/products/SLUG/categories/ID/posts/ID?preview_token=...`, which redirects twice; the admin's posts list (`/api/admin/products/ID/posts`, seen in the progress probe) carries each lesson's `urls.previewUrl` and `urls.viewCommentsUrl`, so every lesson's link is available from one admin request, no clicking. (2) The first comments are in the page's HTML; \"Show More\" is `GET /posts/ID/comments/show_more?desc_from=...&per=...`, answered with JavaScript (`text/javascript`), not JSON: a cursor (`desc_from`) and a page size (`per`). (3) The page-structure function returned nothing, a bug (`tsx` injects a `__name` helper the browser lacks), now fixed, and the probe also describes the markup inside any HTML or script response (`describeMarkup`: element, class and attribute names and link paths only), which is where the comment markup should show. **First real dry run (2026-11-01, a 52-lesson course):** 79 comments in 4 lessons, every id from the reply link (none hashed). Most lessons showed no comments list because this course makes members finish a lesson before the next opens, and the preview opens as a member with no progress: the page says \"This Post is Locked\". The capture counts those separately; their comments can only be read once the preview account has completed the earlier lessons (or the course does not gate lessons). **Second dry run (2026-11-02):** the one lesson that was none of the expected cases was a quiz page (radio-button questions, no comment box), so it has no comments by design; the capture now counts quiz pages on their own, which matters for a course with many (Self-Editing Academy's Reflection lessons are quizzes). **The decisive unknown** is whether the comment markup carries anything that identifies the author beyond the displayed name (a Kajabi member or contact id would map to an email through the Product Progress roster, which has both); without it every imported comment is a name only, which is the decided fallback.
- **Capture (built, unverified against the live site).** `npm run courses:content -- --course <id> --comments [--apply]` (`scripts/kajabi-course-content.ts`, `src/lib/kajabi/comments-capture.ts`, `comments-run.ts`). It opens the course's admin product page, takes the posts list the page requests itself (`/api/admin/products/{id}/posts`, `data[].attributes.urls.viewCommentsUrl`), then opens each lesson that Bronze has (2 at a time) in a new page, waits for the comments to stop arriving (it scrolls down and counts until the count holds still), presses Show More and any control reading like "View previous comments" or "3 replies" until none is left (at most 200 presses; a lesson that hits the cap, or whose press brings nothing while the button remains, writes nothing and is counted), and reads the page. It only navigates and presses those controls (`EXPANDER_PATTERN`: nothing that writes matches): it never types, submits a form or clicks Reply or Post Comment. **The markup it reads** (names from the probes): `div.comments[data-comments-list]` holds `div.comment[data-comment]`, each with `p.comment__name` (the displayed name), `p.comment__date time[datetime]`, `div.comment__body` (paragraphs and `br`; read as text, so markup in a body never survives), `a.comment__reply` and `div[data-comment-reply]`; replies are `div.comment`s nested in their parent's `div.comment--reply`. There is no author id, email, profile link or staff marker, and avatar addresses are not stored (they can carry a name). **Comment id.** Unknown until the first dry run: the key is the id found in the reply link's query (a numeric parameter named like parent or comment), else the digits ending the reply container's `id`, else a numeric `data-comment` value, else a hash of lesson, author name, `datetime` and normalized text (prefixed `h:`; an id that two comments of one lesson share is not a comment's own, for example a reply link that names the thread, so it is dropped for all of them and each is hashed; identical hashes get `~2`...). The summary prints how many ids came from each source and how many were hashed. **Writes** `bronze.kajabi_lesson_comments` (key `lesson_kajabi_id, comment_key`; `parent_comment_key` from nesting; `author_name`, `posted_at`, plain-text `body`; admin read, service role write; names are personal data and are never logged): per lesson a snapshot, changed rows updated, comments no longer listed soft-deleted **only when the read is shown to be complete** (the page prints a total, at least that many were read, at least one was read, and no Show More or replies control is left; otherwise nothing is removed and the summary counts the comments held back), unchanged writes nothing. A page with no comments list writes nothing and is counted. Output is counts only. The Silver import below reads these rows.
  - **Reading the capture's summary (compare it with Kajabi).** The posts list is followed across pages when it has them, and the run prints how many lessons it listed. Each lesson prints `lesson <id>: N comments (M replies), K controls pressed, page total T`, and anything that suggests comments were missed prints a `WARNING:` line naming the lesson id: a comments list on the page with no comment read from it; a printed total higher than what was read; a Show More or replies control still on the page at the end; a frame on a page with no comments read; a lesson with a comment box and no list whose page still prints a total; stored comments not marked removed because the read was not shown to be complete. The end prints `seen:` (lessons listed, read, with comments, comments, replies, controls pressed) and `warnings:` (a count and a breakdown). **To check a run:** open three or four lessons in Kajabi, one with many comments, one with replies, one with none, and compare each lesson's line with what Kajabi shows; the totals should match, and `warnings: 0 (none)` is the goal. A lesson id is not personal data; the output holds no name or text. In `pnpm courses:migrate`, the K2 dry run adds a **completeness line** ("N captured comments sit on X of Y lessons"), and says so when a course of five or more lessons has comments on fewer than a fifth of them, or on one lesson only.

See "Verify the comments capture against the live site" in `docs/TODO.md`. A lesson that Bronze already holds a quiz for (`bronze.kajabi_quizzes`, by lesson id) counts as a quiz whatever its page looks like (Kajabi quizzes have no comment thread), so it is not listed under "with none of those (look at these)".
- **Bronze** (raw, replaceable): one row per Kajabi comment (Kajabi id, course, lesson, parent, author email and display name as Kajabi has them, body, posted at, status) with `deleted_at`, re-captured idempotently like lesson progress (changed rows updated, comments Kajabi no longer lists soft-deleted, an unchanged capture writes nothing).
- **Silver** (into `lesson_comments`, never overwriting what members wrote in the Hub): rows with `source = 'kajabi'` and Kajabi's comment id as `source_key` (unique), so a re-run inserts nothing twice. `posted_at` keeps Kajabi's date. Threading follows Kajabi's parent, flattened to the Hub's one level. Author found by exact Kajabi contact name that resolves to exactly one member (see "Authors" below), never guessed; an author who is staff gets the instructor badge. Imported comments are not held for review and do not notify anyone, and write no activity.
- **Authors: matched by name, never guessed.** Everyone who commented in Kajabi is in the Hub, but the capture reads only the name Kajabi shows (no email, contact id or profile link: the member page has none, and the avatar's address is not stored because it can carry a name). So an author is matched through the Kajabi contacts (`bronze.kajabi_contacts`, name and email): the comment's name must equal a contact's name exactly (case, spacing, markup and Unicode composition aside; no punctuation, accent or nickname folding), each such contact's email resolves to a member (their own email or an active alias), and the name is settled only when that gives **exactly one member** (several contacts of one person are fine). A name that matches no member, or more than one, is never guessed: its comments are **left out and listed in the dry run** (name and number of comments, most first, at most 100 names; the owner reads them in the terminal and the response is admin-only, neither is logged), and `member_id` stays required. If the dry run lists many, the next step is a better identifier in the capture (see docs/TODO.md).
- **Lessons.** A comment on a lesson the Hub does not have is counted and skipped, not guessed.
- **Silver import, as built.** `importKajabiLessonComments` (`src/lib/courses/comments-import.ts`) reads `bronze.kajabi_lesson_comments` of a Kajabi course (`KajabiCourseRepository.commentsOfCourse`, past 1000 rows) and writes `lesson_comments` through `CourseCommentRepository.upsertImported`/`importedOf` (migration `20271125000000`: `source` and `source_key`, unique together, a check that both are set or both null, and a trigger that refuses a signed-in member writing them).

It runs as step K2 of `pnpm courses:migrate` (dry run with counts and the unsettled names, then asks, `--yes` skips it, `--skip-comments` leaves it out) or on its own as `POST /api/process/course-comments` with `{course, dryRun}` (admin, same auth as the other `/api/process` routes).
  - **Keyed** `source = 'kajabi'`, `source_key = "<Kajabi lesson id>/<comment key>"`. A re-run changes nothing. A comment whose key was a hash (the capture's fallback) gets a new key when its text is edited in Kajabi: the old row is soft deleted (Bronze stops listing it) and a new one is added, so the thread shows one.
  - **Written as its member, with the badge for the course's instructors.** The row's `member_id` is the matched member, and `is_staff_reply` is true when that member is one of the course's instructors (`course_instructors`; no name list is kept in code), so an instructor's old reply shows as theirs. Staff who are not instructors of the course are ordinary authors. Both are brought up to date on a re-run, and the dry run says plainly when the course has no instructors (no comment shows the badge until one is added; adding one recomputes the course's comments).
  - **What the import owns:** the author, the badge, the text (reduced to plain text if it carries markup, at most 4000 characters, the rest cut and counted), Kajabi's time as `created_at`, the thread position, and whether Kajabi still lists it (a comment Bronze marks removed is soft deleted with no `deleted_by`, and brought back if listed again). **What it never touches:** a staff hide, handled mark, reports, or a delete by a person (`deleted_by` set); comments written in the Hub.
  - **Threads.** A reply goes under its comment; a reply to a reply goes under the comment above it (one level); a reply whose comment is not imported (not in Bronze, or its author unsettled) becomes a top-level comment and is counted (`orphanReplies`). A comment removed in Kajabi that the Hub never had is skipped.
  - **No side effects.** No follow, notification, report or "N new" is created: imported rows are left out of the daily digest's activity, of the thread's new count, of the outline's new and unanswered counts and of the unanswered-questions list (they still count in a lesson's total); they are never held. A member's later reply under one notifies its author like any reply.
  - **Members' rights.** An imported comment is its author's: they can delete it (the 15 minutes to edit it ended long ago), others can't edit or delete it, members can report it, staff hide or delete it from the moderation queue, and the database refuses a member writing the import columns.
  - **Run it** (never against production without the owner reading the dry run): `pnpm courses:migrate --course <kajabi id> --env .env.prod` runs capture, import and the rest; or call the route with `dryRun: true` first and read the counts (comments in Bronze, would add, updated, unchanged, replies, orphan replies, authors matched / staff / unmatched / ambiguous with their names, comments on lessons the Hub does not have, skipped, truncated).
- **Counts.** Until the import has run for a course, an admin view of its comments shows "not imported" and not 0, so an unknown is never shown as none.
- **Consent.** The earlier objection was that there is no consent trail for moving a member's words. These were posted by members on the course they bought, to the course's own audience, and move with the course; say so in the launch announcement, and give a member the same delete as for a Hub comment.
