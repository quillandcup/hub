# Hedgie Care: tracking a community manager's reach-outs

> **Status:** living. **Last reviewed:** 2026-11-21. Review: yearly. Kept up to date with the code; if it is wrong, fix it.

The community manager reaches out to members (an introduction, a check-in on their writing, a welcome back) and some of those turn into long conversations. She started with a spreadsheet, one row per message. **Hedgie Care** tracks it in the Hub instead: one record per **care plan** (one reach-out period with one member; the code, table and events still call it an engagement), a status that follows from the dates and who spoke last, and results measured against the member's own activity before, during and after.

Status: **built** (2026-11-11; its events are in the Activity Log and each plan lists the member's activity during it, 2026-11-14, below): write access, the `care_engagements` table, the repository, the measured results and the page at `/admin/care`, with manual logging. **Slack DMs fill in who spoke last** (step 2, 2026-11-11): her own 1:1 DM with each member she holds a care plan with, below; **no choices to make** (2026-11-14), and **a care plan changes hands only when the receiver accepts** (2026-11-14), see "Owners". Email filling in the messages, the cohort comparison and the import of her sheet are design and are in `docs/TODO.md`, "Hedgie Care: what is left". It replaced a first build called Conversations (one open-ended row per member and channel), which was never announced and had no rows.

**A care plan belongs to the member, not to a channel** (2026-11-12, the owner's decision). The first build gave each plan a channel (Slack DM, prickle, email, other) and allowed one open plan per member *and* channel. The point of Hedgie Care is one place to read a member's care: staff will have all the channels (Slack DMs now, emails and Instagram DMs later) and the member's other activity during a plan, and more channels will come, so nothing bakes in "a plan has a channel". One open plan per member; the channel selector and the "They wrote to me first" tick are gone from the start form (who spoke first comes from a tracked DM or the log buttons); the DM derivation reads the owner's tracked DM with the member whatever the plan is about. The `care_engagements.channel` column is gone (dropped 2026-11-14 in `20270726000100_care_engagements_drop_channel.sql`, once nothing read or wrote it any more).

## Why not the spreadsheet, and why not Outreach

- **One row per message** hides the thing she asked for: how a reach-out is going. "Add a new row to contact them again" gives a history but no way to say "this is still the same engagement".
- **Most columns are derivable.** Date reached out, whether and when they replied are facts the Hub can see. Typing them is the part that gets skipped when she is busy.
- **Outcomes need the member's record.** Whether a hedgie came to a prickle after a check-in is in `member_activities`; a sheet can't see it.
- **Outreach (`outreach_leads`, `outreach_touches`) is the wrong base.** It is about prospects, with a daily queue that only picks members with an Instagram handle, and the community manager can't open `/admin/outreach`. What is worth copying is the pattern: each event is also written to the member's timeline (`member_activities`).

Do not call these "check-ins": that name belongs to the prickle mood check-ins (`prickle_checkins`, `docs/product/CHECKINS.md`). The program is **Hedgie Care**; the unit on the page is a **care plan** ("Start a care plan", "Renew this care plan", "second plan"); one is started for a **reason**. **Why "care plan":** a plan has an explicit timeline, and when it ends there is simply no explicit plan for the member, not "we stop caring". The words went from "engagement period" (confusing next to the **engagement score**, below) to "care period" and then to "care plan" (2026-11-12); the page, its buttons, dialogs, messages and this document say care plan, and a plan in a chain of renewals is called by its ordinal ("first plan", "second plan": `planOrdinal`, `src/lib/care.ts`), never "Period 2". Only the words changed: the table (`care_engagements`), columns, routes, config keys (`careEngagementDays`), events (`care_engagement_*`) and code keep the older names, which `docs/TODO.md` tracks. Where this document still says "period" in prose it means a care plan's stretch of days (`period_ends_on`, `periodEndsOn`). The **engagement score** keeps its name, and so does "engagement" wherever this document talks about the code.

### Next to the Big Sister program, and the work queue

Hedgie Care is a **sibling of the Big Sister program** (`docs/product/BIG_SISTERS.md`: every new member is matched with a big sister for 30 days). The same person is likely responsible for both. "Welcome" (and "introduction") is a reason here **on purpose**, so a welcome she sends is tracked the same way as any other reach-out; it overlaps what a big sister does and the two are not tied together. The admin work queue's hiatus nudges and welcome-back suggestions (`/admin/work-queue`) also overlap, and are left as they are: they say who might need a message, Hedgie Care records that she sent one.

## A care plan

One row is one **care plan**. It has an end date, and when the period is over she **renews** it (a new row that links back to the one before, `renewed_from`) or **closes** it with how it ended. A long relationship is a chain of periods, each with its own start, its own end and its own measured results, so "did the 30 days work" has an answer per period.

- **Length is config, not code:** `careEngagementDays` in `config/app.ts` (read through `src/lib/config.ts`). It starts at **30; the community manager should confirm it.**
- **The period's last day is an organization-timezone (Eastern) calendar date:** the day `careEngagementDays` days after the one it opened on, computed with calendar arithmetic (`periodEndsOn`, `src/lib/care.ts`), so neither a daylight saving change nor an evening opening (already tomorrow in UTC) can move it. The period runs through the whole of that day. Tests cover both sides of an Eastern midnight and both clock-change days.
- **One open care plan per member**, whatever the channel (a partial unique index on `member_id`, `20270707000000_care_plans_per_member.sql`), so a second hello continues the first. Production had at most one open plan per member when the index was built (checked read-only: 8 plans, 7 open).

### Status is derived: no job, no stored lifecycle

`careStatus()` in `src/lib/care.ts`, from the dates, who spoke last and the clock; nothing moves it in the background.

| Status | Rule (first match wins) |
|---|---|
| Closed | she closed it, or renewed it |
| Needs your reply | the member wrote last (their message is later than hers, or hers is missing). **However old the period.** |
| Lapsed | the period ended more than `careLapseGraceDays` (14) Eastern days ago and nobody dealt with it |
| Review due | the period has ended, inside the grace window: renew it or close it |
| Gone quiet | nothing from either side for more than `careQuietDays` (14) days |
| Waiting on them | she wrote last (or opened it) and the quiet window has not passed |

**The lapse rule.** A member who wrote last is never silently dropped: it stays "Needs your reply" until she replies, renews or closes it, even months after the period ended. Everything else with a period that has run out waits a grace window for her, and then **lapses** into the Earlier tab, so the Active list cannot grow forever. A lapsed engagement is still open in the database: she can renew it from Earlier. The grace window and the quiet window are config (`careLapseGraceDays`, `careQuietDays`); all three numbers are starting points to settle from real use.

### How an engagement ends

Four endings, plus `renewed`, which only Renew sets. The close dialog lists them top to bottom with a one-line definition under each and the measured results beside them; **the first that fits is the one to pick**:

| Ending | Means |
|---|---|
| **Handed to someone else** (`handed_off`) | The need was not hers (billing, Big Sister, a host, an admin). The note says to whom. Also used if a member asks her to stop, and she tells an admin in the note. (There is deliberately no "do not contact" ending or warning: the owner doubts it fits a subscription whose point is accountability.) |
| **No reply** (`no_reply`) | She wrote and they never answered, even after a follow-up. Never if they replied even once. |
| **Back in the community** (`back_in_community`) | They replied **and** the measured results show participation resumed after her first message. |
| **Ran its course** (`ran_its_course`) | The exchange is done, or they are content but busy (not in a writing season, busy at home, no sign they might leave) and nothing is pending; consider setting a later check-in date. |

If the conversation is still alive and she has a concrete plan, she **renews** instead of closing. The start form gives each reason a one-line guide the same way (`CARE_REASON_HELP`).

### Renewal is a decision, not a habit

Renewing starts the next care plan from today (a renewal's start is the renewal day) and asks for a **short reason** (what is the plan; at most 1000 characters), which becomes the **new period's note**, and shows the engagement's measured results at that moment. A row reads "first plan", "second plan", counted along the `renewed_from` chain (an ancestor that was removed ends the count there). The typical period is `careEngagementDays`; she can close earlier any time, and renew before or after the period end, including a lapsed one from Earlier. A renewal keeps the owner.

### Starting in the past, and changing a start (built 2026-11-12)

She enters conversations after the fact, so a care plan can be started on (and moved to) the day it really began. The rules, all in `src/lib/care.ts` and the repository, so the form, the action and the tests share them:

- **The day is an Eastern calendar day**, picked with the shared `DatePicker` ("When did this start?"), **today by default and never after today** (a day that has not begun in Eastern time is refused, even when it is already tomorrow in UTC; a day that is not real is refused). The action validates it again (`startDayProblem`), so a call that skips the form gets the same answers.
- **How it is stored (`careStartInstant`).** Today is **now**. An earlier day is **noon Eastern of that day** in `opened_at`: a time that exists on every day (clocks change at 2 a.m.) and sits well inside the day, so the day it lands on is never in doubt. The period's last day (`period_ends_on`) is the chosen day plus `careEngagementDays`.
- **The first message** (hers: who spoke first is not asked, a tracked DM or the log buttons say so later) is recorded for that day too, but for an earlier day it is placed at the **start** of the Eastern day, not at noon (`careFirstMessageInstant`). It is only a placeholder for "somebody wrote that day"; Slack DMs count from the start of that day, and a time only moves forward, so a real DM from that morning replaces the placeholder instead of being hidden behind a made-up noon.
- **Change start date** (`changeEngagementStart`, `CareRepository.changeStart`; a `care.write` staff action, wrapped in `withAction`, event `care_start_date_changed`) works on an open or a closed care plan with the same day rules. **The last day moves with the start and keeps the length the period had** (`shiftedPeriodEnd`: from the Eastern day it opened on to its last day, not today's `careEngagementDays`), so one that ran 45 days still runs 45 and the end can never fall before the start. A first message that is still the placeholder opening wrote moves to the start of the new day; times that came from a log or a DM stay. A start already on the chosen Eastern day changes nothing (the time of day stays). The audit log shows the change as a delta of `opened_at` and `period_ends_on` (the existing trigger; the message times are left out of it as before).
- **The chain stays in order.** A care plan can't start before the one it continues (`renewed_from`), after the one that continued it, or after it was closed; the page says which day stops it. A start on the neighbour's own day is allowed and takes that neighbour's moment when noon would put it out of order (`clampToChain`). A removed neighbour is no limit. A **renewal's start stays the renewal day** unless she moves it, inside that order.
- **One open per member is untouched:** the index is on `member_id` and "not closed, not removed", none of which a start change writes (the update is conditional on the start it read, so a second change in between is refused as "gone").
- **Slack DMs pick up earlier messages:** the derivation counts from the start of the Eastern day it opened, so a start moved earlier (or opened in the past) brings those DMs in, at once for the acting user's own tracked DMs and when the owner next opens the page. Moving a start later does not unwind times a DM already filled in.
- **The member's timeline row moves with it.** The row written when the plan opened (source `care`, type `care_started`, or `care_renewed` for a renewal, `related_id` the plan) takes the new start (`ActivityRepository.moveOccurredAt`, both implementations and the contract). A community manager can insert timeline rows but not change them (RLS), so it runs with the service role after the staff check and `care.write`, best effort like the row's creation (a failure is logged, the plan is the record); only `occurred_at` changes (the description carries no date), and only when the start really changed. It is the plan's start, never the member's membership start; the close row is not touched.

### Owners

Every care plan has an **owner** (`owned_by`, an admin or the community manager; several people can own care plans). **A new care plan is owned by whoever starts it** (2026-11-14, the owner's decision), never silently by the owner of the member's earlier plan: nobody can be given a member's care without agreeing. When the member's earlier plan had a different owner, the start form says so ("Previously cared for by <display name>": information, nothing is handed over). A **renewal keeps its owner** (it is the same person, `renew_care_engagement`).

**Hand-overs need both sides** (the owner's general rule, now in `.claude/rules/hand-overs.md`): a thing that belongs to one person changes hands only when the receiver accepts. For a care plan, open or closed:

- **Propose.** The current owner, or an **admin on their behalf** (when the owner is gone or unreachable), offers the plan to another staff member who may hold plans: an admin or a community manager, the same rule as `isCareOwner` (assistants are not offered; the person must be someone else, and an admin cannot offer a colleague's plan to themselves, since that would be one person on both sides). One offer per plan. The plan **keeps its owner** while the offer waits.
- **Accept** (the receiver; the only way `owned_by` moves) or **decline**. The proposer, the owner or an admin can **cancel**. Accepting makes the receiver the owner **and tracks the receiver's own 1:1 DM with that member** if one exists in Bronze (below); from then on the derivation uses the receiver's DM. Earlier plans' messages stay readable as before.
- **A plan with no owner** (the owner's account was removed: `owned_by` is `ON DELETE SET NULL`) belongs to no one, so no DM is read for it, until a staff member who may own plans **claims** it (`claim_care_plan`: accepting it for themselves). Offering one is for admins only.
- **An admin can take a plan over** (the owner's decision, 2026-11-15: "yes, admins can always take over a care plan, and tell the previous owner on their page"). This is the **one exception** to "hand-overs need both sides", for an owner who is unreachable or when an admin has to step in. An admin takes any **open** plan that is not already theirs in one step (`claim_care_plan`, the same function that claims a plan with no owner), without the owner agreeing. Nobody else gets it: a community manager still needs the receiver's agreement and may only take a plan that **has no owner**; an assistant or a plain member cannot. A **closed (or renewed) plan keeps its owner**: there is nothing being looked after to take over. A plan that is open but past its last day (**lapsed**) can be taken, since that is when it is needed. An admin who already owns the plan is refused (`already_yours`). Any pending offer is cleared. The takeover tracks the **new owner's own 1:1 DM** with that member, exactly like accepting a hand-over, so who spoke last follows the new owner's DM; the earlier plans' messages stay readable per member as designed.
- **The previous owner is told, on the page only.** The plan records its last takeover in four nullable columns (`taken_from` the previous owner, `taken_by` the admin, `taken_at`, and `taken_seen_at` when the previous owner dismissed the notice). They are written **only** by `claim_care_plan` (and `dismiss_care_takeover_notice` for `taken_seen_at`, which only `taken_from` can call), and the guard refuses a forged change like it does the offer columns. The previous owner's Active and Earlier tabs show a quiet **Taken over** section next to "Waiting for you": "<admin display name> took over <member display name>'s care plan on <date>" with **Dismiss**. A later takeover replaces the notice (and shows it again); a hand-over does not touch it, since it stays a true fact about the plan until its reader dismisses it. Nothing is sent: a notification would go through `createNotifier` and start in dry run (`docs/TODO.md`, "Hedgie Care: tell the previous owner of a takeover").
- **A pending offer follows a renewal:** the closed plan no longer shows it, the new one carries it, so the receiver is always offered the plan that is live.

**How the database enforces it** (`20270726000000_care_handover.sql`). The offer is three nullable columns on `care_engagements` (`handover_to`, `handover_by`, `handover_at`, `uuid`s that reference auth with `ON DELETE SET NULL`). The steps are `SECURITY INVOKER` functions, so row-level security applies, and each checks who is calling: `propose_care_handover`, `accept_care_handover`, `decline_care_handover`, `cancel_care_handover` and `claim_care_plan` (a refusal raises `care_handover:<reason>`: `not_found`, `not_allowed`, `receiver_not_allowed`, `same_owner`, `self`, `already_pending`, `no_handover`, `has_owner`). The one `SECURITY DEFINER` piece is `can_own_care_plans(user)`, a boolean for staff only, because a community manager's own session cannot read other users' profiles. A **`BEFORE UPDATE` trigger refuses any change of `owned_by` that does not come through accept or claim, for every role** (an admin, the service role) **and any change of the offer columns that does not come through a step**, so an offer cannot be forged either. `20270801090000_care_admin_takeover.sql` gives `claim_care_plan` a new body (same signature): it sets the setting to `takeover` for an admin taking an owned plan (the trigger then also lets the offer and the `taken_*` notice change), and the trigger refuses a change of `taken_from`, `taken_by`, `taken_at` or `taken_seen_at` from anywhere else (`care_handover:notice_change_refused`). A refusal of the takeover raises `has_owner` (a community manager), `already_yours` or `plan_closed`, and `dismiss_care_takeover_notice` raises `no_notice` for anyone but `taken_from`. The audit log records the takeover as one delta written as the admin (owner, offer cleared, notice) and the dismissal as one delta written as the previous owner (`taken_seen_at`; a second click writes none); the audit trigger is not changed. The functions set a transaction-local setting (`app.care_handover`) that the trigger reads, like `app.audit_skip_course`; PostgREST exposes only functions in the public schema, so nothing a client sends can set it. Inserts are not affected. An account removal still empties the owner and the offer through the foreign keys' own action (the trigger lets a change to NULL through when it runs inside another trigger, and refuses it from a plain UPDATE). The audit log records the proposal and the accept as deltas of the plan, written as the person who did them; the handover columns are not noise, so they are not left out of the audit trigger (`updated_at` and the two last-message times still are).

**In the page.** On both tabs, the receiver sees a **Waiting for you** section at the top with each pending hand-over (the member by display name, from whom, and "offered by" when an admin made the offer on the owner's behalf) and **Accept** / **Decline**. The proposer's plan card says **Waiting for <name> to accept** with **Cancel**; the receiver's copy of the plan card says it is offered to them and leaves the answer to the section. The old **Reassign** control is **Hand over to…** (a staff picker, `EntitySearch`; it offers the owner's colleagues, not the owner or the signed-in user) and only offers; a plan with no owner shows **Take this care plan**, and an admin sees **Take over** on every open plan someone else owns (a one-line confirm: "Take this care plan over from <name>? They will see a notice on their page."; no button for anyone else). The **Mine / Everyone** filter, the avatar chips and the owner search still work, with the owner shown being the owner now. The receiver is told **only by the page for now**; a notification must go through `createNotifier` with a registered kind, starting in dry run (`docs/TODO.md`, "Notify the receiver of a hand-over"). Events: `care_handover_proposed`, `care_handover_accepted`, `care_handover_declined`, `care_handover_cancelled`, `care_plan_claimed`, `care_plan_taken_over` and `care_takeover_notice_dismissed` (no names, no ids), and each action reports its outcome through `withAction`. The refusal sentences are in `src/lib/care-handover.ts`, with the pure `handoverControls` that decides what the page shows (the database is the check).

### Lapse is an accountability signal

The page opens with the number of **lapsed** and **review due** engagements, and, when any are overdue, a small table of **whose** they are (including "Unassigned"), so a capacity problem is visible to the community manager and to admins alike. The Earlier tab shows the lapsed count too. Lapse stays derived: no cron moves it. **Not built:** a reminder to the owner when a period reaches "review due" and an alert to admins when one lapses. Both must go through `createNotifier` with registered kinds and start in dry run behind a feature flag (`.claude/rules/member-notifications.md`, "Member Notifications" and "A new automated sender starts in dry run"); see `docs/TODO.md`.

**Active** = open and not lapsed. The page has two tabs, which are paths (`.claude/rules/tabbed-pages.md`, "Tabbed Pages"): `/admin/care` (Active: needs reply, review due, gone quiet, waiting), `/admin/care/earlier` (closed and lapsed, most recently ended first, paged). There is no DM tab any more (2026-11-14): tracking her DMs is automatic, see "Slack DMs fill it in". `?member=<member id>` on either tab shows one member's care plans (a plan's own id is never in an address). Actions: **Renew** (closes this period as renewed and opens the next in one step: `renew_care_engagement`, a database function, safe against a double click because the close is conditional on the engagement still being open), **Close** with how it ended (every ending except "renewed", which only Renew sets), log a message, set a follow-up day, edit, remove (soft delete), and **hand over** (it takes two: see "Owners").

## Data model

Local layer (operational data owned by this app, not reprocessed). Ids are text and server-made (`care_` prefix); the member id is `text REFERENCES members(id)`. This is Tier 2: **no id appears in a URL, link or export** (the page never links by engagement id).

- **`care_engagements`** (`20270626000000_hedgie_care.sql`): `id`, `member_id`, `opened_by` (staff, uuid: it references auth), `reason`, `opened_at`, `period_ends_on` (date), `renewed_from` (the engagement this one continues), `closed_at`, `ended_how` (null while open, set with `closed_at`: a CHECK keeps the two together), `last_staff_message_at`, `last_member_message_at` (both null when nothing is derived or logged), `follow_up_on`, `next_step` (at most 280 characters), `notes` (at most 1000, short and light: no sensitive detail), `deleted_at` (soft delete: a member's reach-out is a record of someone), `created_at`, `updated_at`. There is no channel column: a plan belongs to the member (the first build's `channel` was dropped in `20270726000100_care_engagements_drop_channel.sql`). Reasons and endings are checked sets; the sets live in `src/lib/care-values.ts` and the migration only. `owned_by` is the owning staff user (uuid, `ON DELETE SET NULL`); it changes only through a hand-over the receiver accepts (see "Owners"), and `handover_to`, `handover_by` and `handover_at` (`20270726000000_care_handover.sql`) hold the one pending offer, if any.
- **`care_tracked_dms`** (`20270630000000_care_tracked_dms.sql`, id prefix `caredm_`): the 1:1 Slack DMs read for care plans. A row says "this staff user's DM with this member is read for the plans they hold": it exists **because a care plan exists** (it is made when a plan is started or accepted, and by the projection that runs when Hedgie Care opens and after the nightly DM pull), not because anyone chose it. `id`, `tracked_by` (the staff user, uuid, references auth, cascades), `slack_channel_id` (the Slack DM conversation id: an external id Slack made, not one of ours, and not a foreign key because Bronze is rebuilt), `member_id` (the other person in the DM, `text REFERENCES members(id)`), `created_at`, `deleted_at` (the earlier opt-in design soft deleted a row when she stopped tracking a DM; nothing sets it now, every read leaves such a row out, and tracking again adds a new live row: the old ones are history, not a choice to respect). A partial unique index keeps one live row per user and DM. **No message content, ever.** `CareTrackedDmRepository` (`src/lib/data/care-dms.ts`). Metadata of the messages themselves is not copied anywhere either: the derived times are the two `last_*_message_at` columns above, which Bronze can rebuild at any time (the `conversation_messages` table this section once sketched is not needed and is not planned).

**Access.** Reads and writes are for admins and the community manager: `requireActionPermission("care.write")` in the actions and `requirePermission("care.read")` on the pages (`src/lib/admin-auth.ts`; the community manager's bundle is in `src/lib/permissions.ts`), and RLS with `is_community_manager()`, never a widened `is_admin()`. RLS lets staff read, insert (with `opened_by` forced to the caller) and update; there is **no DELETE grant** and no `anon` grant. Her write controls are the opt-in permission **`care.write`** (`STAFF_FEATURES`, `src/lib/permissions.ts`), granted to her (every admin has every permission); a future staff role gets neither the page nor the feature until added. Every action is wrapped in `withAction`. **Tracked DMs are narrower than the engagements:** `care_tracked_dms` is readable and writable only by the staff user whose row it is (RLS: `tracked_by = auth.uid()` and admin or community manager; an admin does **not** read a community manager's list, and nothing widens `is_admin()`); a user can add a row and set `deleted_at` (a column grant left from the opt-in design that the app no longer uses), nothing else; there is no `DELETE` grant and no `anon` grant; pgTAP covers each.

**Audit.** The `care_engagement` entity is audited by three triggers; the update one leaves out `updated_at`, `last_staff_message_at` and `last_member_message_at`, so logging a reply writes no row. **The note and the next step are redacted** (`audit_row_change('care_engagement', 'id', 'notes,next_step')`, `20270730000000_care_activity_log.sql`, which also scrubbed the rows written before): the audit row says they changed, never what they said, because the Activity Log shows its rows to every admin and nothing a staff member typed belongs in a feed sentence or its data. A hand-over proposal and its accept are deltas (`handover_*`, `owned_by`), as the person who did them. `audit_row_change()` counts a community manager as staff (changed in the first build). A renewal is two audit rows by nature (the old period closed as "renewed", the new one inserted with `renewed_from`); the central feed lists only the second, so a renewal reads as one entry. **`care_tracked_dms` is deliberately not audited:** it is a staff member's private working list (which of her DMs feed her engagements), and the audit log is readable by admins, who must not see another staff member's list (`docs/architecture/ACTIVITY_AND_AUDIT_LOG.md`, "Deliberately not audited"). The engagement's `last_*_message_at` stay out of the audit trigger, so a derivation writes no audit row either.

**Timeline.** Every event of a care plan that has a name writes a `member_activities` row (source `care`, actor staff, engagement 0, `related_id` the plan) from the server action, best effort: `care_started`, `care_renewed` (with `data.plan_number`), `care_closed` (with `data.ended_how`), and, for a hand-over, `care_handover_offered` (`data.to_user_id`), `care_handover_accepted` (`data.from_user_id`, the owner it came from), `care_handover_declined` (`data.from_user_id`, who offered it), `care_handover_cancelled` (`data.to_user_id`) and `care_plan_claimed`. The data holds fixed values and the ids of staff accounts, never a name and never anything she typed (the reason of a renewal, a note, a next step). RLS gives a community manager an INSERT on `member_activities` for that source, as herself, and nothing else. **A member does not read these rows about themselves** (the member policy leaves the source out, `20270730000000`); admins and the community manager do. They exist for the plan card's "Activity during this plan" (below), because a community manager can read `member_activities` but not `audit_log`. **The central Activity Log lists the audit rows and not these rows**, so each event is one entry there (`get_activity_feed` and `count_activity_feed` leave out source `care`); see "Care plans in the Activity Log".

**Removal request.** `member_data_handling` says `care_engagements.member_id` is deleted with the member. A member merge drops the duplicate's engagements for now (`NOT_MOVED`, `src/lib/member-merge-coverage.ts`; `docs/TODO.md`).

The repository is `CareRepository` (`src/lib/data/care.ts`) with a Supabase class, an in-memory twin and one contract suite, and the table is registered in the guard test.

## Care plans in the Activity Log (built 2026-11-14)

Owner's decision: a feature launches with its events in the activity log (`.claude/rules/audit-and-activity-log.md`, "A Feature Launches With Its Events in the Activity Log"). The unified log is the point of Hedgie Care: staff read, during a plan, what the member did and what staff did. Each event is one plain sentence, in the admin Activity Log (`/admin/activity`) and on a member's Activity tab (`/admin/members/<id>/activity`; the community manager's tab is limited to the member's own activity and reads these events from their timeline rows instead of the audit rows she cannot read, `docs/architecture/ACTIVITY_AND_AUDIT_LOG.md`, "The community manager's Activity tab"), read from the audit row of the plan (`care_engagement`) by `describeRow`, with the sentences in one pure module (`src/lib/care-events.ts`) that the plan card shares:

| What happened | How it reads | From |
|---|---|---|
| A plan starts | "started a care plan" | the plan's insert |
| It is renewed | "renewed the care plan (second plan)" (the number counted along `renewed_from`: `withCarePlanNumbers`; no number when the chain ends at a removed plan) | the new plan's insert, with `renewed_from` |
| It is closed | "closed the care plan: ran its course" (or no reply, back in the community, handed to someone else) | `closed_at` and `ended_how` |
| It is offered | "offered the care plan to <name>" | `handover_to` set |
| The receiver accepts | "accepted the care plan from <name>" (the old owner) | `owned_by` changes from an owner |
| The receiver declines | "declined the care plan <name> offered" | `handover_to` cleared by the receiver |
| It is taken back | "took back the offer of the care plan to <name>" | `handover_to` cleared by anyone else |
| A plan with no owner is taken | "took a care plan that had no owner" | `owned_by` set from none |
| An admin takes the plan over | "took over the care plan from <name>" (the previous owner), written as the admin, with the member's timeline row `care_plan_taken_over` (data: the previous owner's account id) | `owned_by` changes from an owner with `taken_at` set in the same row, whether or not an offer was waiting and cleared with it (read before the accept) |
| The previous owner dismisses the notice | "dismissed the notice that a care plan of theirs was taken over", written as them; audit row only, no timeline row (it is about the staff member, not the member) | `taken_seen_at` set, nothing else changed |
| An owner changes with no offer accepted and no takeover | "took over the care plan from <name>" (kept for a row with neither, which nothing writes today) | `owned_by` changes from an owner, the offer not cleared in the same row |
| The start moves | "moved the care plan's start to <date>" (the Eastern day) | `opened_at` |
| The plan is edited | "changed the care plan (note, next step, reason, follow-up day)": the names of the fields, never their values | those columns |
| It is removed or brought back | "removed the care plan" / "brought the care plan back" | `deleted_at` |

Names are display names (`getMemberDisplayName`: the audit row holds account ids, which `withReferenceNames` resolves through the member behind each account; one that cannot be resolved reads "a colleague"). **No typed text anywhere**: the sentences are built from the kind of event, a fixed ending, a day, a name and a number; the audit trigger redacts the note and the next step; the `member_activities` rows carry only fixed values and account ids. A renewal's reason is the new plan's note, so it is redacted too. Tests: `tests/lib/care-events.test.ts` (every sentence, a name with `&` and `<`, no typed text, both sides of Eastern midnight and both clock-change days for a moved start), `tests/lib/activity-feed.test.ts` (the rows through the fetchers), `supabase/tests/database/care_activity_log.test.sql` (redaction, the feed's one entry per event, who reads the care rows).

**What does not write a row, and why.** Logging "They replied" / "I replied" writes nothing: it moves a time on the plan (no audit row either, by design) and is not an event worth a line for each click. A tracked DM (the derivation of who spoke last) is derived state, rebuildable from Bronze at any time, and is not an action by anyone. A hand-over's three database steps are one audit row each by nature. **Still not in the log:** emails she sends or receives outside the Hub, Instagram DMs, and a manual touch with a channel (`docs/TODO.md`, "Hedgie Care: what is left in the unified timeline").

### Activity during this plan (built 2026-11-14)

On every plan card (open or earlier), admins and the community manager (the `care.write` feature) have **Activity during this plan**: a read-only list, newest first, 25 rows a page ("Show older"), of the member's activity inside that plan's own window. The window runs from the plan's start (`opened_at`, included) to when it closed, or to now while it is open (not included): an earlier plan lists its own stretch, so a renewal's list starts where the one before ended (`planActivityWindow`, `src/lib/care-activity.ts`).

**It nests with the Activity tab** (2026-11-17): this list is a subset of the community manager's Activity tab on the member page (`/admin/members/<id>/activity`, feature `member.activity_tab_limited`), which holds every `member_activities` row about the member and is itself a subset of an admin's. Both read the member's log, so nothing here is missing from her tab; the exception is that an admin's feed leaves the care rows out (the audit row of the plan says each event). Proven by `supabase/tests/database/activity_nesting.test.sql`; `docs/architecture/ACTIVITY_AND_AUDIT_LOG.md`, "The community manager's Activity tab". Moments that are not `member_activities` rows (a moved start, an edit, a removal) are in neither.

- **What is listed** (and nothing else, so a new kind of activity does not appear until someone chooses it): the member's own activity of the types "last in the community" counts (prickles, Slack and Hub chat messages and reactions, check-ins and check-outs, commitments, lessons, writing logs: `PARTICIPATION`, `src/lib/care-participation.ts`), never a login; the emails the Hub sent them and what they did with them (sent, opened, clicked, bounced: `email_*` rows of source `resend`); and the plan's own care rows (started or renewed, closed, handed over) whenever they happened, so a close at the exact end of the window is still shown. Each row is labelled with the Activity Log's own wording (the stored title: "Posted in #writing", "Attended Morning Writing"), a care row as "<who> <sentence>" ("Cam started a care plan").
- **No text, ever.** A message is a time and a place. The repository method returns a narrow row (`PlanActivityRow`: no description, which for a Slack or chat row can be the message text, and no `data` except what a care row may carry); a writing log is labelled in fixed words ("Logged writing progress") because its title can name a project; Slack message text stays service role only.
- **Where it comes from.** `ActivityRepository.planActivity` (`src/lib/data/activities.ts`; the in-memory twin and one contract suite), through the signed-in user's own client. A community manager reads `member_activities` through RLS (`20270430000500_community_manager_role.sql`); the admin-only `get_activity_feed` is not used and `is_admin()` is not widened. A page is a database range over a stable order (time, then id), so a member with more than 1000 rows in a window is read in full, one page at a time. The action is `loadCarePlanActivity` (staff with `care.write`; an assistant, a plain member or a signed-out caller is refused). A plan's own window, so it also works for earlier plans.
- **Analytics.** `care_activity_opened` (whether the plan is open, how many rows; no names, no ids) when the panel first loads; "Show older" is autocaptured as `care-activity-more`.
- **Tests.** The contract (`tests/helpers/activity-contract.ts`: start included, end not, the vocabulary, care rows at any time, no text, paging over 1000 rows), `tests/lib/care-activity.test.ts` (the window and both sides of Eastern midnight and the two clock-change days), the action in `tests/lib/care-actions.test.ts` (who may read it, each plan's window, the open plan's end at now, names, paging) and the panel in `tests/components/pages/care-page.test.tsx`.

## What success is

The owner's definition of success for the program. **Primary: churn reduction through engagement and belonging.** **Secondary: upsell** into courses, programs and opportunities that fit where a writer is in their journey. The graded retention view below is how the first is read (active, on hiatus or cancelled at each checkpoint, and the first time they lapsed); the programs started and the commitments held are the start of the second (a course the member started is not derivable yet, `docs/TODO.md`).

## Measuring her influence

Each engagement is compared with the member's own activity. Nothing is stored: the windows are counted when she opens "Results" on an engagement (`getEngagementResults`, `src/lib/care-results.ts`), so any window can be recomputed for any date. The windows (`careWindows`, `src/lib/care.ts`; the numbers are config: `careBeforeDays` 28, `careAfterFromDays` 30, `careAfterToDays` 60):

| Window | From | To |
|---|---|---|
| **Before** | 28 days before it opened | when it opened |
| **During** | when it opened | the end of the period's last day, or when she closed it if that was sooner (a renewal closes the old period when the new one starts). Still running: counted up to now ("so far") |
| **After** | 30 days after the period's last day | 60 days after it. Until it has fully passed it reads "Not yet": a half-finished window would look like a result it is not |

Windows are half open (the start counts, the end does not). Before is exact 24-hour days; after is by Eastern calendar day. For each window the panel shows the parts, not a score (`engagement_score` is capped at 100 over 30 days and hides what changed):

- **Prickles attended**: distinct prickles, never attendance records (a member who leaves and rejoins one counts it once, `.claude/rules/attendance-table.md`, "Attendance Table Design"). From the `prickle_attended` rows of `member_activities`, which are one per member and prickle.
- **Slack messages**: messages and thread replies, not reactions.
- **Firsts**: whether their first prickle ever and their first Slack post ever fall in the window. Derived from the activity log (first row of each kind), not assumed.
- **Staying (graded retention)**, the way the primary goal is read. Not one number: **active, on hiatus or cancelled at each checkpoint** after the period's last day (`careRetentionCheckpointDays` in `config/app.ts`, starting at 60, 90 and 180 days, **for the owner to confirm**), each "Not yet" until it has passed, derived from membership history with the same engine as the Membership Timeline (`memberStatusTimeline`, `src/lib/member-status.ts`, the logic `recomputeMemberStatus` uses, now answerable for any instant). The three states are shown apart because they mean different things: **hiatus is roughly half a churn** (a member on hiatus has not left, and has not stayed either), and churning at day 31 would still mean Hedgie Care was fairly ineffective. Any weighting of the three for a later cohort view must be config, never code. Staff read as active. Nothing assumes what counts as churn.
- **First went on hiatus or cancelled**: the first moment after the engagement opened that they did either, as "N days after it started" (or none so far), so a day-31 lapse stays visible even if they came back later (`MemberStatusTimeline.firstLapse`: the start of the first hiatus or gap, or the end of an active stretch with nothing after it, since the timeline drops a trailing open-ended gap).
- **Commitment held** (the longer the commitment, the stronger the anti-churn signal): the programs they were enrolled in at each checkpoint, each with the length of its own cohort ("Example Program, 6 months", from the cohort's start and end dates). **There is no annual plan yet** (the owner, 2026-11-11); the longer commitments today are the courses The Ready Writer and Self-Editing Academy (6 to 12 months, membership included), so **program length is the commitment signal for now**. Annual plans are planned and need a dated look at Stripe's price interval (structured: `data.items.data[].price.recurring`; all 393 subscriptions in production on 2026-11-11 are monthly) and at how Kajabi offers are sold (they carry only booleans and a free-text price description) when they launch; see `docs/TODO.md`. Nothing is flagged as "long": the length is shown and she reads it.
- **Programs started**: the programs whose cohort starts inside the window (`ProgramRepository.enrollmentsOf`), by name and length. It includes programs that come with membership, so it is a list to read, not a count of upsells. This is the secondary goal's row (below).
- **People connected**: the distinct members they interacted with (`member_interactions`: DMs, thread replies, mentions, reactions, in either direction), **not counting staff**: every member who is in the `staff` table, and the person who opened the engagement (she is paid in comps and may not be in `staff`). If it rises after, the outreach opened a door; if only their contact with her rises, it did not outlast her.

The membership and program rows read tables she cannot all read with her own client. Her client can read the membership timeline's billing Bronze (Kajabi and Stripe customers, purchases, offers, subscriptions), the hiatus history and `members` (`20270430000500_community_manager_role.sql`), but no policy found gives her `member_status_overrides` or `member_program_enrollments`, which the membership at each checkpoint and the programs started need. That is why `getEngagementResults` hands the service role to `loadCareResults` after `requireActionPermission(...)` and `care.write`, and only the derived answer (member, on hiatus, not a member, program names) leaves it. Courses are **not** included: `course_access.starts_at` says access began (a membership grants many courses), not that the member started one (`docs/TODO.md`).

The reads are repository methods with both a start and an end date, paginated past 1000 rows with a stable order: `ActivityRepository.countsBetween` and `firsts` (`src/lib/data/activities.ts`) and the new `MemberInteractionRepository.counterpartsBetween` (`src/lib/data/member-interactions.ts`; nothing owned `member_interactions` before: the member network page reads it through an RPC). `member_interactions` was admin-only, so the migration gives the community manager a **read-only** `is_community_manager()` SELECT policy on it. It holds who interacted with whom, when and whether in the open or in private (DMs and private channels included), never what was said; she sees only counts on this page, but the policy lets her read the rows.

### On every care plan: the engagement score and last in the community (built 2026-11-12)

She used to type these into the note, so each row (and the Results panel) shows them, worked out when the page opens, never stored:

- **Engagement score, then and now.** The member's score and tier (at risk, active, highly engaged) when the care plan started and today, by the same rules as the member's own page: points of the 30 days up to the instant, capped at 100 (`computeMemberEngagementMetrics`, with `engagementScoreAt` for "as of an instant", `src/lib/member-engagement.ts`; `src/lib/care-scores.ts` loads them for the page). A prickle is worth 10, other activity a few points, and a login one; under 10 is at risk, 10 to 49 active, 50 and over highly engaged. Reads are batched: one `ActivityRepository.engagementRowsForWindows` for every row on the page (windows in chunks, only rows that carry points, paginated past 1000 with a stable order). The SQL function `admin_member_engagement` repeats the rule for the Members list and is left as it is (`docs/TODO.md`).
- **Last in the community** (`src/lib/care-participation.ts`): the latest thing the member did themselves anywhere in the Hedgie House, as a date and what it was ("3/19/2026, a Slack reaction (yesterday)"). It reads `member_activities` and counts only rows with **`actor_kind = 'member'`** and a type from a list: a prickle, a Slack message or reply, a Slack reaction, a prickle check-in or check-out, a prickle commitment, a lesson watched or completed, a writing log. It leaves out logins, feedback to staff, taking a check-in back, and everything staff or the system wrote (care, outreach, sudo, email events, a commitment an admin made while viewing as the member). A new kind of activity does not count until it is listed. `ActivityRepository.lastParticipation` finds it for all the members on the page at once: it looks back a week, a month, three months, a year and then all time, each step only for the members not found yet.
- **It is not the DM line.** "Last message: they wrote 3 days ago" (who spoke last in the care plan's messages, from the DM she tracks or what she logged) is its own line; the two new lines are about the member's own life in the community.
- **Access.** The community manager's own client reads `member_activities` (the policy added with the role), so the page uses it; nothing needs the service role. If either read fails, the page logs it and shows the rows without ("not available").
- Each label has a tooltip, and the Results panel prints the help in full, saying exactly what is counted (the sentences are the code's, `src/app/(admin)/admin/care/CareFacts.tsx`).

**Honest limits** (also on the panel)
- **These describe; they do not prove.** Nothing here shows the outreach caused a change. The business outcomes behind the page are churn reduction through engagement and belonging (primary) and upsell into other courses and programs (secondary); membership at the end and programs started are the two rows that look at them, and the cohort comparison below is what could say more.
- Some quiet members come back unprompted. A fair comparison is a group of similar at-risk members she has not contacted; that cohort view is not built (`docs/TODO.md`).
- At about 10 a week the totals stay small for months. Read individual before-and-after stories now and cohort numbers each quarter.
- Slack messages and people connected exist only as far back as the Hub's own records (the Slack activity mirror, the chat mirror behind `member_interactions`), so a "before" window early in that history can read low and a "first" can be the first the Hub knows of.
- Don't derive "who counts as at-risk" or "returned" from a guess: take it from membership history and the data (`.claude/rules/business-facts.md`, "Business Facts: Derive, Don't Assume").

## Slack DMs fill it in (built)

The Slack DM bridge already pulls 1:1 and group DMs each night using a member's own Slack token (`src/modules/chat/slack/dms.ts`, `/api/reconcile/slack-dms`; `docs/product/SLACK_BRIDGED_CHAT.md`, "Slack 1:1 DMs through members' own tokens"). The bot cannot be in a 1:1 DM, which is why it is the member's token. When she has connected her Slack with the DM scopes and `slack_dm_bridge` is on for her, every DM between her and a member reaches Bronze. Hedgie Care's status derivation reads **who wrote and when** in her DM with each member she holds a care plan with, and nothing else. A separate, narrower reader shows the messages themselves inside the member's care plans ("Plan messages", below).

### The decisions (the owner's)

- **Tracking is automatic, with no per-DM choices** (2026-11-14; before, 2026-11-12, it was an opt-in switch per DM and then "tracked unless she stopped it"). A DM is tracked **because a care plan exists** with that member and the plan's owner has a 1:1 DM with them: staff do not pick DMs. There is no "Your DMs" tab, no "Stop tracking this DM", no "Read this DM again", no `trackCareDm` / `untrackCareDm` and no `care_dm_tracked` events, and no rule that remembers a stop. Tracking is made **when a plan is started** (`startEngagement`), **when a hand-over is accepted or a plan with no owner is claimed** (the receiver's own DM with that member), **when Hedgie Care opens** (a plan she owns whose DM is not tracked yet) and **after the nightly DM pull** (everyone's plans: `/api/reconcile/slack-dms`). A row soft deleted by the earlier design is history that is never read; tracking again adds a new row. The DM stays tracked after a plan closes (continuity of care reads earlier plans through it).
- **Staff are never offered.** A DM whose other person is staff is never tracked: a member in the `staff` table (`staff.member_id`) or the member behind an admin, assistant or community manager account (`StaffRepository.memberIds()`, the one definition of a staff member; an assistant was added 2026-11-12).
- **Group DMs are out of the first version.**
- **Metadata only, for the derivation.** The derivation of who spoke last never reads message text, files or raw payloads, in the UI, logs, errors, analytics or the table (the plan messages below are a different path). The Bronze read is `SlackMessageRepository.lastWritesIn`: the database function `care_dm_last_messages` returns, for each Slack conversation and person, the time of their latest live message, reading the content-free `bronze.slack_messages_meta` view, with the service role only (the function refuses a signed-in user). Every caller checks who is asking first (`requireActionPermission(...)`/`requirePermission(...)`, then `staffCan(role, "care.write")`).
- **Only her own DMs.** The DM is found from the DMs her own Slack user is in now (`SlackWorkspaceRepository.directMessagesOf(her Slack user id)`, the id coming from her own connection), never from a channel id someone supplies; another person's DM can't appear, not even for an admin. The projection re-checks that she and the member are the two people in the conversation before it reads a word of metadata, so a row filed straight into the table (RLS only decides whose row it is) for a DM that is not hers reads nothing.

### What the page says when her DM is not there

On a plan she owns that is open and whose DM is not tracked, the card says why, quietly (`dmNote`, from `ownDmAccess`, `src/lib/care-dm.ts`):

- **Slack is not connected for her** (no connection, a dropped one, one without the DM permissions, or `slack_dm_bridge` not on for her): "Connect Slack so your DM with them fills in", with the existing Slack connect flow (`/api/oauth/slack/start?next=/admin/care`, back to Hedgie Care; not a gate of its own, since nothing here is mandatory: `.claude/rules/gates-and-return-to.md` is not involved). On the Active tab one calm banner says the same, **only when she holds at least one open plan** and it applies; it can be dismissed on that device ("Not now"). Where she connects Slack used to be the DMs tab.
- **Connected, but no DM with them yet:** "No DM with them yet.", nothing more.
- An account with no member profile has no Slack account to connect, so nothing is offered. It is never shown on someone else's plan or a closed one, and nothing nags.

### What the page says about privacy

The Hub's Slack connection already brings her direct messages in for Hub Chat (`src/modules/chat/slack/dms.ts`), and Hedgie Care adds nothing to that: to fill in who spoke last it reads who wrote and when in her DM with a member she holds a care plan with, and who spoke last shows on the care plan, which everyone who opens Hedgie Care can see. Other staff, admins included, don't see which DMs she has tracked (RLS). The messages themselves are shown only in a plan's **Messages** panel ("Plan messages", below), and `/privacy` ("Messages in a Hedgie Care plan") tells members. **Open fact, still:** the pull reads **all** of a connected member's 1:1 and group DMs, so what Hedgie Care uses is narrower than what is pulled.

### Open the DM

A care plan with a tracked Slack DM (hers, open or closed) has an **Open the DM** link (`src/lib/care-dm-link.ts`: `dmLinkTarget`, one small function so the choice can change as DMs move into Hub Chat). It leads to **Hub Chat** (`/chat/<conversation id>`, a Tier 1 id) when the Hub has that conversation and she is in it now (a Slack 1:1 DM reaches a member's chat once they have pulled their own DMs; `ChatRepository.idsBySlackChannels` and `joinedChannelIds`, so a conversation she is not in is never offered), else to **Slack** with Slack's documented `https://slack.com/app_redirect?channel=<DM id>`, adding `&team=<workspace id>` when `SLACK_TEAM_ID` is set (an existing, optional variable; without it Slack opens the DM in the person's default workspace, which is what Slack's deep-linking docs say). No workspace id is written in the code, and a link carries only ids, never text. Only the person who tracks the DM sees it: the link needs her tracked list, which is hers alone (after a hand-over the new owner sees her own link, not the previous owner's).

### Plan messages: the text of tracked DMs, inside the plan (decided 2026-11-12)

The owner's decision: the team does **not** get a general reader for DMs with staff (a DM with a staff member outside a care plan is exactly as before: break-glass only; no chat policy changed). Instead the **Messages** panel on a care plan (`CarePlanMessages`, the `getCareMessages` action, `src/lib/care-messages.ts`) shows the **full messages** of the member's tracked DM(s) sent **inside the windows of the member's care plans**, to whoever can open Hedgie Care with the `care.write` feature (admins and community managers; `requireActionPermission(...)` refuses an assistant, a member and a signed-out caller).

- **The window of a plan** runs from its start (`opened_at`) to its end (`closed_at`, or now while it is open); the end is not included, so plans that follow each other never claim the same message. A message in no window is never read. Deleted messages and the notices Slack posts itself are left out (`SlackMessageRepository.dmMessagesBetween`, service role only; `src/lib/care-messages.ts` is its only caller).
- **Per member, not per owner.** The panel shows all of that member's plans, newest first, each with its messages (who and when, oldest first inside a plan), a page of 50 at a time ("Older messages"). A new caregiver (once they accept a hand-over), or the owner of a later plan, sees the earlier plans' messages.
- **Which DMs.** Every live tracked DM with that member (`CareTrackedDmRepository.forMember`), read from the database, never from the request (the action takes a member id and a cursor). A DM is tracked because a care plan exists (made when a plan is started, a hand-over is accepted or an ownerless plan is claimed, and by the projection when Hedgie Care opens and after the nightly pull), and nothing untracks it, so the earlier owner's DM stays after a hand-over: the messages are in Bronze already and stay readable while a plan has no owner or an offer is pending, and the receiver's own DM with the member is tracked when they accept, so their messages appear too (a different Slack conversation). Rows left from the opt-in design with no plan of that staff member behind them would also show (checked in `docs/TODO.md`).
- **Read with the service role after the checks**, never by widening Bronze or chat policies: `getCareMessages` runs `requireActionPermission("care.member_activity.read")` (held by admins and community managers, deliberately not by the assistant), `staffCan(role, "care.write")`, then `CareRepository.logMessagesRead(memberId)` with the reader's own client (an admin previewing as a community manager is that role), and only then `loadCareMessages(getServiceRepos(), ...)`. If the log entry cannot be written, nothing is shown. `tests/lib/staff-definition.test.ts` fails if `dmMessagesBetween` gets a second reader or the order of these checks changes.
- **A trail.** Opening the panel writes one audit row per reader, member and hour (`care_log_messages_read`, entity `care_dm_read`, the member as entity and member, **no text**), shown in the Activity Log's Privacy view, and, because the entry names the member, on that member's Activity Log tab (admins only).
- **Telling members.** A one-on-one DM in Hub Chat that is tracked in a plan of its member (open or past; `chat_dm_in_care_plan`) shows `CarePlanNotice` at the top, for both people: "Our community manager and admins can read what is sent in this conversation during a Hedgie Care plan, our way of staying in touch with members." No note on any other DM. Slack's own app cannot show one; `/privacy` ("Messages in a Hedgie Care plan") says so.
- **Read-only.** No quoting, no copy into notes. Summaries of the conversation are a future idea that needs an AI SDK (none is installed, and member writing must never go to an AI service without the owner's decision); recorded in `docs/TODO.md`, "Hedgie Care: what is left".

### The rule for "who spoke last" (derived, `src/lib/care-dm.ts`, `src/lib/care-dm-rules.ts`)

For an **open** care plan, whatever it is about, owned by the staff user (`owned_by`; a plan with no owner reads no one's DM) who tracks a DM with that member:

- the **staff side** is the latest message her Slack account wrote in that DM, the **member side** the latest one the member's wrote (the pair is matched by Slack user id through the Hub's Slack-to-member links, and re-checked in Bronze);
- a **thread reply** or **file share** counts; a **deleted** message, a Slack **join or leave notice** (`SLACK_SYSTEM_SUBTYPES`) and anything written by someone else in the conversation (a bot) does not;
- only a message **on or after the start of the Eastern calendar day the engagement opened** counts (`dmCountsFrom`, `ORG_TIMEZONE`): a message she sent earlier that day, just before logging the start, still counts; yesterday's does not. A calendar day, not 24 hours, so the 23-hour and 25-hour clock-change days and an evening opening (already tomorrow in UTC) land on the right midnight (tested on both sides of each);
- a time only ever moves **forward** (`CareRepository.moveMessageTimesForward`, one conditional update per side): a manual "they replied" that is later stays, and an older message never undoes anything;
- **idempotent and rebuildable**: running it again changes nothing, and it can be re-run from Bronze at any time. It writes no audit rows (the two columns are left out of the audit trigger).

It is **pull-based** (nothing relies on a webhook alone): **(a)** when she opens Hedgie Care (her own open plans and tracked DMs; it first tracks her DM with the member of a plan she holds when it was not tracked: the page reads what Bronze has and does not trigger a DM pull; Bronze is kept current by the live `message.im` events for covered members and the pulls when she opens chat), **(b)** after the nightly DM pull for every staff member's plans and tracked DMs (`/api/reconcile/slack-dms`, inside the route's time budget; a failure or an out-of-time pass is reported with the run's other problems), and **(c)** when she starts a care plan, accepts one handed to her or claims one. The webhook itself does not project: reading Bronze when the page opens is as fresh as a webhook would make it, since nothing sends her anything when a member replies (`docs/TODO.md` records it).

The status on the rows and the "Needs your reply" count follow from those times, and a small **from Slack** hint on a row says its last-spoke is kept up to date from her tracked DM, so a manual log isn't needed. (The hint shows on her own engagements only: nobody can read another staff member's tracked list.)

**What is hidden when** (the page's own condition is `followsTrackedDm`, `src/lib/care-dm-rules.ts`, the pairing the derivation reads: an **open** care plan **owned** by the signed-in user, who **tracks a DM with that member**; the page already loads her tracked list, so there is no extra read):

- **"They replied" / "I replied"** are hidden on such a period and the row says "Filled in from your Slack DM" where they were (the **from Slack** hint stays, and the "Last message" line). The DM sets both times and a manual log can only move them forward, so the buttons would do nothing useful. They stay for a contact the Hub can't see (a phone call, a prickle, an email), where she has no DM with them, and on a plan owned by someone else (nobody can read another staff member's tracked list, so the page can't tell). The `logCareMessage` action is unchanged and refuses nothing new.
- **The start form asks who, why, the day it started, a note and a follow-up.** There is no "Where" and no "They wrote to me first" tick: a care plan belongs to the member, and who spoke first comes from her tracked DM or the "They replied" / "I replied" buttons. (A plan starts with her message at the start day, as before; a tracked DM moves times forward from there.)
- **The start form is in a dialog.** The top of the Active tab has one button, "Start a care plan" (`data-track="care-start-open"`; not on Earlier), so the form does not push the care plans down; The start form's member is searched, and when someone else cared for the member before it says "Previously cared for by <display name>" (information only). It opens the shared `Modal` (`src/components/Modal.tsx`, titled "Start a care plan"; focus moves in and comes back to the button, Escape, the close button and a click outside close it and drop what was typed, and it can't be closed while a start is on its way). A start the server refuses or a missing field keeps it open with the message; a start that works closes it and the list refreshes, so the next opening is empty. The member search and the date fields sit inside the dialog's own scroll area, so nothing is clipped. Layout inside it follows the container-query rules.
- Under the buttons of an open period, a line says what **Renew** (starts a new care plan for them) and **Close** (ends this one and asks how it ended) do; the start form's submit button is "Start plan".
