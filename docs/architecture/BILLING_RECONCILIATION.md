# Billing reconciliation

> **Status:** living. **Last reviewed:** 2026-11-26. Review: yearly. Kept up to date with the code; if it is wrong, fix it.

Staff need to see where what Stripe billed disagrees with what the Hub expects, so they can fix Stripe or the Hub. The unit is **one member's purchase**: a schedule of the days a payment is expected, lined up against the Stripe transactions, with a status per day. It never changes Stripe; what staff know that the Hub does not is recorded as an acknowledgement (see "Acknowledging a row").

Payment data is service role only (Bronze), so both entry points check the permission first and read through `getServiceRepos()`.

## How a membership schedule is built

- **Expected days.** For each Stripe subscription of the member (not `incomplete_expired`): the creation day, then the billing day of each following month (the day of the subscription's `billing_cycle_anchor`, clamped to short months) until it is cancelled. Today and later days are not expected yet. A paused subscription (`pause_collection`, read from the raw record: `behavior` and `resumes_at`) expects nothing before its resume day, and billing again from that day; a pause with no resume date is indefinite and expects nothing more.
- **Stripe is the truth about now.** A paused subscription means the member is on hiatus now (its `resumes_at` is the hiatus end), a cancelled one means cancelled. Past hiatuses can only come from the Hub (`member_hiatus_history`): Stripe keeps no history of pauses. When the two disagree today the schedule gets a `hub_stripe_disagree` row: the Hub says hiatus but the subscription is not paused; Stripe is paused but the Hub has no hiatus (and the member is not in a program); the Hub end date differs from `resumes_at` (days, in Eastern days), or one of them has no date; the Hub says cancelled but a subscription is live; the Hub says active but every subscription is cancelled (and there is no program).
- **A short pause is not a hiatus (heuristic).** A pause is not always a hiatus: a short one can be a gifted month. A pause of `SHORT_PAUSE_MAX_BILLING_PERIODS` (1) billing period or less is the neutral status `paused_short` ("Paused, short"), which does not count as a problem and does not raise "Hub and Stripe disagree" when the Hub has no hiatus row; two or more periods, or no resume date, is read as a hiatus. Stripe stores no pause start, so the length is counted from the first billing day after the member's last payment up to the resume day. No payment is expected during any pause; a payment taken during one (found when draft or void invoices, which a pause leaves, sit within a billing period on both sides of it) is `charged_when_not_expected` with cause `pause`. All of this is a guess from the data, and the UI says so.
- **The invoices check the Hub's hiatus records (heuristic).** The Hub's `member_hiatus_history` is the claim, the payments the evidence. `inferBillingGaps` finds two consecutive paid invoices more than one billing period apart, leaving out a gap with a failed, open or uncollectible invoice in it, a gap that spans a cancellation (not inside one subscription's life), and a gap mostly covered by a program that includes membership. `auditHiatuses` compares each gap with the hiatus rows over it: `hiatus_confirmed` (the hiatus ends within 5 days of the day billing resumed), `hiatus_mismatch` (it ends earlier or later, with the day difference; `hiatus_billing_day` is the case of a hiatus that ended on the member's billing day and still lost a month), `unrecorded_gap` (no hiatus and no program: the Hub may be missing a hiatus, or billing was missed) and `gap_short` (one period: a possible gifted month, neutral). A hiatus with a payment inside it is `charged_when_not_expected`, as before. The overview groups them under "Hiatus and program billing" (and the old `?filter=` ids still narrow to each).
- **Not expected.** A billing day strictly inside a hiatus (`member_hiatus_history`) or a cohort of a program that includes membership (`programs.includes_membership`, read by `ProgramRepository.membershipEnrollmentWindows`). The first and last day of such a window are neither expected nor flagged: the first is the cycle that started it, the last the one that resumes billing.
- **Matching.** Each membership invoice (product named in `MEMBERSHIP_PRODUCT_NAMES`) matches the nearest expected day within 5 days, each side at most once, using Eastern days (`ORG_TIMEZONE`). A refund is read from the charges joined to the invoice (from either side).
- **Statuses.** `paid` (a failed charge that was retried and paid stays paid, with the tries in the reason), `refunded` (in full), `missed` (expected, no invoice, or only a draft or void one), `unpaid_behind` and `unpaid_earlier` (an invoice Stripe has not collected, open or uncollectible; see "Unpaid invoices"), `charged_when_not_expected` (a payment inside a hiatus or a program), `unexpected` (a payment matching no day), `not_expected`, `hub_stripe_disagree`. A row also has a `cause` (`hiatus`, `program`, or `after_hiatus` for a missed billing day within 45 days after a hiatus ended; its reason says when the hiatus ended on the billing day), which the overview filters use.
- **Unpaid invoices are rolled up, in two kinds.** Consecutive unpaid invoices (open or uncollectible) of one Stripe subscription, with no payment on it in between, are one row ("9 invoices unpaid since 2026-03-02, $423.00 total"), listing every invoice in `transactions`, keyed like the first one (member, purchase, month of the first unpaid invoice). `rollUpUnpaid` then reads the member's payments: `unpaid_behind` when nothing was paid after the run's latest unpaid invoice (the member is behind; listed first), `unpaid_earlier` when a later invoice was paid (the member pays again and earlier months were never collected; still real missed revenue, so still needs a look, less urgently). Each is its own status (not a `cause`), so the member's status chips show the split; both are under "Collect money" in the overview, and `?filter=behind` / `?filter=paying_again` still narrow to each. The wording follows the subscription's `collection_method` (read from the raw record by `toReconSubscription`): `send_invoice` only emails an invoice and never charges a card, so the reason says "emailed" and "unpaid for N days" (Eastern days, from `today`) and never counts tries; `charge_automatically` (or unknown) keeps "tries" and the decline codes. Voiding an invoice in Stripe drops it from the report (draft and void invoices are skipped).
- **Needs attention.** `missed`, `unpaid_behind` and `unpaid_earlier` only for a member whose status is `active`; `charged_when_not_expected`, `unexpected` and `hub_stripe_disagree` always. A program that ended more than 7 days ago with no membership billing since (no live subscription: none, cancelled or paused) is a `missed` row on the program's end day, for an active member.
- **How far back.** One member's grouped view covers their whole billing history (`lookbackMonths: null`: the loader is given every invoice and charge the Transactions tab read, and the header range runs from the first subscription or invoice to the end), so it has as many transactions as the flat list. The overview keeps a 12-month lookback (`DEFAULT_LOOKBACK_MONTHS`, reading 24 months of invoices for the hiatus and program checks), so it stays cheap and about what needs a look. Whatever a schedule leaves out is named in `PurchaseSchedule.notes`, shown under the heading: invoices before the range, draft or void invoices (they billed nothing and only mark a pause), invoices for other products, and one-off payments with no invoice.
- **Row keys** are what an acknowledgement is bound to, so they never change scheme. A membership row is member + `membership` + month (a counter when a month has several rows, in the order of their expected days); a purchase row is member + type + event or offer + the Kajabi purchase id + the row's place in that purchase (a counter when one purchase or payment-only block repeats). A key does not contain the status or the amount, so it stays the same when a payment arrives; `tests/lib/billing-purchases.test.ts` pins that two purchases of one offer never share a key and that a status change keeps it.

## Events, programs and courses: one block per Kajabi purchase

`src/lib/billing-purchases.ts` (`buildPurchaseSchedules`, pure; `loadPurchaseSchedules` in the loader reads its inputs) builds one `PurchaseSchedule` per Kajabi purchase of an offer that sells an event (a `community_event_offers` link whose purchase window holds the purchase's Eastern day), or maps to a program or a course (`loadOfferCoverage`). The block shows what Kajabi's Purchases tab shows (`PurchaseInfo`: offer, Kajabi's price description, the day, quantity, status) and the Stripe payments under it, one row each. The membership block carries the same header (offer name, last price, subscription status).

**How a purchase finds its payment.** Kajabi collects through our Stripe account and writes its offer id into the metadata of the charge and of the subscription of a payment plan, in one of two shapes: `kjb_offer_id` (2022 to August 2026) or, since July 2026 and with no `kjb_offer_id`, `platform_product_ID` (`platform_order_ID` beside it is not a Kajabi purchase id and is never used). One helper, `src/lib/kajabi-charge-metadata.ts`, reads both for the repository query (an `or` over the two metadata paths), its in-memory twin and the loader; a charge with Kajabi keys (`kjb_` or `platform_` prefix) but neither offer id is counted, and the page says so, because its purchases would silently show as missing a payment. The Hub keeps the raw record, so no import change is needed. A single purchase takes an unclaimed one-off charge (no invoice) of the same offer within 2 days; a `multipay` purchase takes the subscription of that offer created within 2 days, and its invoices are the payments. The buyer's Kajabi email is not required to be the Stripe customer's (that differs about one time in eight), so candidates are ranked by: succeeded first, the member's own Stripe customer, the equal amount, the nearest day. Each charge and subscription is used once. A purchase left over matches a charge of the member's customers whose description is the offer's name; a charge of these offers that no purchase claims is a payment with no purchase on record. Production (2026-11-25): 352 single purchases of linked event offers, 306 matched by the buyer's email, all 352 by offer, amount and day; 32 of 32 payment plans matched a subscription by offer and day; 356 charges with a linked offer id, 534 charges with no invoice in all.

**What is expected of an event** (only an event with a linked offer; a free session expects nothing):
- An attendee with no ticket source bought: `paid` payments are fine; no payment is `missing_payment` (no Kajabi purchase, a purchase with no Stripe payment, or everything refunded while still on the roster).
- Comped, gifted and transferred attendees expect nothing here (neutral `comped`, `gifted`, `transferred`; a transferred ticket's payment belongs to the origin event). A payment from one of them is `unexplained_payment`.
- A payer who is not attending needs an explanation, in this order: every ticket went to a gifted attendee (`gifted`), the ticket was moved to another event (`transferred`), a mark in `event_buyers_not_attending` (`forfeited`, or `marked_not_attending` for other with its note); otherwise `unexplained_payment`. A payer who was refunded in full needs none. These are the rules of `src/lib/kajabi-event-matching.ts`, read from the same tables (`ticketRecords`, `notAttendingMarks`).
- Payments are listed, never totalled against a price: a deposit or an instalment is a `paid` row, and there is no "partly paid" until a total is stored (`docs/TODO.md`).

**Granted purchases** (a purchase with no price) are usually our workaround for a gift, a comp or a payment collected directly in Stripe, so they do not mean "no payment". The member's status override in force on the purchase day (`member_status_overrides`, the same table `subscription-reconciliation` reads) says what to expect: gift and special expect none (`granted_no_payment`; a payment is `unexpected`); `direct_stripe` expects Stripe payments, which are the schedule (`granted_paid_stripe`; none found is `missing_payment`); with no override the purchase is `granted_no_override`, and a payment with no `direct_stripe` override is `paid_override_missing`, so staff can record it. Today Kajabi's purchases carry no grant marker, so no row has these statuses yet (`docs/TODO.md`).

The overview lists them under "Event, program and course purchases" (and `?filter=missing_payment`, `unexplained_payment` and `granted` narrow to each), with the event linked to the admin event page and the member to their Transactions tab.

## The overview page

`src/lib/billing-issues.ts` turns the loader's schedules into `Issue`s (pure, serializable): one per `ScheduleRow` with `needsAttention`, keeping the row's `key` (a later acknowledgement attaches to it). The page (`BillingOverview.tsx`, a server component) builds them, filters them from the URL and hands them to `BillingIssuesTable.tsx` (client), the standard admin table: `useDataTable` (search, sort on every column, 50 per page), `SortableTh`, `DataTablePager`, `TableKeyboardNav` (the member link is the row's main link; the disclosure button keeps Enter and Space, and the open detail row is `data-kbd-skip`). The table scrolls sideways on a narrow window instead of reflowing.

- **Columns.** Member (display name, legal name beneath where it differs; links to their Transactions tab), Purchase (the event links to its admin page), Issue (the status as a badge), Amount, Age, Hub status.
- **Amount** is the money at stake, by kind: for unpaid rows, what was billed and not collected (the whole run); for a payment that should not have happened (`charged_when_not_expected`, `unexpected`, `unexplained_payment`, `paid_override_missing`), what was kept; for anything else the amount expected (the last price, or the purchase's price), else what its transactions billed. Blank when none is known (gaps, Hub and Stripe disagreements). The header sums it per currency.
- **Age** is the days from the expected billing day (else the earliest transaction) to the page's `today`, counted as Eastern calendar days, so a daylight saving change or the hour of day never shifts it. Blank without a day.
- **Default order** (`compareUrgency`): members who are behind (`unpaid_behind`) first, then the largest amount, then the oldest, then member name and key; rows with no amount or age after rows that have one. Clicking a header sorts by value (amount and age numerically); a third click returns to this order.
- **Filters.** Five groups, each row in exactly one (`issueGroupOf`), so the counts add up to All: Collect money (`unpaid_behind`, `unpaid_earlier`, and `missed` apart from after a hiatus or a program), Hiatus and program billing (charged during a hiatus, pause or program, missed after a hiatus or program, hiatus and billing gaps that differ, gaps with no hiatus), Hub and Stripe disagree, Event, program and course purchases (missing, unexplained and granted), Unexpected payments. Plus a Hub status select (any, active, on hiatus, cancelled). `?filter=<group>&hub=<status>` is the URL. The ids of the earlier, narrower filters (`behind`, `paying_again`, `payment`, `missed`, `charged_hiatus`, `missed_after_hiatus`, `unrecorded_gap`, `hiatus_mismatch`, `billing_day`, `program`, `missing_payment`, `unexplained_payment`, `granted`) still work: they narrow inside the groups and the page says so. Counts are rows, not members; the header says issues, members and dollars for the current filter.
- **Digging in.** A row opens (a disclosure button) to the full sentence, then the Stripe invoices or payments behind it (date, what, amount, status, tries, a link to Stripe from `stripeInvoiceUrl` / `stripeChargeUrl`, as on the Transactions tab) and "Open member's transactions". An admin also finds "Mark as…" here (see "Acknowledging a row"). Writing off and changing Stripe are in `docs/TODO.md`.

## Acknowledging a row

Many rows are not problems but decisions the Hub does not know about: a purchase that was comped, a month gifted, a non-refundable payment kept after a cancellation, a payment made outside Stripe. Staff record **why a row is as it is**; the row leaves the to-do count, and the decision stays visible and reversible. It never touches Stripe, and it is not a write-off (no amount is written off; that and writing back to Stripe are in `docs/TODO.md`).

- **Reasons** (a fixed set, `src/lib/billing-ack-reasons.ts`): `gift` (a month or a purchase given), `comp` (complimentary: staff, a volunteer, or promised), `forfeited` (payment kept and not refundable, such as a retreat deposit after a cancellation), `paid_elsewhere` (cash, PayPal or another processor), `other` (a note is then required). A note is optional for the rest, at most 500 characters.
- **What is stored** (`billing_acknowledgements`, id prefix `brak_`, Tier 2: never in a URL; repository `BillingAcknowledgementRepository`): the row's `key`, the member, the purchase type, the reason and note, **the row's status and the amount at stake when staff marked it** (the server's own figures: the action recomputes the overview and takes the row from it, so a forged status or amount from the browser is ignored, and a key the overview does not list as needing a look is refused), who and when. Only one active acknowledgement per key (a partial unique index); marking the same thing again changes nothing, and marking with another reason (or a row that changed) revokes the old one and writes a new one.
- **Reversible, never deleted.** Undo sets `revoked_at` and `revoked_by`; a trigger refuses any other edit and there is no delete grant, so the history stays (a removed member takes theirs with them, `member_data_handling`: delete). One click, no confirmation: it can be undone, and so can marking.
- **Bound to what staff saw: "changed since".** An acknowledgement applies while the row's status and amount at stake equal what was recorded (`ackStillApplies`, `src/lib/billing-acknowledgements.ts`). When either differs (a payment arrived, the run of unpaid invoices grew, a gap became another kind of problem) it stops applying: the row is back in the list and the count, with "Was marked complimentary on 2026-11-27, but it has changed since." Marking it again records the new status and amount.
- **In the overview**: `buildIssues(overview, acks, staff)` puts each row's `ack` on its issue. Rows that an acknowledgement still settles are out of the group chips' counts, the "N issues for M members, $X at stake" line and the default list. A quiet line says "N acknowledged, $X, left out of the count above" with a "Show acknowledged" link (`?ack=1`, kept by the filters); they then show last and muted, with the reason beside the status, and are found by the search (reason and note). The dollars at stake stay honest: the acknowledged amount is reported apart, never inside.
- **On a member's Transactions tab** the same decision shows under the row (`acknowledgementsForSchedules`), the "Only rows that need a look" count leaves out settled rows, and the same buttons work there. The tab shows acknowledgements only to who may read the page (`billing.reconciliation.read`). A row older than the overview's 12 months, or one only the member's own view finds (a second Stripe customer), is not on the page, so the server refuses to mark it ("not on the billing reconciliation page").
- **Permission**: `billing.reconciliation.acknowledge`, held by the admin only (and the assistant, whose list is the admin's); every other role holds none, as for `billing.reconciliation.read`, since the page names members and their payments. Anyone who can read the page sees the decisions read-only. The controls are shown by `can(viewer, "billing.reconciliation.acknowledge")`, never a role name; the server actions (`src/app/(admin)/admin/hygiene/billing-reconciliation/actions.ts`) check it again. Row-level security allows only `is_admin()` to read and write the table; the page reads it through the service role after the read permission check, and the writes go through the signed-in person's own client so the audit log names them. The assistant therefore cannot save until the database backs the role (`docs/TODO.md`, "Back the assistant role in the database").
- **Activity Log**: the table has the `audit_row_change` trigger (`billing_acknowledgement`, the note redacted): "acknowledged Ada's billing row as complimentary" and "undid the acknowledgement of Ada's billing row" (`describeRow`, `src/lib/activity-feed.ts`), listed under the member. No typed text is in the sentence or the log. Each action also records its outcome (`billing_row_acknowledged`, `billing_acknowledgement_revoked`).

## Adding a purchase type

`PURCHASE_TYPES` names them. Membership is built in `buildSchedules`; Kajabi purchases in `buildPurchaseSchedules`, which skips an offer whose kind is not event, program or course (`ReconOffer.kind`). Return `PurchaseSchedule`s with a unique `key`; the component and the overview need no change.

## What the data says (production, 2026-11-25)

Read once with the checks above over the last 12 months: 128 schedules; 85 rows needed attention across 45 members (before unpaid invoices were rolled up into one row per run; counted per invoice then; rerun with the pause and gap checks: failed 34, Hub and Stripe disagree 13, unexpected 12, open 13, missed 5, charged when not expected 7 (4 inside a program, 3 inside a pause), hiatus and billing gap differ 6, unrecorded gap 5; plus 10 hiatuses confirmed, 3 short pauses and 1 short gap, which are not problems). None was a missed day after a hiatus ended. These counts move every day; the page is the source.

The billing-day question (does a hiatus that ends on the billing day skip an extra month?) is answered by the `hiatus_billing_day` rows: in the same run, 5 hiatus rows ended on the billing day and differ from the billing gap, 4 of them by about a month (billing resumed a month after the Hub's end date). Earlier, by a direct query: of 49 hiatuses ending on the member's billing day in the last two years, 37 were charged on the end day itself; 4 had their next membership payment 30 or 31 days later, 1 three days later, and 7 had none after it.
