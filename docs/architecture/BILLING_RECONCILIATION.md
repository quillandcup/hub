# Billing reconciliation

> **Status:** living. **Last reviewed:** 2026-11-25. Review: yearly. Kept up to date with the code; if it is wrong, fix it.

Staff need to see where what Stripe billed disagrees with what the Hub expects, so they can fix Stripe or the Hub. The unit is **one member's purchase**: a schedule of the days a payment is expected, lined up against the Stripe transactions, with a status per day. It is read-only.

## The pieces

| Piece | Where |
|---|---|
| The pure model: `buildSchedules(input)` returns `PurchaseSchedule[]` (purchase type, member, rows). No database. | `src/lib/billing-reconciliation.ts` |
| The loader: reads inputs through the repositories (`loadMemberSchedules` for one member, `loadBillingOverview` for everyone) | `src/lib/billing-reconciliation-loader.ts` |
| One member's view: a "Flat list" / "Group by product" switch on the Transactions tab (permission `member.transactions_tab`; the choice is remembered in the browser); grouped is the Billing schedule | `src/components/BillingSchedule.tsx`, `src/app/(admin)/admin/members/[id]/` |
| The overview, "Hiatus & Billing Reconciliation": members with a row that needs a look, with filters (all, Hub and Stripe disagree, charged during a hiatus, not billed after a hiatus ended, program and membership, failed or open, missed, unexpected); permission `billing.reconciliation.read`, admin only | `src/app/(admin)/admin/hygiene/billing-reconciliation/` |

Payment data is service role only (Bronze), so both entry points check the permission first and read through `getServiceRepos()`.

## How a membership schedule is built

- **Expected days.** For each Stripe subscription of the member (not `incomplete_expired`): the creation day, then the billing day of each following month (the day of the subscription's `billing_cycle_anchor`, clamped to short months) until it is cancelled. Today and later days are not expected yet. A paused subscription (`pause_collection`, read from the raw record: `behavior` and `resumes_at`) expects nothing before its resume day, and billing again from that day; a pause with no resume date is indefinite and expects nothing more.
- **Stripe is the truth about now.** A paused subscription means the member is on hiatus now (its `resumes_at` is the hiatus end), a cancelled one means cancelled. Past hiatuses can only come from the Hub (`member_hiatus_history`): Stripe keeps no history of pauses. When the two disagree today the schedule gets a `hub_stripe_disagree` row: the Hub says hiatus but the subscription is not paused; Stripe is paused but the Hub has no hiatus (and the member is not in a program); the Hub end date differs from `resumes_at` (days, in Eastern days), or one of them has no date; the Hub says cancelled but a subscription is live; the Hub says active but every subscription is cancelled (and there is no program).
- **A short pause is not a hiatus (heuristic).** A pause is not always a hiatus: a short one can be a gifted month. A pause of `SHORT_PAUSE_MAX_BILLING_PERIODS` (1) billing period or less is the neutral status `paused_short` ("Paused, short"), which does not count as a problem and does not raise "Hub and Stripe disagree" when the Hub has no hiatus row; two or more periods, or no resume date, is read as a hiatus. Stripe stores no pause start, so the length is counted from the first billing day after the member's last payment up to the resume day. No payment is expected during any pause; a payment taken during one (found when draft or void invoices, which a pause leaves, sit within a billing period on both sides of it) is `charged_when_not_expected` with cause `pause`. All of this is a guess from the data, and the UI says so.
- **The invoices check the Hub's hiatus records (heuristic).** The Hub's `member_hiatus_history` is the claim, the payments the evidence. `inferBillingGaps` finds two consecutive paid invoices more than one billing period apart, leaving out a gap with a failed, open or uncollectible invoice in it, a gap that spans a cancellation (not inside one subscription's life), and a gap mostly covered by a program that includes membership. `auditHiatuses` compares each gap with the hiatus rows over it: `hiatus_confirmed` (the hiatus ends within 5 days of the day billing resumed), `hiatus_mismatch` (it ends earlier or later, with the day difference; `hiatus_billing_day` is the case of a hiatus that ended on the member's billing day and still lost a month), `unrecorded_gap` (no hiatus and no program: the Hub may be missing a hiatus, or billing was missed) and `gap_short` (one period: a possible gifted month, neutral). A hiatus with a payment inside it is `charged_when_not_expected`, as before. The overview has a filter for each.
- **Not expected.** A billing day strictly inside a hiatus (`member_hiatus_history`) or a cohort of a program that includes membership (`programs.includes_membership`, read by `ProgramRepository.membershipEnrollmentWindows`). The first and last day of such a window are neither expected nor flagged: the first is the cycle that started it, the last the one that resumes billing.
- **Matching.** Each membership invoice (product named in `MEMBERSHIP_PRODUCT_NAMES`) matches the nearest expected day within 5 days, each side at most once, using Eastern days (`ORG_TIMEZONE`). A refund is read from the charges joined to the invoice (from either side).
- **Statuses.** `paid` (a failed charge that was retried and paid stays paid, with the tries in the reason), `refunded` (in full), `missed` (expected, no invoice, or only a draft or void one), `failed` (uncollectible, or open with a declined charge), `open`, `charged_when_not_expected` (a payment inside a hiatus or a program), `unexpected` (a payment matching no day), `not_expected`, `hub_stripe_disagree`. A row also has a `cause` (`hiatus`, `program`, or `after_hiatus` for a missed billing day within 45 days after a hiatus ended; its reason says when the hiatus ended on the billing day), which the overview filters use.
- **Needs attention.** `missed`, `failed` and `open` only for a member whose status is `active`; `charged_when_not_expected`, `unexpected` and `hub_stripe_disagree` always. A program that ended more than 7 days ago with no membership billing since (no live subscription: none, cancelled or paused) is a `missed` row on the program's end day, for an active member.
- **How far back.** One member's grouped view covers their whole billing history (`lookbackMonths: null`: the loader is given every invoice and charge the Transactions tab read, and the header range runs from the first subscription or invoice to the end), so it has as many transactions as the flat list. The overview keeps a 12-month lookback (`DEFAULT_LOOKBACK_MONTHS`, reading 24 months of invoices for the hiatus and program checks), so it stays cheap and about what needs a look. Whatever a schedule leaves out is named in `PurchaseSchedule.notes`, shown under the heading: invoices before the range, draft or void invoices (they billed nothing and only mark a pause), invoices for other products, and one-off payments with no invoice.
- **Row keys** are member + purchase type + month (a counter when a month has several rows), stable so a row can be acknowledged later.

## Adding a purchase type (retreats, events, programs, courses)

`PURCHASE_TYPES` already names them. Add a builder next to `buildMembershipSchedule` that returns `PurchaseSchedule`s of its type with the same `ScheduleRow` statuses, and call it in `buildSchedules`. The component and the overview group by purchase and need no change.

## What the data says (production, 2026-11-25)

Read once with the checks above over the last 12 months: 128 schedules; 85 rows needed attention across 45 members (rerun with the pause and gap checks: failed 34, Hub and Stripe disagree 13, unexpected 12, open 13, missed 5, charged when not expected 7 (4 inside a program, 3 inside a pause), hiatus and billing gap differ 6, unrecorded gap 5; plus 10 hiatuses confirmed, 3 short pauses and 1 short gap, which are not problems). None was a missed day after a hiatus ended. These counts move every day; the page is the source.

The billing-day question (does a hiatus that ends on the billing day skip an extra month?) is answered by the `hiatus_billing_day` rows: in the same run, 5 hiatus rows ended on the billing day and differ from the billing gap, 4 of them by about a month (billing resumed a month after the Hub's end date). Earlier, by a direct query: of 49 hiatuses ending on the member's billing day in the last two years, 37 were charged on the end day itself; 4 had their next membership payment 30 or 31 days later, 1 three days later, and 7 had none after it.
